As you sit in the airport at Buenos Aires after such a hefty few days of flying, it's starting to really dawn on me that I'm about to flying to Ushuaia - the gateway to Antarctica. And in just a few days, I'll be getting onboard an expedition ship Going to the Antarctic Peninsula! 

There's a big part of me that is simply not processing that fact - that in a few days I'll be in Antarctica. I'm not sure if it's the numbness you've experienced a lot of these last few years, but it's not clear if my lack of excitement is because I haven't come to terms with the reality of the trip yet, or if I'm downplaying it all in an attempt to protect my psyche from potential disappointment. 

What is clear is that I'm not overly excited, and I wonder if that's just always been my way? Thinking back I've often done the same thing before big trips - busy getting things ready, other bullshit things popping up that demand attention, and just general tiredness from juggling too many priorities at once. Likely a combination of all these things which are slowing you down from unbridled enthusiasm. But I think this trip will cut through much of that - you're off on a lifelong dream to see the white continent, and you're doing all you can to see leopard seals while kayaking. That is going to be fucking WILD. 

Even as you type that though, you're trying to downplay it and be fine with it not happening. It was genuinely tough to write :That is going to be fucking WILD" because there's part of you that is adding doubt to protect yourself from it not happening. But you don't need protecting - you need to throw yourself out there and be wrong instead! Say "I'm going to Antarctica to kayak with leopard seals because it's something I've always dreamed of." and don't try to protect your ego by adding caveats. Do you have control over what the leopard seals do? No! Will you be disappointed if you don't see any? Probably! But will it ruin the entire trip? FUCK NO! Because this is all about putting yourself out there, making bold claims, and then owning it when things don't work out as hoped or planned. 

You had someone at your last job who could never commit to any thing or any measurable statement - it was always qualified with a whole lot of bullshit and hand-waving so he couldn't be pinned to any specific statement later. I fear that my lack of enthusiasm for life might be stemming from a cynicism and insecurity about the things I do - that I've spent too long taking people's comments personally that I've been Self-censoring and adding caveats to everything. YOU DO NOT NEED TO BE RIGHT ABOUT ANYTHING! Be wrong! Be wrong on a regular basis, and be okay with it, because being wrong makes life more interesting - it makes for a better story, and it makes you stronger too! 

So why is Antarctica so important to you? Because this trip is an opportunity to be raw and open and honest about how much this shit excites you! Fuck, you're about to fly to the southern-most tip of South America (a continent you've never been to before!) and then get on a fucking boat to the bottom of the world! You're going to rock and roll through the Drake Passage, you're going to see an absolute shitton of penguins and seals, and you're going to see leopard seals fucking them all up! 

This is an important, expensive, and exciting trip - stop trying to protect yourself from disappointment and start living life as fully as you can as you experience things you love!
