**“Feeding Mars to Feed Earth”**

[![Mars atmosphere 2014 National Science Week   Feeding Mars to Feed Earth workshops](https://web.archive.org/web/20140920063350im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/Mars_atmosphere.jpg "2014 National Science Week   Feeding Mars to Feed Earth workshops")](https://web.archive.org/web/20140920063350/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/Mars_atmosphere.jpg) Keeping a vegetable patch can be pretty hard. You have to pick the perfect spot, make sure your plants have the right soil, plant them in the right season, give them enough water and fertiliser, remove weeds and pick your vegetables at just the right time. Even if you’ve got a green-thumb, it sounds like a lot of work. But now imagine designing and running your own vegetable patch… on Mars!

Mars One astronaut candidate Josh Richards is presenting in-school workshops for Year 7 to 12 during National Science Week (August 16-24), exploring how the sustainable food technology being developed to feed the future colonists of other planets is being used to tackle the agricultural challenges we face right here on Earth.

Some of the topics covered in this half-day workshops include:

- Sustainability in Martian Greenhouses
- Growing specialist crops for astronauts
- Calculating food requirements for Mars colonists
- Water recycling on the International Space Station
- Using “humanure” as a fertiliser
- Why India started a space agency to help their farmers

Through a mixture of class discussions and hands-on challenges, Josh explains the vital role space technology plays in sustainable food production. Following the 2014 National Science Week theme of “Food for our future – Science feeding the world”, this workshop is an innovative way to inspire Year 7 to 12 students to embrace environmental responsibility and agricultural sustainability through the concept of “Spaceship Earth”.
