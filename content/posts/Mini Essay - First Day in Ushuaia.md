It's your first day in Ushuaia and you're naturally shattered from not sleeping in the last 2.5 days, but you're managing well considering that. You definitely got a good feel for this place though, enough that you certainly reached a point earlier where you were tired, hungry and sore, and the magic of being so close to Antarctica (with the matching weather) had worn off and you just wanted to stop. 

Based on how you've done today, you should be able to check in to the Antarctica Hostel, have a shower, then nap for a couple of hours to recover, then figure out your next steps. You'll likely meet folks once you check in, and I'm sure there will be something social happening tonight (it's Friday afterall). Worse case though you can simply squirrel away and write or read tonight until you know you're ready for sleep. Tomorrow is an open-ended questions, and one that you don't need to answer just yet (even though you' like to). You also don't NEED to do anything tomorrow - it could easily be a day of wandering around Ushuaia and then going through your writing and tasks. You could even tackle the PRC paperwork tomorrow if you felt inclined. You have no obligations though, and it's important to remember that - you don't *have* to do anything at all. 

Getting food here is straightforward too, so you could simply sit, write, read, and drink coffee if you wanted. It feels weird not to have any obligations, but knowing there's plenty you *could* be doing, which is always dangerous. Ultimately the only thing you need to do today is to check into the Antarctica Hostel, which you'll do within the hour. I'd love to go through all the amazing things that I have coming up over the next few months, and how that is all setting up even more incredible things further down the line, but right now I just want to do the one thing I need to do today, then shower and sleep.

When I do have the bandwidth to start sorting through things, don't forget that there's an A5 notepad slipped into the laptop sleeve of my bag. Sketching things on that may help spread the ideas out enough that I can acknowledge, process, and prioritise them. Go through your bag, reorganise things now that you're no longer flying, and then start working through your own brain too. Lay out what's happening over the next few months, figure out what's important to you and what you need to do for it, and layout what your priorities are and how you'll put them first.
