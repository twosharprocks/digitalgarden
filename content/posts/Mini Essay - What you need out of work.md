The last few years have all been about work and study, and now that you've left work and have several months to yourself, you're not sure what to do with yourself. Not because there's nothing to do, but because there's dozen of different potential projects that you could spend your time and energy on, and you're not sure which will help you achieve your long term goals. Primarily because you're not sure what you want to want! 

The first thing that comes to mind is the development of your websites, both personal and the Soggy Wombats. But you also know that they primarily showcase what you've created and done, and you don't really know what you want to be showcasing or working towards right now. There's no doubt that you want to be writing books though, and you know that writing (like you are now) is important for you to establish where you're going, what you're doing, and why you're doing it. 

You have things like [2025]({{< relref "posts/2025.md" >}}), [2025]({{< relref "posts/2025.md" >}}) Goals, Tasks, and Stray - Ideas all crowding your mind, and that's without looking further afield to your [Bucket List]({{< relref "posts/Bucket List.md" >}}). The real question becomes "What do you want to do in 2026, and what can you do to support that over the next 3 months?". 

So figure out 2026. 

You're going to visit Thailand in early January, which you've already started the conversation on. Why are you going to Thailand? Because you want to explore caves, there are plenty to discover there, and you want to take people on overseas expeditions. That's going to mean getting a ChOptima (which you're already organising) and doing a crossover (which you have multiple options for, but need to figure out your preference). 

You're going to need to find some sort of cyber work in order to earn money for Bikini Atoll. Having [CISSP]({{< relref "posts/CISSP.md" >}}) helps a lot, but the bigger decision you need to ask is what kind of cyber work can you do that will use your skills in a way that is not draining. Because ultimately cyber work is not what you want to be doing with your life - it should be an easy and well-paying job that leaves plenty of bandwidth so you can write books and go on cave diving expeditions. That's what you really want to be doing, and in some ways you're doing what Harry did with medicine - studying and working in something well paying and respected even though your real interest is exploration.

So ask yourself what kind of cyber work will allow you to disconnect emotionally while still paying really well and being flexible? There are dozens of different roles out there, and one factor may be how much money you want/need to earn to pay for Trip - Bikini Atoll - 2028 June. You have plenty of money right now, but you'll need to pay for the CCR, crossover, a trip to get hours, a trip for MOD2, and then to pay for the Bikini Trip itself. You don't need much, but you do need to work in 2026 to do the training and pay the Bikini balance before 2027.

The other factor with work is that all the exceptional roles require NV1, and that is starting to become a barrier for you. Doing your IRAP assessor course is a fast way to organise that, although you're not sure if you want to spend a significant chunk of time studying for [Cyber - Training & Certifications]({{< relref "posts/Cyber - Training & Certifications.md" >}}) when you're currently so burned out. There's no doubt that you'll do both the CISA and IRAP Assessor courses in 2026 though... he writes hesitating. If IRAP is just a pathway to NV1, why not apply for easy and straight-forward jobs that will sponsor an NV1? Or are you interested in it because it's remote? Remote auditing? 

Something tells me I'll end up accepting a full-time role that is less dysfunctional. I'll do that for a year, earn CISA/CRISC & IRAP, and then see where things stand for 2027. The important thing is that I have plenty of flexibility to write what I need to write and go on the cave diving expeditions that I so keenly want to experience. The last 3 years have been very full-on, and I'm not sure I want to keep doing what I'm doing, but I also don't want to tear up everything I have. Writing is something I simply need to allocate a little time to each and every day. If I need to get up at silly o'clock and write something before I start my day in a regular job, then do it! 

You also know you're very capable of multi-tasking when it comes to work, so you can pretend to be doing one job while preparing for the next. That may be a bonus for taking on something much lighter or fully remote - do what you need to do to stay in the bosses good books, show up without over-achieving, and then make sure you're putting your mind and heart into writing books.

Maybe cybersecurity is the thing that gets you enough money to afford a CCR and a trip to Bikini Atoll, but you don't owe it anything. The real question you need to ask is what DO you owe your heart and mind to? If it's writing, then you should be making damn sure that you're putting an enormous amount of effort into developing a platform to share what you write, and then be turning up every single day to write something that you're proud of. This isn't about legacy, it's about developing and sharing things that you care about and that other people can appreciate.

So do only what you need to with work - find something that pays well and you can be emotionally disengaged from. You don't need to be passionate or try to leave a legacy. You're doing what you're good at for money to be able to live. The bigger question you need to answer is how you want to live.
