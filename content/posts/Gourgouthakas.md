# About

Wikipedia - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourgouthakas
Gourgouthakas (Greek: Γουργούθακας; from "small cutting on rocks, in which rain water is collected and from which animals drink water") is a cave located in the Lefka Ori mountains on the Greek island of Crete. Until resurveying in 2022, it was considered the deepest cave in Greece, with an explored depth of 1,208 metres (3,963 ft),[2] and was the 43rd deepest in the world in 2010.[3] Following the re-survey conducted by the Gourgouthakas expedition of 2022,[4] the depth has been re-evaluated at 1,100 metres (3,600 ft).

Gourgouthakas was discovered in 1990 by the Speleological Mission of the Catamaran team, based in Μontbeliard, France.[5] The depth of 985 m was reached in 1997.[6] In 1998, the same group reached the small lake (sump) at the bottom, at a depth of 1,208 metres (3,963 ft).[7] The lake was first dived in August 2025.

The cave is primarily a deep vertical shaft, filled with intersecting arcades and huge stalagmites and stalactites.[5] Due to its configuration, specialized equipment and expertise is required to explore it

# Notes

- **Gourgouthakas Cave**: The **deepest cave in Greece** and among the deepest in Europe, reaching over **1,200 meters vertical depth**. Located in the Lefka Ori (White Mountains), it’s a challenging vertical cave system requiring advanced SRT (single rope technique).

- **Gourgouthakas Cave Entrance**, 35.2975, 23.9642, Deepest dry cave in Greece; potential unexplored sumps.
