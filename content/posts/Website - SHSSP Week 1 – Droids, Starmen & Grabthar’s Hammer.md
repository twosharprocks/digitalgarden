I’M BACK! There’s a major news post on the way with all the mischief I’ve been up to lately, and with even greater mischief happening in 2016 you can expect some more regular posts.

But before I bring you all up to speed with the last few months and the future, the International Space University’s (ISU) “Southern Hemisphere Space Studies Program” started last week at the University of South Australia’s (UniSA) Mawson Lakes Campus, and since I’m helping run it I figured I’d kick a busy year off with weekly updates from the course![![shssp](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/shssp.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/shssp.jpg)

Many of you will remember I was lucky enough to hang out with [astronauts and watch rocket launches in 2014 for ISU’s course in Adelaide](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/2014/01/14/isu-shs-sp14-astronauts-flags-fan-girls/), so it’s obviously pretty awesome to be invited back to help run this year’s course as one of 4 Teaching Associates (TA).

We kicked off a full day of registration on January 10th, with 31 people from 10 different countries flying in to participate in this year’s course.  The TA’s & faculty were on their feet all day (I racked up +23,000 steps) but we managed to get everyone registered, setup on the university network, moved into apartments, shown to the restaurant and fed, and even introduced to each other at an icebreaker event hosted by our droid of honour – BB8

With all this welcoming it wasn’t until Tuesday that we finally got stuck into some real lecture material, kicking off the academic phase with a lecture from European Space Agency astronaut Jean-Jacques Favier on the origins of the space age. The participants didn’t have long to get their teeth into the lectures though, before we were off to the International Astronaut Panel![![astro panel](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/astro-panel-1024x576.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/astro-panel.jpg)

The 2016 International Astronaut Panel, with Canadian astronaut Dr Robert Thirsk (left), ISU President Walter Peeters (centre), and ESA astronaut Dr Jean-Jacques Favier (right)

The entire 5-week program is based on participants learning the international, interdisciplinary and intercultural nature of the global space industry – the ISU’s “3I’s”. So while the course has a heavy academic program, the participants also need to [organise a stratospheric balloon launch on Australia Day](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/http://w3.unisa.edu.au/unisanews/2016/January/story2.asp), as well as produce a 30 page “white paper” on using space technology to support food & water security. So they’re busy, and the staff are busy *keeping* the participants busy – there’s a reason it’s informally known as the “Insufficient Sleep University”.

That’s not to say we don’t take time out to appreciate what’s going on around us though. Music plays a huge role in every ISU course, with almost every course forming their own band. So with the passing of David Bowie on registration day, we celebrated his life and the start of the program with countless Bowie songs from our musically talented staff & participants, celebrating the [star constellation recently named after him](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/http://www.theguardian.com/music/2016/jan/18/david-bowie-astronomers-give-the-starman-his-own-constellation), as well as playing [Chris Hadfield’s rendition of “Space Oddity”](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020420/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KaOC9danxNo) on the big screen before lectures.

It’s been one hell of a first week for both the participants and the staff, but the SHSSP16 machine is running smoothly. Before the end of week 2 next Sunday, the participants will have submitted their initial white paper proposal, held their first participant debate, and hosted their first culture night.

With live rocket launches, team building activities, a space and security panel, as well as a full week of lectures from industry experts like Dr Joeseph Pelton and Kerrie Dougherty – we’re going to need to keep the high tempo and efficiency to stay on top of it all.

But I wouldn’t be anywhere else – it’s good to be back among the ISU space mafia.
