[![ron](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/ron.gif)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/ron.gif)  I have no idea where to even begin describing the roller-coaster my last 5 months have been, so let’s start with the basics.  I Moved to Melbourne   Yup, up and left. No real warning – just gone. Leaving Perth had been in the works since the start of 2015, and given I’d been visiting Melbourne at least once a month since November it made sense it’d be where I wound up… but I sure as hell wasn’t expecting it to happen so fast. The plan had been to visit Tasmania for a holiday in late April, return to Perth to close out a few things and say goodbye, then leave on May 31st – the day before the Sunday Times named me in their list [“75 People who make WA great”](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.perthnow.com.au/news/western-australia/people-who-make-wa-great-entrepreneurs-health-and-science/story-fnhocxo3-1227377154745).  Instead what happened was:  - Mid April I off-handedly applied for a job in Melbourne as a school lab technician, - The school interviewed me via Skype 5 days later, - They gave me the job an hour after the interview, - I flew to Tasmania for 3 days, - Then flew from Hobart to Melbourne and started work at the school the next day  The whole thing – from applying to starting work – took less than 2 weeks. With that rush I wasn’t fully prepared for when I got here, so for the first month I was commuting 2 hours a day to get to a job I’d initially thought was a part-time, but was actually full-time – 8 hours a day, 5 days a week. Needless to say it was one hell of an adjustment. As with everything though, once you’re over the initial shock and start to figure things out, you adapt and problem-solve. Learning Melbourne’s public transport system, buying a bike, and moving into an amazing share-house in Carlton solved the commuting issues – 10 minute bike ride to one school campus, and a 10 minutes **walk** to the other.  [![dicaprio](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/dicaprio-184x300.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/dicaprio.jpg)  Convenience, bitches  I switched to part-time work   My lab tech job was a lifesaver initially – it got me out of Perth, used my degree and chemistry knowledge, and paid far more than I’d been paid by any job since I left the mining industry. But the plan had *always* been to work part-time – just enough to pay the bills so I could focus my time and energy on writing & speaking about space. **That** is what I’d wanted to move to Melbourne for. So while my job was pretty easy – I had far too much free time – and ticked a lot of boxes, being full-time wasn’t really letting me do what I’d moved here to do.  [![Adding further evidence to the idea I'm just a cartoon character...](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/dexter-300x265.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/dexter.jpg)  It also added evidence to the idea I’m a cartoon character…  After a visit to the Victorian Space Science Education Centre ([VSSEC](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/https://www.vssec.vic.edu.au/)) and bumping into a friend in science communication, I decided I’d prefer the uncertainty of casual science communication work than keep going in a secure job that stopped me from doing all the cool stuff I really wanted to do. Rather than leaving immediately and leaving the school in a lurch, I told the school I’d stay the 6 weeks till the end of Term 3 – that way they had plenty of time to find a replacement for the start of Term 4 & I had a regular pay-check while I found something more suitable.  The school started planning for my departure, and I started looking for a casual science job in between working and [organising everything for National Science Week](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/2015/08/10/news-helena-national-science-week-tour/) … until a week later when the principal struck on the genius idea of having me go part-time.  [![bomb](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/bomb-300x285.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/bomb.jpg)  It’s a great job, but it doesn’t need someone full-time – the reality is I was bored and frustrated that I had to be there 5 days a week. So now I’m on 3.5 days a week I’ve got the part-time job I was after, I’ve reclaimed the time & energy I needed to write (hence why you’re reading this), and the school has an enthusiastic lab technician who’s excited to be there rather than wishing he was doing something else. It also means I can keep riding to work four mornings a week: one hand on the handlebars, the other casually holding a soy latte like a job-less hipster.  But of course right when all this job mischief was being discussed, I was also preparing for National Science Week…  Helena Payload’s National Science Week Tour  In case you missed it [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.abc.net.au/news/2014-12-16/wa-team-selected-for-mars-lander-project/5969188), [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.dailymail.co.uk/sciencetech/article-2874826/The-gadget-help-breathe-MARS-Helena-device-creates-oxygen-water-alien-soil-heading-red-planet-2018.html), [here](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.gizmag.com/breathable-air-on-mars/35265/), or [any](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.news.uwa.edu.au/201412157266/events/engineering-students-aim-generate-first-breathable-air-mars) [one](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.techtimes.com/articles/22282/20141217/australian-students-attempt-to-solve-problem-of-breathable-air-on-mars.htm) of the [dozen](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://phys.org/news/2014-12-students-aim-breathable-air-mars.html) other places it was covered by the global media, I helped UWA’s Motorsport Team develop a payload for Mars One’s University Competition this time last year. What you might not know (because I never mentioned it until the [previous post](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/2015/08/10/news-helena-national-science-week-tour/)) is that the fine folks at Inspiring Australia gave us $15,000 to travel around the country during National Science Week to speak about Helena’s development and the experience of working on an aerospace engineering project. So Andre Van Vulpen, Rowan Heinrich, Claire Quy, and I bundled off for a flash tour of the country – taking in Perth, Hobart, Adelaide, Sydney and Melbourne. And somewhere in there I turned 30 too.  Becoming Adult(ish) and doing Adult(ish) things   Apparently 30 is some sort of arbitrary figure where ordinary people grow up emotionally, settle into a safe and secure job, get married, have 2.4 kids, and start the slow descent into death by mediocrity. But because I’m awesome and intend to die on a different planet to the one I was born on, I turned 30 and got a tattoo of a [7-leaf clover](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://futurama.wikia.com/wiki/Luck_of_the_Fryish);  [![clover](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/IMG_20150901_095501-300x300.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/IMG_20150901_095501.jpg)  100 points if you get why this is about Mars  … and because I’m wildly irresponsible I had a [robot spaceman tattooed on my right ass cheek](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurama:_Bender%27s_Big_Score) at the same time;  [![Bender](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/IMG_20150901_100341-300x300.jpg)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/IMG_20150901_100341.jpg)  By this point you might suspect I watch a lot of Futurama. You would be correct.  Turning my life back into one long punch-line   In July [Mars One announced that the final astronaut selection phase will take place in September 2016](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.mars-one.com/news/press-releases/mars-one-presents-more-details-on-next-astronaut-selection-rounds). The candidates knew there were two possible selection periods (March or September 2016), and after being on this roller-coaster for nearly 3 years already a big part of me was hoping they’d go with March. But instead of being frustrated by another 6 months, I’ve realised  *I’ve got another 6 months to do all the stupid shit I want to do from the [101 Things list](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530020051/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/101-things/) and write/tour a new comedy show about it*.  So now the race is on: since July I’ve attended a film premiere (#44), gotten a tattoo (#75), and I’m still chipping away at the greatest books list (#52). This whole website is due for some serious renovation, and updating the 101 Things list is the first thing to be updated. It’ll continue to be updated too as I do more stupid shit from the list when I return to full-time comedy next year.   I’m currently writing my next science-comedy show “Cosmic Nomad”, so I’ll leaving my lab tech job (properly this time) at the end of 2015 to perform the show throughout 2016 – touring all the way around this shiny blue-green ball we call Earth until the final selection phase in September. The details of the tour are are still being worked out, but I’ll share them here as soon as everything is confirmed.  In the meantime keep an eye out as I start to publish chapters from “Becoming Martian”; the book I wrote last year on how humans will change in physically, psychologically and culturally by colonising Mars. After sitting on the manuscript for 12 months I’ve decided I’d rather it be available for free online, rather than have it remain unread on my hard drive waiting for a publisher to wave a magic wand over it.
