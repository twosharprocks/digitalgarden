**You’re the boss, not your bratty kid**

![](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/lion-roar-300x300.jpg "Roaring Lion")

You’ve already survived growing up – you finished school, lived life, gotten to the point where you can care for yourself, met all sorts of people and had a child with one. At what point is that kid *better*than you? That kid wouldn’t **exist** without you. So until they can feed, bath and care for themselves without your constant physical and financial assistance\*, you’re the boss.

You’re responsible for a developing human being, so act responsibly: set out clear instructions on how to behave, and make sure they are clear on the benefits of following those instructions as well as the consequences of **not** following them. Because any kid that depends on you but acts like your their puppet clearly needs to be reminded about the natural order of things – *fast*. If they know there will be real consequences to disobeying you, they’ll learn to get smarter when they’re up to shit. You’ll still know what’s going on, and even occasionally let them think they’ve gotten away with it, before blowing their minds years later by proving you knew all along (Cheers, Dad – now I’m the one who finds YOUR porn)

But for the most part kids need a clear and instantaneous response to their actions, good or bad. For example last Thursday the family I lodge with had four 10 year olds over. The kids were all running around with Nerf guns, causing all sorts of chaos and having lots of fun doing it. Not a problem – they’ve got lots of energy and playing pretend wars lets them express some of that natural aggression rather than fighting. But they were **specifically** warned not to shoot the landlady or the younger brother. So they did. Repeatedly.

And when they continued to shoot the landlady in the head/face, she called out saying if they didn’t stop she’d “get an ex-Australian soldier to sort them out”. I poked my head out of the downstairs room to see if everything was okay and to get a coffee, saying to the kids in the process to quit battering the landlady. About the time one of them decided to shoot me in the eye with a nerf dart – because clearly at 10 he still hadn’t grasped the concept that “actions” have “consequences”. So I re-educated him.

![](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/respect-300x300.jpg "respect")

I’m not sure if you’ve ever seen someone leap across a room at four 10 year olds with loaded nerf guns, rip the all guns out of their hands, then throw the kid who shot him in the eye across the room into a kitchen oven; all inside of 5 seconds\*\*. But I can assure you it sends a very clear message – don’t shoot a ginger in need of a caffeine-fix.

**Make your kids eat dirt**

[![](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/kid-dog-bowl.jpg "kid dog bowl")](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/kid-dog-bowl.jpg)Here’s a news flash for you all – **almost anyone can have a baby**. So unless you had to seek some sort of medical assistance to conceive, your kid probably isn’t any more special than the 3 minutes of trusting it took to make. Has your 4 year old daughter discovered a cure for cancer while fingerpainting? Is her treatise about a day trip to the zoo worthy of submission to the Nobel committee for Literature? Didn’t think so. So stop treating the little oxygen-stealing bag of amino acids like she’s made out of bone china – tell her to get outside, play kiss-chasey, get dirty and bring home dead animals from the swamp.

If you treat kids like their precious, they’ll wind up being high-maintenance princesses. I’m not saying don’t be there for them, but realise your kid isn’t perfect and scraping knees is part of life. When I was 7 I spent every lunch and recess trying to swing all the way across a set of monkey bars. And after two weeks I made it across. So I immediately tried again, but this time only grabbing every second bar. Needless to say I slipped off and broke my arm. And holy shit did it hurt – I ran to the school nurse balling my little eyes out.

But as my folks said, I’d done it to myself and being upset wasn’t going to make it stop hurting. So I put some concrete in my fruit box and hardened the fuck up. And that was the day I decided to be a badass instead.

[![](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/happiness-281x300.jpg "happiness")](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/happiness.jpg)

Along the same vein, make your kids try out new shit. Sure they might be a football prodigy – but when they inevitably blow out their knees, you’ll wind up being the parent of an autistic savant, minus the savant. Or worse, they’ll become a sports journalist. So encourage your kids to learn lots of different things: teach your son to sew up the hole in his football shorts himself, or teach your daughter to kill a rapist with her bare hands. Or better yet, teach them to learn things for themselves. When the job I’d moved out to Gloucestershire for gave me 6 weeks notice before laying me off, I didn’t stress in the slightest because I knew I could step into one of a dozen different jobs anywhere in the world – because my parents made sure I learnt a broad range of skills (and because I’m awesome).

If kids never get pushed outside of their comfort zone, they’ll wind up living in your basement playing World of Warcraft while they’re “studying photography part-time” instead of getting a real life. And the last thing this world needs is more World of Warcraft players. Or part-time photography students. Or sports journalists…

**Love them, but do weird shit to mess with them**

![](https://web.archive.org/web/20120207185916im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/faces-on-toes-300x155.jpg "Toe Faces")

Your kid might respect you, be a model citizen, and have a broad range of different skills. But if the little bastard can’t laugh at life, you’re not raising a child superstar – you’re raising a future serial killer. You won’t always be there to protect them when everything goes tits up, so why not teach them to laugh at the relatively small stuff? Don’t let your kids get too attached to things, and make sure they develop a sense of humour for when life throws them on their ass. Because it will, and you don’t want them to start making a suit out of the skin of women when it does. Or worse, they could start LARPing…

Knock over their sandcastle or snowman, then help them make another one. Convince them you invented post-it notes, or that sticking 1st class stamps on geese helps them fly south for the winter faster. Then shatter their illusions years later. Help them construct elaborate cushion forts, then bombard them by raining plushy soft-toy based death.

And not a Cirque du Soleil kind of travelling circus – the kind of travelling circus where they’ll be dressed up as winged monkeys and exhibited next to the elephant man.
