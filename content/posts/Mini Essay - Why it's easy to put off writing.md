One of the challenges I routinely face is trying to prioritise writing as a regular practice. or as any kind of practice, regular or not. I know that I love writing, and even as I type this I can feel that sense of creation that I truly crave whenever I sit down and start typing out my thoughts on a keyboard - a particularly visceral experience where each click of the keyboard is another step towards an artistic expression that's yearning to be shared. 

Over and over again however, I find myself either distracted or making other things a priority over sitting down to write something I really want to write. There is SO much in my [Mini Essays]({{< relref "posts/Mini Essays.md" >}}) document which is just begging to be written about, and that's not even taking into account all the writing I want to do towards a cave diving book, all the writing I'd like to do about the cave diving sites I love, or all the writing you have queued up around the #101-Things and your #bucket-list. 

There is an extraordinary amount of writing that you genuinely want to do, but time and time again you find yourself trying to finish other tasks before you'll write. Today is a classic example - you made writing a mini essay your highest priority for today, and you're only just sitting here typing it out at 9pm after a full day of doing other shit. Was that other shit important? Absolutely! You spent a significant chunk of today fixing your [Digital Garden]({{< relref "posts/Digital Garden.md" >}}) so that you can better share the notes and ideas that you have. But was it the highest priority for today? Certainly not. 

I think part of the challenge is not feeling inspired to write about certain things when you sit down. But you also know that inspiration is bullshit - you simply need to write, and the inspiration comes from turning your creative outlet into a habit and an obsession. You have an extraordinarily long list of potential mini essay topics to write about already, so there's no reason why you can't glance at any one of them and start typing. In fact, the entire point of the mini-essays is to start writing when you're NOT inspired! They're there to lower the barrier to entry on writing - something that doesn't mean much, but you can use to process ideas in a quick and easy way without being too invested on whether the writing is good or not. Assume the writing is bad, but simply get writing and turn it into a habit.

The idea of making writing a priority/habit/obsession is all about turning up to work on it every day. My journal is good for processing things on a personal level, and it's nice to slow down and write things out by hand. But what I write there is never going to be published (burning my journals still feels important) and something certainly changes when you know that your writing may have an audience (however small). Journaling the things that shouldn't be said aloud seems vital to my mental health, but not as important as writing out my ideas and making them available for others to read too.

So how do I make writing a priority and avoid being distracted? Easy - you make writing something that you must do each day. Journaling doesn't count: you need to make sure that you write something each day that could be read by other humans. It may have taken you until 9pm tonight to sit down and write this, but you've done it, and 20 mins later you're struggling to wrap this up into a conclusion because you want to keep typing. 

Make writing for publication a priority for each day, even if it's just a mini essay (most of them will probably be mini essays), and before you go to bed each night you need to set a plan on what to write the next day. You can even build it into your Template - Mini Essay - add "tomorrow's mini essay" to the template so that you can put down some sort of plan for the next day before you close out today.
