Week 2 of the Southern Hemisphere Space Studies Program got off to a roaring (if early) start, with many of the participants waking up for 5am to watch SpaceX launch the Jason-3 satellite aboard their final Falcon 9 v1.1 rocket (all future launches are the updated 1.2 version).

The launch went perfectly, the satellite deployed cleanly, and the first stage booster landed softly on the drone ship. But then… well…

[Watch archived video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jshk8ZVIgdI)

Canadian astronaut Dr Bob Thirsk gave his final lecture to the program on “Expeditionary Behaviour” – sharing critical insights into the attitude required to work as an effective team on long-duration spaceflight missions. We couldn’t let our two visiting astronauts leave without gathering our participants together for a group photo though.

![Bob and JJF group photo](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/12622368_1041622162566587_6420706059677567290_o-1024x480.jpg)

With his lectures over [Bob Thirsk wrote about his week with SHSSP16](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922/http://robertthirsk.ca/2016/01/15/a-week-in-australia-with-sh-ssp-students/), before joining our first participant debate. Participant debates are a staple of all ISU courses, where everyone on the course argue for or against a critical idea relevant to space – the trick being they can’t choose which side they had to argue. Our participants were given the challenge of arguing whether humanity should go direct to Mars or return to the moon first – something our [course director John Connolly is deeply interested in](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922/http://www.theleadsouthaustralia.com.au/industries/education/connolly-to-head-up-mars-mission/).

After a lot of laughs and a lot learnt though, it was time to say goodbye to our visiting astronauts…

I also had the chance to help run a couple of workshops this week – working with [Robert DeBelle](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922/http://debelle.com/) to present ISU’s “Crisis Communications” workshop, as well as organising some team building activities that had some frankly hilarious results…

Week 2 saw our participants complete their first academic quiz, as well as a huge ramp up in activity on the stratospheric balloon payload, with many of the participants working some very long hours to ensure everything worked during the Australia Day launch. But among the hive of activity they also found some time to embrace some culture, whether it’s learning about space culture with Kerry Dougherty;

![Kerrie Dougherty on the how existing culture shapes the way we see space](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/kerrie.jpg)

…or relaxing over a BBQ dinner & pavlova/lamingtons/tims tams for dessert, all while watching a sunset from a patio with a koala sitting in the front yard’s gum tree.

![bbq](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/bbq.jpg)

Arguably the most Australian cultural experience ever

After a full-on Week 2, SHSSP16 seriously has their work cut out for them in Week 3 – a full day at the Institute for Telecommunications Research, the launch of their stratospheric balloon payload on Australia Day, another two culture nights, as well as the white paper project ramping up… all alongside their usual load of lectures from world-class space specialists like [Juan De Dalmau](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922/http://www.ctae.org/staff/juan-de-dalmau) (Head of ESA’s ESTEC Communications Office) and [Dr Charley Lineweaver](https://web.archive.org/web/20160530015922/http://www.charleylineweaver.com/) (Convener of the Australian National University’s Planetary Science Institute).

It’s going to be one hell of a week… like **every** week with ISU.
