![Florida](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-300x150.jpg)

By the time I left D.C. the Cosmic Nomad tour was seriously starting to catch up with me. I’d been on the go non-stop since January, completing an Australia-wide tour right on the back of working on the ISU course in Adelaide AND being locked in a box like a Damien Hirst artwork. It was a month since I’d started the overseas leg of the tour, in which I’d done a lot of soul-searching while bouncing from place to place, experience to experience… quite frankly I was feeling a bit wrecked from it all.

![wambulance](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/wambulance-300x171.jpg)

Florida had always been on the agenda to visit, but it was also the biggest unknown – I’d allocated 10 days to tour around, but it wasn’t clear what I wanted to do besides visit the Kennedy Space Center and maybe visit Disney or something… I really hadn’t thought it through. I’d been having such a great time in D.C. that I hadn’t slowed down enough to plan & booked anything in Florida either, figuring I could just sort it out using the wifi on my 19 hour overnight Amtrak train to Orlando.

It wasn’t until I was on the train that I remembered just how frustrating the on-board wifi is.

![ron-swanson-computer-throw-out-parks-and-rec](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ron-swanson-computer-throw-out-parks-and-rec-300x173.gif)

Once I did start to book stuff through the dismal wifi, concerns about finances started creeping in: Orlando is NOT backpacker/space-hobo friendly. There’s no cheap hostel option in Florida as it’s all designed to offer that “complete” experience, where everyone “completely” over-provides and over-charges every step of the way. I quickly decided to shorten my time in Florida down to just 6 days – 3 near Orlando, 3 near Cape Canaveral – before heading back to D.C. for the Humans 2 Mars conference.

As soon as I arrived in Orlando I knew I’d made the right call with shortening the trip: the parallels with summer in Perth were immediately obvious, and I knew (as soon as I stepped off the train in Kissimmee and had to smear on sunscreen, then back it up with a hat and sunglasses) that my gingerness would appreciate the early return to the rain in D.C.

![terminator](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/terminator-300x128.gif)

What I didn’t make the right call on was when I _arrived_ in Florida – turns out I missed SpaceX’s JCSAT-14 launch out of Cape Canaveral **BY A MATTER OF HOURS**. It’d already been delayed once, and while I’d never **hope** for a launch delay I figured the weather would probably push it back again in time for me to be there and see it. And then I woke up with the train in Florida to see pictures like this…
![spacex-landing-image](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/spacex-landing-image-300x200.jpg)

So the “See a Rocket Launch” entry on my [101 Things list](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/101-things/) remains incomplete. But while it was disappointing missing such an awesome launch, all hope is not lost – I’ll be back in the US early September, hopefully in California around the time of SpaceX’s Iridium NEXT launch out of Vandenberg AFB (~Sept 12), or in Florida for SpaceX’s Echostar 23 launch out of Cape Canaveral (~Sept 14).

So after crash landing into Kissimmee, taking a ridiculously long bus ride out to where I picked up my hire car, negotiating Orlando’s roads to find my hotel, and checking in… I just bombed out. For most of the following 3 days.

![dog-humor-funny-just-tired-puppy](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/dog-humor-funny-just-tired-puppy-300x209.jpg)

It’s not even remotely physical exhaustion – it’s a mental exhaustion from trying to juggle too many things at once (where you’re going to sleep that night, where you need to get to next, how you’re going to get there, ect.) while still responding to other people’s requests/needs without pausing long enough to process what’s going on or even really look after yourself.

So I stopped, spent a few days working out what I **actually** wanted to do in Florida, and then did it. That’s not to say I didn’t **do** other stuff – I still went out and met [Benny The Spaceman](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/https://twitter.com/LEGOBenny)…

![florida - benny](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-benny-300x300.jpg)

… and played mini-golf and had [dinner at one of the best steakhouses in the US](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/http://www.talkofthetownrestaurants.com/history/awards/) with the folks who run Benny’s twitter account. I also managed to meet [Hampton Black](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/https://community.mars-one.com/profile/9ba576e8-11db-423d-ae29-1f54e74b808a), an aerospace engineer and fellow Mars One candidate who’s spent most of his life in Florida working in the space industry!

![florida - Hampton](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-Hampton-300x300.jpg)

But really the first few days in Florida was just _stopping_. There had been a vague plan to visit Disney’s Epcot theme park, but I was in an okay hotel with decent wifi and a gym no one else seemed interested in using, and had a car if I _really_ wanted to go anywhere. So instead of being at an amusement park surrounded by screaming kids playing chicken with diabetes; for a few days I tried to attack the email monster, wrote up some of my travels so far, wrote far more in my journal about what had happened the previous weeks, and made the most of having a well-equipped & air-conditioned gym.

And in the evenings I had the option to meet up with people OR STAY IN AND EAT ALONE. Which was highly appealing.

![antisocial](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/antisocial-300x206.jpg)

One of the most incredible moments of the trip involved Disney anyway though. Driving back from the steakhouse after dinner with the Benny the Spaceman folks, I missed the turn off the interstate for my hotel. I took the next exit to try and get back to where I was meant to be, but didn’t register what road I’d exited onto until the sky in front of me started to light up and the windows started to shake – I’d turned onto the main entrance to Disneyland, and had coincidentally done it right when their nightly fireworks display began. I don’t care what you think about Disney, there’s definitely something special about accidentally driving through the main gate to Disneyland with a firework display going off right above your head.

After 3 days of winding back and catching my breath though, I was ready to get on with what I’d ultimately come to Florida for – the Kennedy Space Center. After a surprisingly expensive drive along the toll road from Orlando to Titusville and checking into my hotel, I figured I’d go straight to Kennedy Space Center’s visitor complex to sort out my pass for the next few days and maybe check out a few things before it closed.

![florida - KSC entry](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-KSC-entry-300x300.jpg)

Since I’d be visiting KSC each of the 3 days I was in Cape Canaveral **and** I’d established it was the real reason I was in Florida, I figured I’d go the whole hog and get one of their annual VIP passes…

![Florida - Badge](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-Badge-300x300.jpg)

… so that the next day I’d be able to do a bundle of the tours and have lunch with an astronaut, as well as get free parking & admission when I’m back in Florida around September (just before the 2016 [International Aeronautical Congress](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/https://www.iac2016.org/) in Mexico).

It was 3pm by the time I finally got into the KSC visitor center, but it turns out it doesn’t close until 7pm on a Monday. I figured if I didn’t get far I still had another 2 days to explore it… although I may have overestimated how much I could pack into 4 hours.

![Obligatory selfie in front of the KSC visitor complex NASA globe](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-KSC-300x218.jpg)

Obligatory selfie in front of the KSC visitor complex NASA globe

As soon as you get through the admission gate you’re greeted by KSC’s “Rocket Garden”. Now in most circumstances you’d rightly assume “Rocket Garden” means some hipster’s shitty patch of questionable vegetables in a community garden, but it turned out to be pretty awesome instead:

![Gemini-Titan II that paved the way for the US's lunar Apollo program](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/titan-II-240x300.jpg)

Gemini-Titan II that paved the way for the US’s lunar Apollo program

![saturn 1B](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/saturn-1B-300x273.jpg)

Saturn 1B upper stage for the MUCH larger Saturn V that took men to the Moon

As soon as I managed to pull myself away from the gargantuan rockets (there were **much** bigger ones to see later), it was into NASA’s “Journey To Mars” exhibition, displaying the pathway and some of the often-argued-over technologies that NASA is currently planning to use for future Human Mars exploration missions.

Of course no NASA exhibition about Mars would be complete without a section with the Sojourner & Curiosity rovers…

![florida - rovers](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-rovers-300x300.jpg)

… or an opportunity for me to be a massive space-dork…

![Preferably in real life with an updated mission architecture & without the American flag on the suit...](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-mars-300x232.jpg)

Preferably in real life with an updated mission architecture & without the American flag on the suit…

… or an appearance from [Mr John Connolly](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/http://ssp16.isunet.edu/academics/16-participants/staff/isu/122-john-connolly-usa) – my boss at the [International Space University’s summer program in Haifa](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/http://ssp16.isunet.edu/) until September, when he returns to NASA to become their [new head of Human Mars Mission Planning](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/http://www.theleadsouthaustralia.com.au/industries/education/connolly-to-head-up-mars-mission/).

![florida - JC](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-JC-300x300.jpg)

And a trip to **any** American theme park/exhibition wouldn’t be complete without a trip to the gift shop. I’d end up coming back to the gift shop on my last day to buy a waterproof jacket with the NASA logo embroidered on it, knowing I’d need it for storm chasing two weeks later… but it was nice to dream in the meantime;

![Chances of this surviving the trip home intact 0%](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-cup-300x300.jpg)

Chances of this fitting in my backpack Also 0%

The heat was knocking it out of me, but with a few hours left until the visitor’s complex closed I decided to fully commit to seeing the best KSC had to offer:

![Entrance to the KSC visitor complex's Atlantis exhibition](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-atlantis-entry-300x300.jpg)

Entrance to the KSC visitor complex’s Atlantis exhibition

I’d wanted to save seeing Space Shuttle Atlantis until the next day – I’d been repeatedly told it was the best of all the space shuttle exhibits, so I wanted to experience it when I was fully rested… but the temptation was too great to wait till the next morning;

![SO. TOTALLY. WORTH IT.](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-atlantis-300x187.jpg)

SO. TOTALLY. WORTH IT.

I’m yet to see Space Shuttle Endeavor at the California Science Center, but of the three shuttles I’ve seen so far KSC’s Atlantis exhibition is without a doubt the most extraordinary shuttle experience yet. From walking under the external tank/boosters and passed Carl Sagan’s “The Sky Calls To Us” quote on the wall, to the ground shaking in the theater during the simulated shuttle launch, to the moment the theater screen suddenly goes transparent and you’re greeted with a view of the very final shuttle to fly – seeing Atlantis in all her glory is breathtaking.

I’ve heard from more that a few who’ve been that they became quite emotional – I just know probably shouldn’t have been cutting onions at a space shuttle exhibit.

![florida - atlantis back](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-atlantis-back-300x169.jpg)The shuttle itself is absolutely stunning, and with Atlantis tilted over with its payload doors open to show the inside the hold you get a true sense of just how BIG it is. There’s also a whole bundle of shuttle artifacts on display, from the original shuttle prototype:

![prototype](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/prototype-300x300.jpg)

To the van that took astronauts from their suit-up area to every shuttle flight;![van](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/van-300x206.jpg)

Right through to tires flown on Atlantis for STS-135, the very final shuttle flight:

![florida - tires](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-tires-300x300.jpg)

And while I couldn’t get any pictures of it, the “Shuttle Launch Experience” is **one hell of an experience**. I can guess at how they do it, but numerous astronauts have said that it is easily the most accurate simulation of a real space shuttle launch they’ve experienced outside of _actually_ _riding a space shuttle_. It was pretty incredible, but I’d recommend using the free lockers to secure your wallets, keys, sunglasses, ect – with all the shaking and tilting there is no way you’re going to want that stuff in your pockets.

Beneath space shuttle Atlantis there was also a special section dedicated to two of the space shuttles that didn’t come home: Challenger & Columbia. Personal artifacts of each of the 14 astronauts who perished in the two disasters are displayed in their memory.

![Several of the wall quotes, the gallery, and Ilan Ramon's personal item display](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/disaster-300x300.jpg)

Several of the wall quotes, the gallery, and Ilan Ramon’s display

So the first (half) day at the KSC visitor complex was frankly pretty full-on. I basically collapsed on the bed back at the hotel that night with a hamburger and watched This Week Tonight, until I heard through Twitter around 11pm that the SpaceX barge had returned to Port Canaveral with the landed JCSAT-14 first booster on top of it! Port Canaveral was only 25 minutes away in the car, but at nearly midnight I didn’t like my chances getting there, driving around a port to find it, then driving back – I figured I’d have better luck in the morning.

And luckily, 14 story rockets are pretty easy to spot in the morning daylight;

![florida - falcon](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-falcon-300x300.jpg)

Port Canaveral is obviously an active port so I didn’t want to snoop around too much, but getting a good photo (without chain-link fence in the way) proved surprisingly difficult. Getting from Port Canaveral back to the KSC visitor complex was pretty straight forward though, so I drove in ready for another full on day – starting with lunch listening to, then meeting, Apollo 15 Command Module Pilot Al Worden!

![Apollo 16 CMP Al Worden with yours truly](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-al-worden-300x300.jpg)

Apollo 15 CMP Al Worden with yours truly

The remaining Apollo astronauts are an interesting bunch, so it was fascinating hearing Colonel Worden talk about his experiences before, during and after the Apollo years. As the Command Module Pilot he never walked on the Moon, but stayed in orbit around it for 3 days alone – a unique experience as one of the most physically isolated human beings in history. He’s also one of the very few people who have conducted an EVA in the deep space transiting between the Moon back to Earth.

![Apollo_15_Al_Worden_during_EVA](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Apollo_15_Al_Worden_during_EVA-300x225.jpg)

Al Worden during his Apollo 15 EVA

There’s so much more to see at Cape Canaveral than just the visitor center though, so straight after meeting Al I headed out on a tour of the Cape’s launch pads

![Space Launch Complex 40, leased to SpaceX](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-spaceX-300x300.jpg)

Besides looking at launch facilities from a far, we also got to stand a fairly significant distance from the Vertical Assembly Building;

![The star I'm standing on is exactly the same size as each of the 50 stars painted in the flag on the VAB](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-VAB-300x258.jpg)

The star I’m standing on is exactly the same size as each of the 50 stars painted in the flag on the VAB

As you can tell the “up close” part of the tour was pretty oversold, but again these are working launch facilities. On the flip side, we got significantly closer than we probably wanted to the local alligator population;

![Don't pull over on the side of the road & get out is all I'm saying](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-aligator-300x300.jpg)

Don’t pull over & get out is all I’m saying

After the tour I had to make a critical decision: there was _just_ enough time to get out to the Apollo/Saturn V center to have a look before it closed, but it would mean missing out on getting Al Worden’s signature at the same time. I couldn’t do both, so while I enjoyed meeting Al and lunch wouldn’t have minded getting him to sign a photo, I really couldn’t miss the opportunity to see a full-sized Saturn 5.

![I can't even begin to describe how big this thing is](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-Saturn-V-300x257.jpg)

I can’t even begin to describe how big this thing is

The Apollo center is also home to the original firing room used for the launch of Apollo 8 – the first launch to send humans around the Moon and back;

![Florida - Control room](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-Control-room-300x169.jpg)

The firing room was a complete experience just like space shuttle Atlantis, with the full Apollo 8 launch being re-enacted through voice-over and lights showing the control panel operators confirming GO/NO-GO for the launch. I was genuinely blown away by it all. Out in the main hall near the Saturn V was also the Apollo 15 Lunar Module that never was;

![florida - Apollo 15 LEM](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/florida-Apollo-15-LEM-300x300.jpg)

The Apollo 15 LEM was completely built and ready, but a redesign to incorporate the lunar rover meant it was cheaper and easier to build a whole new LEM and launch it rather than modify this one – so it survived for display, and the other one is in pieces on the Moon. Ultimately the only things that came back to Earth in one piece from _any_ of the Apollo missions were the Command Modules… and they had one of those on display too;

![Apollo 14 Command Module](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-Apollo-14-300x300.jpg)

Apollo 14 Command Module

The Apollo/Saturn V center was closing, but I managed to catch the very last show in the Apollo theater before the final bus back to the visitor center left me there;

![apollo](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/apollo-300x300.jpg)The show was a perfect end to what had been an absolutely extraordinary couple of days at KSC. When I flopped back into bed in the hotel that night I was overwhelmed by everything I’d been lucky enough to see – so many things you’ve read about, thought and dreamed about for so long… right there in front of you. Real.

After all the exhibits, tours, lunch with an astronaut, and so much more; I had one more thing left to do at the Kennedy Space Center. I arrived early on the third day and had a breakfast burrito & coffee outside at one of the cafes, making a new friend in the process;

![Breakfast at KSC: "Squirrels are cute" *jumps up leg, steals food, runs away* "Squirrels are assholes"](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/squirrel-300x300.jpg)

Breakfast at KSC: “Squirrels are cute” *jumps up leg, steals food, runs away* “Squirrels are assholes”

Then headed into the IMAX theater to watch a movie about the future of human Mars exploration narrated by Patrick Stewart (which subtly mentions Mars One)… but I was really just killing time. Because what I was _really_ back at KSC for was to meet another Mars One candidate: [George Hatcher](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243/https://community.mars-one.com/profile/721f2fb0-4b97-4379-b7e5-25a4080fadde)!

![GINGERS REPRESENT!](https://web.archive.org/web/20160628102243im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Florida-George-300x300.jpg)

GINGERS REPRESENT!

I’d put out a call to Mars One candidates in Florida to meet up while I was there, and both Hampton & George had responded, but logistics made all three of us being in the same place impossible. It was great getting to meet George – like Hampton he worked on the space shuttles when they were flying, but he now works as a planetary scientist at Kennedy Space Center. He’d been flat-out the days I was exploring the visitor complex, but there was enough time for us to meet up, have lunch on my last day in Florida, and get a selfie in front of the Atlantis exhibit building.

After saying goodbye to George I headed back to Orlando, returned my hire car and got an Uber to the train station – ready to start the next leg of my adventure _back_ in Washington D.C. for the Humans 2 Mars conference. Florida has been the best and the worst of the trip so far – overpriced & difficult to get around, and yet there was something really magical about being at the Kennedy Space Center. I was frustrated at having missed the SpaceX launch, and yet incredibly grateful for the 3 days I spent in the Orlando hotel just taking care of overdue responsibilities and myself. Florida knocked a hole in my finances more than it should have, yet the entire trip would have felt incomplete without it.

Climbing back on board the overnight Amtrak back to Washington D.C. I was both genuinely glad I’d come and equally glad to be leaving, and grateful again for the time on those long train trips give to think and write about it what’s going on – there was no way I’d have the opportunity to really process things again until I was well and truly on the West Coast, preparing to leave for my two months in Israel with ISU.
