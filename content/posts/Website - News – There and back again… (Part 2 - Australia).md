**Adelaide (September 27-October 1st)**

Make no mistake – I’ve done the flight between London & Australia numerous times now. In 2010 alone I did it 6 times. What I **hadn’t**done befor was fly into Australia at 8am *on grand final day* and then experience the sinking feeling that everything is closed and you can’t check in till 2pm… Nor have I ever called my Mum immediately after one of these flights and babbled on deliriously outside a Woolworths.

Anyway, I **did** eventually manage to check-in and get some sleep, even in a 6 bed dorm – never doubt the power of the classic “Sleep deprivation & industrial earplugs” combo. It was just as well, as the next morning I needed to be up to help organise Australia’s first [SpaceUp conference](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.spaceupaustralia.org/), hosted by Flinders University.

We had some fantastic speakers throughout the day, from students to asteroid miners, defence personnel to commercial space start-up operators.

And the day after the SpaceUp conference? That’s when the Australian Space Research Conference (ASRC) started. In fairness the ASRC *could* have been a nice change of pace from the previous 4 weeks of chaos: food and coffee abounded, as an ISU/UniSA student I still had wifi access in the lecture theatres, and you could pick and choose which presentations suited you best. But why would I take a chance to slow down?

Day 1 (Monday) of the conference was reasonably chilled out (and gave me a chance to answer a few urgent emails), but it wasn’t to last. Day 2 (Tuesday) of the conference was the science communication stream, so I was back out there again: talking about all my National Science Week events, presenting the success I’d had at integrating comedians and poets into space science communication, as well as talking about the incredible outreach potential of the Mars One project.

Glad to say quite a few handy contacts were made, I just wish I’d had time to follow them all up since! After the Tuesday night conference dinner and some **very** misguided late-night karaoke, I skipped out on the third and final day of the ASRC to get myself to Newcastle in time for…

PREEEEEEETTY

I’d hit Newcastle on the Wednesday – a day before the festival technically started – so I’d finally have a chance to pass out without some early morning event or travel trauma waiting for me the next morning. It’s amazing what happens when you’re not LIVING ON THE GOD DAMN EDGE FOR ONE NIGHT. As it was I had to get up on the Thursday to speak to the folks at 1233 ABC Newcastle anyway… but it was such an amazing interview at the relatively luxurious hour of 11 am that I didn’t mind. There’s a [write-up & recording of the interview with Jill Emberson](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.abc.net.au/local/stories/2014/10/02/4099026.htm) that’s well worth checking out.

On the way back, I managed to find an amazing cafe with great breakfasts and free wifi to **finally** sort out a few more urgent emails and find out what was going on with the interview Channel 9’s Today show had booked me for in their Sydney studio for the start of World Space Week the coming Monday. The producer I’d been dealing with had gone on holidays a week before, but had assured me that one of her colleagues had taken over. So when I called the Today show’s Executive Producer to say “Hey, I’m in your studio at 8am Monday, what time do you need me there?” and he didn’t know anything about me or the World Space Week segment…

I got back to the hostel to find I had to move rooms too, but in the grand scheme of my global movements this was nothing. However, in the grand scheme of the *trip’s success* it was an **awesome** turn of events. Seems the festival organisers were grouping a bunch of “quiet writers” into my original room (Room 1) and shifting all the maniac comedians, musicians, journalists & general mischief makers into one big nest of generalised madness in Room 2.

In order of Room 2 appearance: [Dylan “Earplugs” Burns](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/dylan-burns/), [Matt “The Friendly Rocker” Tomich](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/matthew-tomich/), [Lauren “Boklecopter” Bok](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/lauren-bok/), [Rob “Scared Bret Easton Ellis” Coleman](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/robert-coleman/), [Claire “The Naked Beagle” Sullivan](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/claire-sullivan/), [Dave “Trivia Warcrime” Warneke](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/dave-warneke/), and [this guy](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/josh-richards/). Very special mentions to honorary Room 2 members [Kaitlyn “Live Tweet” Plyley](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/kaitlyn-plyley/) & [Alan “Wolfman” Vaarwerk](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/alan-vaarwerk/) too.

Dylan & I had gotten in particularly early and gone to the [Meet & Greet](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/meet-and-greet/), where we both stood around awkwardly until I took a step to introduce myself to a lady raiding the corn chips. She asked me what I did, I told her, then she scowled and told me it was the worst pick-up line ever. Which was pretty unkind, but probably not as unkind as what I replied with. Then I felt the overwhelming desire to leave…

The next day I had to tell a bunch of writer’s [How to Become an Astronaut](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/become-astronaut/) for two hours, which is considerably more challenging than it sounds when you’re in a 30°+ room. Lauren & Claire came along to keep things rolling with questions, but most of the audience just started at me in a mixture of confusion & heat stroke. Thankfully the [Writers Other Jobs](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/writers-jobs/) panel I did an hour later was air-conditioned, and we had some really great discussion going. Room 2 hero Rob, and honorary member Alan, joined me on the panel with [Duncan Felton](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/duncan-felton/) to talk about the stuff we do when we’re not writing.

So basically  a librarian, an editor, an editorial director and an astronaut candidate talk about their day jobs… Pick the odd one out.

Called it early on the Friday – it was just **way** too good knowing I was done by 6pm and didn’t have to travel anywhere the next day. In retrospect,  pretty glad I **did** call it early… because it’d be the last real sleep I’d get for the next 3 days. Or for the 4 days after that too…

The Saturday itself was pretty relaxed – went for a run, had an awesome breakfast and brainstormed some ideas for trying (again) to get [Kerbal Space Program into Aussie schools](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.kerbaledu.com/). I bounced around between a few events, before tagging along to see Lauren and Claire represent Room 2 on the [Funny Ladies](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/funny-ladies/) panel with [Kara Jensen-Mackinnon](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/kara-jensen-mackinnon/) & [Gen Fricker](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/artists/genevieve-fricker/) for what was arguably the best event of the festival.

The last thing either of us needed was to be back in the same little bakehouse I’d done my How To Become an Astronaut workshop in while running on 4 hours sleep and a hangover. But Lauren’s workshop was brilliant regardless: the folks who came were all blown away by what they’d produced & enthusiastic to try their material for real. I found out later Lauren had run her first comedy workshop through [Laneway Learning](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://melbourne.lanewaylearning.com/about-us/) – the same guys I’m now looking to run Kerbal Space Program workshops with in Melbourne.

Anyway a bunch of Room 2 were leaving that same afternoon, so after an audio-guided tour to the beach and some epic chicken snacks, there were goodbyes and the bulk of Room 2’s awesomeness left. For the rest of us though, we had to prepare for our final events – Dave with his [Epic Word-Nerd Battledome](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/epic-word-nerd-battledome/), Matt sexing it up at [Play That Sexy Music](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/play-that-sexy-music/), and me… ranting about mutant space ants eating the Earth at [After the Apocalypse](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.youngwritersfestival.org/festival/apocalypse-late-night-dystopian-readings/).

Sally and I traded sci-com war stories while passing some fairly harsh commentary on the exhibits – the Powerhouse generally seems like a fairly “traditional” museum with a few interactive bits, whereas we both actively encourage kids to ask questions and investigate science in a sandbox- type environment rather than just *showing* them stuff and hoping they’ll care.

That said, I’m very pleased with The Mars Lab – I’d met the team that run it at the ASRC the week before – and I’m **really** looking forward to being there on [November 27th for a Mars One meetup to talk to schools](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.themarslab.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/MarsLabVideoConferenceWithAustralianMarsOneCandidates.pdf).

**Brisbane (October 7th – 9th)**

The morning after the flight to Brisbane I was heading down to speak at Windaroo Primary, where Wyatt from ABC’s “Life at Nine” series goes to school. I’d contacted Wyatt’s science teacher [Rebecca Johnson](https://web.archive.org/web/20141207194026/http://www.rebeccajohnson.com.au/) back in August after I’d seen Wyatt talking about what he wanted to do when he grew up: “An artist, and a scientist… and in the army so I can save people”. Having done **all three** I thought I might have something to offer.

Although I will never have Richard Dean Anderson’s majestic mullet

The visit was wonderful with the kids asking some amazing questions about Mars One… but almost immediately it all started to fall apart. Not the school – me and any form of travel. First I missed the bus out of Windaroo and waited an hour. Then I missed then connecting bus back to Brisbane. Dinner with my sister was uneventful, but the next morning I was heading down to Toowoomba to speak at the University of Southern Queensland (USQ) and I’d lost my debit card. My sister lent me hers, then I found mine again…

So instead I did my presentation on using comedy, poetry & Stevie the Space Lemur in space science communication, jumped in a car on the way to Brisbane’s Western suburbs with the head of USQ’s Astronomy group for an hour & a half discussion about physics, psychology and philosophy… then spent the next 2 hours on buses trying to get back to my sister’s…

You’d think the bus-terror would keep me awake, but I’d genuinely hit the wall – from around the time most of Room 2 cleared out on the Sunday afternoon I was on borrowed time, and it ran out after the Windaroo visit. I startled awake as the bus stopped at Roma Street, and I leapt off it with all my worldly possessions… except my wallet which had fallen out of a side pocket as I hustled off the bus. Thankfully I’d kept my passport & an emergency debit card separate so it wasn’t a huge loss, but it was a clear indicator I just **needed to go home now please.**
