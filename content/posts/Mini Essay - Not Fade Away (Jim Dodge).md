"Not Fade Away" turned out to be quite an interesting read after the initial confusion around the book by Ben Torres-Fong of the same name (accidentally read first before realising the author was different>. There's no way I would have read it unless it had been on the [101 Things - Complete List]({{< relref "posts/101 Things - Complete List.md" >}}) list, but that's the case with so many of those books anyway.

The narrative within a narrative was interesting, and was something that was easy to forget when I was in the thick of George's story and not realising what I was reading was being told by George to someone else. Besides "Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance" I've not read an "on the road" book like it before, and a lot of it felt like going places just to go there, not to actually do anything. Even the idea of burning the Big Bopper's car seemed arbitrary - it really was just about the journey in the end, and the people you meet along the way.

The characters had high's and lows. Double-Gone didn't impress me at all, and the World's Greatest Salesman just felt weird. The car's final destruction seemed to come out of nowhere, but that's more of a reflection that I was enjoying the last 20% of the book - after it feeling like a slog in some of the earlier parts, the last 5th of the book seemed to rip by and suddenly it was done.

I'm not sure what I feel about it in the end - it was certainly a fun read once I got my head around the style. I don't know if I'd recommend it or not, but I'm certainly glad I read it.
