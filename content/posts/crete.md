# About

[**Speleological Club of Crete** (S.P.O.K.)]( https://spok.gr/en/%CE%BF-%CF%83%CF%8D%CE%BB%CE%BB%CE%BF%CE%B3%CE%BF%CF%82-english/) - Runs expeditions

Crete, the largest of the Greek islands, offers **significant caving and some limited but promising cave diving opportunities**, thanks to its complex geology and extensive karst systems. The island is dominated by limestone and dolomite formations, especially in its mountainous interior, which has led to the development of thousands of caves—some still unexplored or only partially mapped.

---
### **Dry Caving Opportunities**

Crete is renowned in speleological circles for its rich variety of dry caves:
#### **Notable Dry Caves**
- **[Gourgouthakas Cave]({{< relref "posts/Gourgouthakas.md" >}})**: The **deepest cave in Greece** and among the deepest in Europe, reaching over **1,200 meters vertical depth**. Located in the Lefka Ori (White Mountains), it’s a challenging vertical cave system requiring advanced SRT (single rope technique).
- **Sternes**: [Wikipedia entry](https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%A3%CF%84%CE%AD%CF%81%CE%BD%CE%B5%CF%82_(%CE%A3%CF%80%CE%B7%CE%BB%CE%B1%CE%B9%CE%BF%CE%B2%CE%AC%CF%81%CE%B1%CE%B8%CF%81%CE%BF))

### **Cave Diving Opportunities**

Cave diving in Crete is **limited** but **not absent**. Several submerged cave systems and karst springs exist, though few are documented in detail. The potential lies mostly in:
#### **Karst Springs and Resurgences**
- **Vrisses and Stylos Springs (Apokoronas region)**: Strong karstic resurgence points that may offer diveable passages, though exploration has been minimal. These springs show high flow and are fed by underground river systems that may be accessible in dry seasons or with appropriate staging.
#### **Coastal Karst**
Crete’s limestone coastline also presents the **possibility of submerged sea caves**. While many are shallow and wave-formed, some deeper submerged karst systems may exist, especially near vertical cliffs in regions like Sfakia or Plakias. These would require further exploration.

---
### **Potential for Exploration**
Crete’s cave systems are relatively underexplored from a **cave diving** perspective. Its extensive mountainous karst, high rainfall zones, and strong resurgence springs strongly suggest unexplored sumps or submerged passages in deeper systems.

---
### **Logistics and Support**
- **Local caving groups**: The Hellenic Speleological Society and its branches in Crete (especially in Heraklion and Chania) are excellent contacts for access, permits, and unpublished cave data.
- **Access**: Some caves are on private land or in protected areas (e.g., national parks in the Lefka Ori), so permits or landowner coordination may be needed.
- **Best Season**: Late summer and early autumn (September–October) are ideal for cave diving, when water levels are lowest and visibility is highest.

---
### **Summary**
Crete is a karst-heavy island with **world-class dry caving**, including extreme vertical caves and vast labyrinthine systems. While **cave diving is less developed**, the presence of active karst springs, underwater resurgence systems, and coastal karst hints at significant potential for future exploration by skilled diving teams. It’s a ripe opportunity for first discoveries and survey work in underwater speleology.
