# About

[Matka Canyon Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matka_Canyon) - [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/2218647744?multi_permalinks=10162120691602745&hoisted_section_header_type=recently_seen)

North Macedonia offers some intriguing caving opportunities, particularly for dry cave exploration, with limited—but potentially promising—cave diving potential. The country lies within the Dinaric karst region, which spans much of the western Balkans and is known for extensive limestone formations, karst plateaus, sinkholes, and underground rivers. Here's a breakdown of what you can expect:

---
### **Dry Caving Opportunities**
North Macedonia is home to hundreds of known caves, many of which remain poorly explored. Some of the most significant include:
- **Slatinski Izvor Cave** (also known as Slatina Cave): One of the longest and most beautiful caves in North Macedonia. Located near Demir Hisar, it features galleries, speleothems, and a river system. While dry sections are well-documented, parts of the cave remain unexplored and could offer sump diving potential.  
- **Vrelo Cave**, Matka Canyon: This is perhaps the most famous cave in the country and where cave diving becomes especially relevant.

---
### **Potential for Further Exploration**

Given the widespread karst landscape, it's likely that many spring sources and sumps in the country remain unmapped or uninvestigated. Particularly promising are:

- **Karst springs in the Mavrovo and Pelagonia regions**, which could hide resurgence caves.
- **Sinkholes and poljes in the western and southern mountain ranges** may conceal deep vertical systems or hydrological connections between dry and submerged cave systems.

---
### **Regulatory and Access Notes**

- Caving and diving are largely unregulated at the hobbyist level, but protected areas like Matka Canyon may require permits for organized expeditions.
- Collaboration with local speleological societies such as the Macedonian Speleological Federation is advisable for gaining access and understanding the current state of cave mapping.
