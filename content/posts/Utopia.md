# Additional Notes

Anchor the boat at a point where the cliffs tower 400m over the sea. Follow a rocky, triangular shaft down to 28 meters. The entrance of this giant cave system looks like “the gate to the center of the earth.” Huge halls open up and the path leads parallel to the cliffs until finally the cave leads inland. The glow of the lamps is barely enough to illuminate the sidewalls. This system is not suitable for everyone and should only be visited by experienced cave divers. The dive should be done with mixed gases as air quickly reaches its limits and long decompressions are required.

EQUIPMENT CONFIGURATION:
A rebreather is ideal for this cave. Backmount and sidemount are acceptable, but you’ll want a couple of stages to really see the belly of the beast.

GASES:
Nitrox (for easier Penetrations)
Trimix (for longer Excursions)

EXPLORATION:
Toddy has been researching “Utopia” Since 2005 and has since found several tunnels, chambers, and new passages. The cave was discovered by the HFGOK in the late 90s and systematically explored up to 4.2 km. The current size of the cave is over 7km. Line has been placed and extended by the likes of Rick Stanton and Markus Schaffhäutle, but the cave continues into uncharted territory. Toddy, with help from Oliver Steinle, created a personalized habitat for increased comfort on decompression stops exceeding 10 hours, to push deeper into the system.
- **Cave Atlas:** http://caveatlas.com/systems/system.asp?ID=593&co=IT

Grotta Utopia is an amazing cave system, one can describe it briefly as “Door to the inner side of earth”. After an approximately 30 minutes boat ride we reach an enormous rock formation where the cliffs reach heights of over 400 meters high. Right in front of such a cliff, we lower the anchor. The dive leads to 13 meters deep. The triangular entry, shaped out of solid rock glides down to a depth of 33 meters. The actual entry to the enormous cave system is located on the bottom of this shaft. Impressive halls appear out of nowhere and the first tunnel leads parallel with the cliffs on the coast until the cave finally leads in to the inner side of the mountain. The system gets larger as you continue as well as the depth (33 m slowly going down to 40m).

The shape of the cave is almost cross round and the beam of the primary light hardly illuminates the inner walls of the system. After approximately 25 minutes we reach an impressively large hall in shaped as a bowling ball. The system goes deeper and the bottom reaches a depth of approximately 50 meters where the cave continues its path. This cave system is not suitable for just any cave diver and should only be visited by the experienced cave diver. The dive should be executed with several different gasses as the limits are reached fairly soon on compressed air resulting in long decompression.

Equipment configuration: Back mount and Stage.
- **SKEP:** https://protecsardinia.com/skep/

TIME FRAME:
The exploration took place on July 22nd – 28th, 2019. Various preparations of the equipment, eg tests of underwater habitat, scooter systems, rebreathers were successful and the dive lasted over 11 hours with a depth of 120m. The deep shaft after more than 4.5 km was further explored by Thorsten Wälde with the result that the shaft ended after about 300 m and a further intention in this passage is now impossible. When diving back, he found another corridor at -50m, which he now wants to explore with his team in 2021.

TEAM:
The team consists exclusively of experienced cave divers who come together from several countries to ensure extensive support. This support is divided into different zones, starting with a surface crew that takes care of the water supply and boat logistics.

Then there is a team whose attention is paid to the underwater habitat and the safety precautions in the entrance area of ​​the cave (gas logistics, scuba tank depot, etc.). Another "deep-support" team, which will support me in the deep part of the cave and will give help when putting on and taking off the scooter system.

The entire project is accompanied by a diving doctor to ensure competent on-site assistance in an emergency. The dives are also documented and evaluated by medical examinations for research purposes.

And of course there are film and photo teams for the surface area as well as for deeper parts in the cave to document the project around the exploration dive.

DIVE/DIVE PROFILE:
The cave can be reached by boat from the port of Cala Gonone in about 40 minutes. The entrance to the cave is at a water depth of approx. 13 m directly at the foot of the cliffs of the Supramonte massif.   Through a narrower shaft you can reach the horizontal part of the system at a depth of approx. 30 m , which now   remains in the depth range between 30 and 45 m for approx. 1 hour diving time. Several ascents and descents follow, until the cave rises to a depth of approx. 15 m and then drops to the first deeper level of - 80 m. After that, the cave slowly moves upwards again to a depth of 15 m. At this point, about 4500 m from the entrance, begins

the exciting part of the exploration. A vertical shaft that drops to a depth of 120 m has to be overcome in order to finally reach the previously unexplored areas of the Utopia Cave. This is where the actual exploration and measurement of the unknown begins.

The varying depths and the long distances to be covered on this route pose a great challenge for the diver, as an extremely complex dive profile is created.   Physical preparation and fitness as well as detailed planning and optimization of all technical aspects (diving gases used, device settings, ascent rates, etc.) are very important for the physiological aspect of decompression.

RECORD DIVE:
Although the focus of our project is clearly on exploring the cave system, another aspect is of particular importance due to the expected duration of the dive. If the expected goals can be achieved and a significant distance of re-survey can be added to the existing plan, one of these dives has a high probability of becoming the longest penetration (solo/DPV) in an underground water-filled cave system (single entrance).

The previous record from 2004 by Gilberto Menezes is 6400 m.
