So you’re all aware, I’ve been discussing seriously about getting out of stand-up since September last year.  
I cut down my number of gigs this year significantly to see if taking a more relaxed approach would help. But without the support of an agency, manager, or even just someone to lend a hand; it still doesn’t feel like it’s worth the emotional physical & financial strain.

A lot of good people over the years have made promises to help me out or attend shows, and it’s unfortunate that most of them failed to follow through – my recent trip to Melbourne & Sydney being the most obvious case. There have been a few wonderful folks who have always been there, always supported what I’m trying to do. But 99.99% of what I get from others is lip service. If I say I’ll help someone or I’ll be somewhere to support them, I mean it. One story I tell in Mars Needs Guitars is how – even after all the near-death-experiences and risky shit I’ve done – the most scared I’ve ever been was when I thought I might not be strong enough to save 3 of my friends and they’d die. The hardest thing I’ve had to learn is that having that level of loyalty and integrity is the exception, not the rule.

I put myself on the line with every show I do. I can’t fake how I’m feeling onstage, so you get the raw me. Emotionally naked. “Apocalypse Meow” may have been about Doomsday theories, but it was written as a metaphor for being deeply lost after leaving the military, being homeless in London for 4 months, and how you dig yourself out of that. I lost hundreds of British pounds & 5 kg performing that show in Edinburgh, and the company that got me off the streets decided not to renew my contract shortly after it. [![Apocalypse Meow Poster No quote1 212x300 News   Turning Off the Mic](https://web.archive.org/web/20140921214458im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Apocalypse-Meow-Poster-No-quote1-212x300.jpg "News   Turning Off the Mic")](https://web.archive.org/web/20140921214458/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Apocalypse-Meow-Poster-No-quote1.jpg)

“Keith Looks Back In Anger” may have been me in a grey koala suit singing awful songs on ukulele, but Keith the Koala was created purely so I could tell stories from my time with the Australian Army & British Royal Marines that were too difficult to tell as myself. Stories that I’d shared with only a handful of people before. Stories I still struggle with. The koala suit put distance between me and the content – it wasn’t ME talking about the awful shit that happened, it was a character. The ONE reviewer who managed to see the show gave it 5 stars and compared me to a young Australian Jack Black. There was only one reviewer because the show had to be cancelled after 6 performances – the biggest crowd I had was 7, and I performed 3 shows to completely empty rooms. Because the venue manager set my show start time 15 minutes before the venue even opened, because the friend who offered to be my manager bailed just before the festival, and I couldn’t afford to advertise the show more than I did.[![keith poster 212x300 News   Turning Off the Mic](https://web.archive.org/web/20140921214458im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/keith-poster-212x300.jpg "News   Turning Off the Mic")](https://web.archive.org/web/20140921214458/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/keith-poster.jpg)

As such, “Josh Richards – Mars Needs Guitars” at National Science Week is likely to be my last solo show for a very long time. It takes all the topics & emotions from the previous two shows, and adds another level – Hope. It’s still comedy just like the previous two, but it’s also my way of saying that becoming an astronaut is something I’ve publicly and secretly wanted since I was 7. Everything else I’ve ever done has been influenced by a desire to go into space, but it was tempered for years by being told it was too hard, too dangerous, or impossible because of my nationality. Now I don’t care what the skeptics think – this is something I’d die for, something I’m more than capable of doing, and I have a very real opportunity to achieve. And it’s something I hope some of you will support, even join.

If you haven’t already bought tickets (and I know from the ticket register many of you who’ve said you would, haven’t) please follow the link and make sure you do. It’s the show I’m proudest of by far, and all the profits are going to the cause I’m dedicating my life to – Mars One and the Human Settlement of Mars.
