Somewhere between memory transplants and three-breasted women, Arnie managed to tell himself  ”Get your ass to Mars”. Well, I’ve been busting my ass trying to do exactly that – spreading the word about Mars One through every conceivable medium known to man. Every medium except through my long-suffering website it seems…

So to help rectify this gross communicative short-coming, here’s the latest updates on Mars!

- Mars One [hit 78,000 applicants](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/http://newsfeed.time.com/2013/05/09/78000-people-apply-for-one-way-trip-to-mars/) in the first 2 weeks – new application numbers haven’t been released since the start of May, so expect this number to keep pushing toward the expected 500,000 before application close August 31st
- The [Aspiring Martians Group on Facebook](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/https://www.facebook.com/groups/aspiringmartians/) has exploded with activity in the last few weeks after it was mentioned in Mars One’s monthly applicant email. The [Aspiring Martians Wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/http://aspiring-martians.wikia.com/wiki/Aspiring_Martians_Wiki) is growing equally rapidly, providing all the other answers you could possibly need about the Mars One mission. Have a look to see what the buzz is about – I’m a regular contributor to both.
- Results from Mars rover Curiosity’s radiation sensor have been released, and [show that anyone doing a round-trip to Mars is likely to fractionally exceed NASA’s exposure limits](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/http://www.guardian.co.uk/science/across-the-universe/2013/jun/03/space-radiation-mars-mission-debate). Just as well we’re only going one-way ![:D](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-includes/images/smilies/icon_biggrin.gif)
- The folks over at [The Verge](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/http://www.theverge.com/ "The Verge") have been covering Mars One fairly consistently – highly recommend having a read of Katie Drummond’s article on wearable sensors titled [“How To Prevent An Astronaut Bloodbath”](https://web.archive.org/web/20131108030812/http://www.theverge.com/2013/5/30/4377120/nasa-mars-psychology-wearable-sensors-astronauts).
