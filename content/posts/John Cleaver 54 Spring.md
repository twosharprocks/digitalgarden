# Additional Notes

(Translated from French)
Situation :
16km exactly west of the border between the states of Victoria and South Australia, 800m north of the ocean coast. On the 1:50,000 map, Gambier sheet 7022-II+7021-I

East of Port Mc-Donnell, near Eight Mile Creek habitat, 1450m south of Ewens Pond. On the private property of John Cleaver (Armagh Ranch).

History

Given its special location hidden from view, this source is known to very few people. According to the owner, David Williams and Noël Stratmann recently came to dive there. An exploration was carried out by O.Knab within the framework of the ASMF 86 project.

Description
Water wells up from a rising crack in the Gambier sandstone. The John Cleaver 54 spring absolutely resembles the well-known Piccaninne Ponds spring.

The oval of the basin measures approximately 40m x 9m and the launching is done by the natural spillway from which the overflow flows, forming a small stream. The dive, leaving a small underwater cave on the left, leads to a canyon 1m wide and 9m deep. A massive block embedded there blocks the entrance into the spring. At 5m depth we find a crack in which we must engage to continue diving. The maximum depth is -15m.

The edges of the clogged slot are bedrock on which pebbles are strewn. In the upper part of the basin there is a very beautiful underwater vegetation.

Water quality
The water is extraordinarily clear as it is found only in South Australia (this is due to its particular chemical properties). Visibility >30m, temperature +15°C.
