If you're planning unique cave diving expeditions for 2026 and want to avoid the Americas, there are several regions around the world—especially in Africa, Asia, and Oceania—with extensive limestone karst, underexplored systems, or untapped potential for first dives. Here’s a curated list of promising destinations, including both established and frontier zones:
### **1. [Madagascar](/posts/madagascar/)**

**Why:** Massive karst regions like the Tsingy de Bemaraha and Ankarana contain hundreds of limestone caves, many barely explored or surveyed. The warm climate and remoteness suggest the potential for flooded sections, especially during the wet season.  
**Status:** Speleologists have mapped many dry caves, but little to no published cave diving.  
**Opportunity:** First cave dives, exploration of submerged passages during seasonal floods.

---
### **2. [Papua New Guinea](/posts/papua-new-guinea/)**

**Why:** The Muller Range and Hindenburg Wall have extensive limestone with large resurgences and sinking rivers. Water clarity is high in karst regions.  
**Status:** Some dry cave exploration has occurred, but very little cave diving.  
**Opportunity:** Potential for world-first underwater surveys and discoveries. Strong community engagement and logistical planning would be essential.

---
### **3. [Iran](/posts/iran/) (Zagros & Alborz Mountains)**

**Why:** Iran has vast limestone regions and one of the deepest known caves in Asia (Ghar Parau). High-altitude karst regions could host phreatic systems.  
**Status:** Limited caving and almost no known diving due to political/logistical challenges.  
**Opportunity:** Advanced logistical planning could allow rare access to pristine cave diving opportunities.  
**Note:** Current geopolitics and travel safety would need close monitoring.

---
### **4. [Vietnam](/posts/vietnam/) (Phong Nha–Ke Bang National Park)**

**Why:** Home to the largest cave in the world (Son Doong), with massive underground rivers. Many flooded caves remain unexplored.  
**Status:** Some cave diving has occurred but very limited compared to the size of the karst.  
**Opportunity:** Access via local cave tour companies or direct collaboration with British-led expeditions.

---

### **5. [Sulawesi - Indonesia](/posts/sulawesi-indonesia/) & [Moluccas - Indonesia](/posts/moluccas-indonesia/) (Indonesia)**

**Why:** Tower karst landscapes and blue holes, particularly in Maros-Pangkep region. Excellent water clarity and remoteness.  
**Status:** Many dry caves explored by Indonesian and foreign teams; underwater exploration extremely limited.  
**Opportunity:** Dive flooded sinkholes, blue holes, and resurgence systems.

---

### **6. [Ethiopia](/posts/ethiopia/) (Danakil Depression and Sof Omar Caves)**

**Why:** Sof Omar is one of Africa’s largest cave systems, formed by a subterranean river. Potential for seasonal flooding and phreatic features.  
**Status:** Explored dry by cavers, but underwater mapping is uncharted.  
**Opportunity:** Seasonal or spring-fed systems may be accessible to divers.  
**Note:** Logistics and permissions would require extensive planning.

---
### **7. [Oman](/posts/oman/) (Hajar Mountains)**

**Why:** Extensive karst with large sinkholes like the Bimmah Sinkhole and underground rivers in Wadi systems.  
**Status:** Mostly dry caving, but hydrogeology supports the possibility of flooded caves.  
**Opportunity:** Dive little-known phreatic conduits and assess unmapped resurgences.

---
### **8. [Türkiye-Turkey](/posts/turkiye-turkey/) (Taurus Mountains & Central Anatolia)**

**Why:** Deep karst systems and many resurgences with limestone aquifers. Systems like Egma Sinkhole hint at deep phreatic development.  
**Status:** Limited cave diving has occurred. Some systems are deep and thermally stable.  
**Opportunity:** Technical diving in alpine karst systems.

---
### **9. [Vanuatu](/posts/vanuatu/) & [New Caledonia](/posts/new-caledonia/)**

**Why:** Karstic islands with flooded caves, sinkholes, and sea-level phreatic systems.  
**Status:** Some exploration, especially in Vanuatu (like Fanafo Blue Hole), but many systems remain unvisited.  
**Opportunity:** Combine oceanic island expedition logistics with inland freshwater karst systems.

---
### **10. [Albania](/posts/albania/) & [Kosovo](/posts/kosovo/)**

**Why:** Underexplored Dinaric karst, similar to Croatia but much less developed for tourism or exploration.  
**Status:** Some known caves; almost no documentation of cave diving.  
**Opportunity:** First cave dives, potentially into active resurgence systems.

---
### **11. [Tajikistan](/posts/tajikistan/) – Pamir-Alay Karst System**

- **Why:** High-altitude karst with known large caves like Festivalnaya and resurgence-fed systems.
- **Status:** Explored by Russian and European dry cavers, but no known cave diving.
- **Potential:** Alpine resurgence diving and high-altitude sump exploration in untouched systems.
- **Best Season:** July to September.

---
### **12. [Sri Lanka](/posts/sri-lanka/) – Central Highlands Karst**
- **Why:** Significant limestone in the Central and Northern provinces, including areas near Dambulla.
- **Status:** Largely undocumented in terms of cave exploration, but karst water systems are present.
- **Potential:** First cave diving in small flooded sinkholes or phreatic systems during wet season.
- **Best Season:** January to March (dry season for access).

---

### **13. [Laos](/posts/laos/) – Khammouane and Bolikhamxay Karst**

- **Why:** Home to the vast Xe Bang Fai River Cave and dozens of other major karst systems.
- **Status:** Limited cave diving despite the presence of massive submerged river systems.
- **Potential:** Extension of dry expeditions; explore downstream sumps and hidden blue holes.
- **Best Season:** January to April.

---

### **14. [Mongolia](/posts/mongolia/) – Gobi Desert and Khangai Mountains Karst**

- **Why:** Isolated karst basins in the south and west, minimal cave or diving exploration.
- **Status:** Very limited speleological work; potential aquifer-fed systems.
- **Potential:** Reconnaissance of high-latitude karst features and hydrological sinks.
- **Best Season:** June to August.

---

### **15. [Cameroon](/posts/cameroon/) – Mandara Mountains Karst**

- **Why:** Unique tropical karst topography near the Nigerian border with surface limestone exposures.
- **Status:** Negligible caving or diving work; area is geologically promising.
- **Potential:** Spring and cave pool diving in little-documented karst regions.
- **Best Season:** December to February.

---

### **16. [Solomon Islands](/posts/solomon-islands/) – Guadalcanal & Choiseul Karst**
- **Why:** Volcanic-limestone islands with known karst features and resurgence-fed streams.
- **Status:** Some dry cave records, but very little underwater exploration.
- **Potential:** Remote karst spring diving with oceanic isolation appeal.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---
### **17. [Bulgaria](/posts/bulgaria/) – Rhodope Mountains**
- **Why:** Part of the same karst belt as the Balkans; caves like Duhlata are among the longest in Eastern Europe.
- **Status:** Extensive dry cave exploration but fewer cave diving ventures than Western Europe.
- **Potential:** High-value survey targets with minimal diving records.
- **Best Season:** June to September.

---
### **18. [Georgia](/posts/georgia/) (Caucasus) – Sataplia and [Tskaltubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsqaltubo) Karst**

- **Why:** Active karst regions with massive springs and unexplored conduits.
- **Status:** Growing caving activity, but few documented underwater dives.
- **Potential:** Deep karst resurgences near Black Sea catchment zones.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---
### **19. [Kazakhstan](/posts/kazakhstan/) – Karatau and Tian Shan Karst**

- **Why:** Vast, underexplored limestone regions with documented spring systems and karst topography.
- **Status:** Negligible caving infrastructure or documentation; excellent for first exploration.
- **Potential:** Large-scale karst mapping with possibility of diving major resurgences.
- **Best Season:** May to August.

---

### **20. [Seychelles](/posts/seychelles/) – Mahé & Praslin Karst Pockets**
- **Why:** Rare karst limestone pockets on granitic islands, overlooked due to small size.
- **Status:** No formal cave exploration documented; pristine diving environment.
- **Potential:** Tropical sinkhole or shallow cave diving in marine-influenced karst.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---
### **21. [Réunion Island](/posts/reunion-island/) (Indian Ocean – French Overseas Territory)**

- **Why:** Volcanic island with isolated limestone bands and possible lava tubes intersecting freshwater lenses.
- **Status:** Known dry caves and lava tubes, but freshwater cave diving is almost nonexistent.
- **Potential:** Search for anchialine pools or submerged karst pockets inland or near coastal freshwater springs.
- **Best Season:** May to October (dry season).

---
### **22. [Lebanon](/posts/lebanon/) – Anti-Lebanon Mountains and Jeita Grotto System**

- **Why:** Home to one of the longest caves in the Middle East (Jeita), with a major underground river system.
- **Status:** Limited diving despite extensive dry exploration and known water-filled passages.
- **Potential:** Dive terminal sumps or investigate secondary karst systems in the north.
- **Best Season:** April to June.

---
### **23. [Rwanda](/posts/rwanda/) – Karst around Bugarama and Nyungwe Forest**
- **Why:** Karst features in the southwest near the Congo border, with known limestone outcrops.
- **Status:** Almost no documented speleology or diving.
- **Potential:** Blue holes and flooded sinkholes possibly linked to geothermal springs.
- **Best Season:** June to September.

---

### **24. [Bosnia & Herzegovina](/posts/bosnia-herzegovina/) – Dinaric Karst Interior**

- **Why:** One of Europe’s most extensive karst terrains, especially the Trebišnjica river basin.
- **Status:** Significant dry caving; some resurgence diving near the Croatian border, but interior karst is less explored.
- **Potential:** Mid-depth resurgences, sump diving, and active hydrogeological mapping.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---
### **25. [Montenegro](/posts/montenegro/) – Durmitor Karst Plateau**
- **Why:** Deep karst pockets, alpine caves, and seasonal lakes; hydrologically active.
- **Status:** Documented caves like Ice Cave and others, but little underwater work.
- **Potential:** High-altitude flooded sumps, glacial karst interaction zones.
- **Best Season:** July to September.

---

### **26. [Mauritius](/posts/mauritius/) – Chamarel Karst and Underground Streams**
- **Why:** Small pockets of limestone and volcanic-limestone interface in the south of the island.
- **Status:** Some known dry caves, often shallow and inaccessible, but potential freshwater inputs remain undocumented.
- **Potential:** Small-scale tropical spring or lava-karst interfaces with pristine conditions.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---
### **27. [Democratic Republic of Congo](/posts/democratic-republic-of-congo/) – Katanga Plateau**
- **Why:** Massive, remote karst regions in southern DRC near the Zambian border.
- **Status:** Very limited cave exploration; almost no diving records.
- **Potential:** First expeditions into flooded karst systems—logistically challenging but potentially world-class.
- **Best Season:** May to August.

---

### **28. [Bhutan](/posts/bhutan/) – Himalayan Karst near Phobjikha Valley**
- **Why:** Little-known Himalayan karst near limestone outcrops, springs, and tectonic boundaries.
- **Status:** Some hydrogeological interest but no known caving or diving expeditions.
- **Potential:** Alpine karst springs, potentially flooded systems at mid-altitude.
- **Best Season:** April to June.

---

### **29. [Timor-Leste](/posts/timor-leste/) (Timor-Leste)**
- **Why:** Tectonically uplifted limestone karst; similar geology to adjacent parts of Indonesia.
- **Status:** Limited cave documentation; no cave diving.
- **Potential:** First exploration of resurgence systems and flooded karst valleys.
- **Best Season:** May to October.

---

### **30. [Sardinia - Italy](/posts/sardinia-italy/)**

- **Why:** While Sardinia is in Europe, it's far less explored than mainland France or Spain. Features major underwater karst systems like the Bue Marino caves and Codula Ilune.
- **Status:** Some known diving, but dozens of systems remain undocumented, especially inland.
- **Potential:** Technical cave diving in semi-marine and inland karst, remote sump access.
- **Best Season:** May to October.
