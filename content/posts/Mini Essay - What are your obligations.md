As I put together the final things ready for the next few weeks in Antarctica and then Perth, I'm asking myself what my outstanding obligations are. This can be my emotional obligations (like people and things I feel I owe something to), physical obligations (like things that I have and feel responsible for), and my own spiritual obligations (the things that I feel I need to work on or do to feel fulfilled). 

Looking at my physical obligations is probably the easiest place to start, as they're tangible and obvious. I have a wide range of different things that I currently own which I feel require attention, as well as things which I likely haven't given enough thought to. My diving gear is the most obvious "obligation" which I see, and it concerns me that I decent chunk of that is hidden away in the storage unit. Things like the compressor and it's spare parts certainly bugs me, and it's certainly a breach of that idea that if you can't carry it then you can't keep it. I'll be keen to sell that compressor when I get back, as well as anything else which I have that doesn't have an immediate purpose.

I'm generally pretty good about getting rid of excess physical things, but the few things which I do hang onto and don't use generally have an emotional obligation attached to them too. My bookcase is a classic example of that - full of unread books that I'd "love" to read and yet haven't made a priority to actually open. Part of that has been my effort to read the 101 Things books first, and yet that hasn't been a priority either. I'd very much like to read those books while I'm away, and there's every reason to believe that's possible - make it a priority when you're not writing.

The other physical/emotional obligation I have is my ukulele, which has sat unloved by your office door for years. I know that right now I have a backlog on artistic expression, and I'm tackling that through writing (specifically by writing these [Mini Essays]({{< relref "posts/Mini Essays (conflict 2026-06-29-23-05-33).md" >}})) but my ukulele is important, and just like writing it's an artistic expression which is vital to my mental, emotional and spiritual wellbeing. Just like writing, it needs to be made more of a priority in the year ahead - it needs to be something that is important to you, and if you're embarrassed by how others might react then simply close the office door.

Spiritual obligations are really the ones I've most ignored over the last few years, and the ones that I most need to reconnect with to express and free myself from the mindset I'm trying to escape from. I have an obligation to pay attention to synchronicity when it occurs, an obligation to celebrate joy and to embrace the ebb and flow of the universe. I haven't paid attention to the subtle notes of life for awhile, and I have a real concern that if I continue to ignore it then I'll lose the ability to hear those messages that have made some many parts of my life feel special in the past. 

The truth is I don't have many "obligations", but the ones I have are very important, and it's vital I meet them more regularly than I have over the last few years. I have an obligation to the physical things I'm responsible for, to my mental and emotional wellbeing, and an obligation to my spiritual practice that has been severely undernourished. Make a solid effort to convert the "obligations" you have into remarkable things - read books and publish notes on them, develop ideas and publish books on them, look after your dive gear and discover new caves with it, and pay attention to the universe around you so you can more fully appreciate all it's intricacies.
