# About

- A ~**300 m main tunnel** links to **two phreatic networks**: one half-filled and one fully flooded. The **NE passage (“60 m line”)** departs the north side of a large rock; it’s classed **Advanced Cave**. Locally named **Gary’s Back Passage** and a “shortcut” from the entrance lake into the main tunnel are also treated as Advanced Cave for training. ([cavedivers.com.au](https://www.cavedivers.com.au/index.cfm?action=details&leca=1831&module=BUSINESSDIRECTORY&pageMode=indiv&page_id=1404675&utm_source=chatgpt.com "Murra El Elevyn (6N47) - cavedivers.com.au"))

- Walls show rich **scalloping**/phreatic morphologies; photographers consistently report **very clear water** with red-to-pink limestone tones. ([lizrogersphotography.com](https://lizrogersphotography.com/2012/08/video-in-murra-el-elevyn/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Video in Murra-El-Elevyn - Liz Rogers Photography"))

- The Nullarbor karst here hosts **microbial mantles (“snotites”)** in several cave lakes including Murra-el-Elevyn—avoid physical contact or wake/silt impacts. ([caveconservationaustralia.org](https://caveconservationaustralia.org/nullarbor-karst?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Nullarbor Karst and Cave Systems complex ecology | Caves and Karst ..."))

- The cave is scientifically notable for **bat-guano minerals** (type locality for biphosphammite) and palaeo finds reported in literature round-ups (e.g., thylacine remains citations). Treat all deposits as protected. ([Mindat](https://www.mindat.org/loc-230.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Murra-El-Elevyn Cave, Cocklebiddy Roadhouse, Dundas Shire, Western ..."))

- There are **instrumented climate datasets** (2021-2023) for Murra-el-Elevyn (bihourly T/RH), useful if you want to time imaging for stable conditions/low humidity swings. ([doi.pangaea.de](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.964570?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Lipar, M; Ferk, M (2024): Bihourly temperature and humidity ..."))
## Potential Leads

1) *North-West (“60 m line”) continuation* 
This is the cave’s most explicitly “Advanced” line and the obvious vector for continuation. Look for undercut ceilings, rising phreatic tubes, or low-ceiling half-pipes paralleling the main trend. Track scallop asymmetry to confirm paleo-flow and prioritize alcoves on bends.

2) *Interfaces around the “large rock” in the main chamber*  
Breakdown piles often mask bypass tubes. Check behind and above the boulder for blind domes or tube lips; inspect for faint percolation staining or clean, eddy-scoured floor patches indicating through-draft water movement.

3) *“Gary’s Back Passage” and the entrance-to-main-tunnel shortcut*
Both are named as Advanced segments—good flags that they’re tighter/less travelled. These are prime spots to find side tubes that were skipped when the main line was established. Slow, close wall-scans often reveal inset phreatic notches. 

4) *Joint-controlled leads aligned with regional structure* 
Nullarbor caves commonly exploit joint sets; historical mapping notes exist for **Murrawijinie** (SA) showing joint frequency plots—useful analogs for what to look for in plan view at Murra-el-Elevyn: straight runs, right-angle turns, bead-string chambers. 

5) *Ceiling domes & cupolas in the half-filled network*  
In mixed air/water sections, inspect cupolas for high-level feeders. Dry-tooling to a visible rim may reveal a tubes’ continuation once submerged a few metres farther on.
