# Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden
Avoiding "personal blog" and more like free-form work-in-progress wikis
A collection of evolving ideas not organised by publication
Half-finished thoughts that grow and evolve with time
"Less perfect" - like the early days of the web
- WikiGardeners https://wiki.c2.com/?WikiGardener and WikiGnomes - https://wiki.c2.com/?WikiGnome
[Mike Caulfield](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ckv_CjyKyZY&feature=emb_logo) coining the term ["Digital Gardening"](https://hapgood.us/2015/10/17/the-garden-and-the-stream-a-technopastoral/)
"Campfires" as conversational spaces to exchange still-forming ideas
Joel Hooks ["My blog is a digital garden"](https://hapgood.us/2015/10/17/the-garden-and-the-stream-a-technopastoral/) 
Amy Hoy's ["How the Blog Broke the Web](https://stackingthebricks.com/how-blogs-broke-the-web/)

`Is GitHub you digital garden? A space to share ideas, work, files, ect in a semi-structured manner?`

Tiago Forte - Building a second brain - https://www.buildingasecondbrain.com/
Learn in Public (Digital Gardens without Code) - https://www.swyx.io/learn-in-public/

Web movement to break free of Squarespace and Wordpress

Six Patterns of Digital Gardens
1. *Topography over Timelines* - Context through links, publication date is irrelevant
	- [thematic piles](https://busterbenson.com/piles/) , [nested folders](https://tomcritchlow.com/wiki/) , tags and filtering functionality, [advanced search bars](https://www.christopherbiscardi.com/garden)  or [central indexes](https://www.gwern.net/index)
	- "The Piles" with links to tags is cool 
2. *Continuous Growth* - constantly growing, evolving, and changing
	- Editing is one of the strengths of the web - there is no "final version"
	- Test ideas, get feedback, evolve.
	- **Gardens make their imperfection known to readers**
3. *Learning in Public* - Don't hide the rough edge
	- Blogging evolved in [premium mediocre](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2017/08/17/the-premium-mediocre-life-of-maya-millennial/) culture (personal brands & SEO-optimised content)
	- Digital Gardening is both public and intimate, weird and welcoming
	- Gardening sits between chaos stream (DMs, Group Chats, Tweet threads) and cultivated artefacts (published articles/books)
	- Include dates I _planted_ and _last tended_ a post
	- [Gwern.net includes](https://www.gwern.net/About#confidence-tags): topic tags, start & end date, stage tag (draft/progress/finished), certainty (impossible/unlikely/certain), 1-10 importance tag
	- Digital Gardening ["Terms of Service"](https://www.swyx.io/digital-garden-tos)
4. *Playful, Personal, Experimental*
	- Question the established norms of a "personal website" - make space for weirded, wilder experiments
	- Gardens avoid cookie-cutter presentation - complete with quirks, contradictions, complexity
5. Intercropping & Content Diversity
	- Blend AV, images, diagrams, animations, tweets, code snippets
6. Independent Ownership
	- Gardening is about claiming a small patch of the web for yourself
	- Do not live on Facebook, LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram, Medium - most rail against personal knowledge development
		- If any of them go down, your writing goes with it
	- Avoid walled gardens
	- Think about how you'll grow your own space over the next few decades, not just the next few months.
