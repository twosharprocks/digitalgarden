For awhile now I've been asking myself what I *really* need to hang onto physically, and what I can let go. It's been part of a larger series of questions around who I am, how I'm living my life, and what I can let go of to be truer to myself... but the physical baggage I have is always the easiest to identify and most obvious to determine if I can let it go.

When I think of the physical baggage I have, it breakdown into a couple of different areas. My van Valerie is the easiest to identify, and also the hardest to reduce - she's a single asset that I love and had dreamed of having for more than a decade before I got her. Within Valerie though, there are certainly things to let go of, and the limited size tends to make that a relatively straight-forward process. While there are things in the Kata box, my glovebox, and under the seat that I can probably let go of; there isn't much else to worry about - she's small and clean enough to identify any gear traps very quickly. The bigger weight with Valerie is ensuring that she's functioning at a level I'm comfortable with. I'm keen to solve the issue with the rear right tire deflating, and I'm also keen to repair the dent to the rear right too. There are a few things with the electrical system I'd like to clean up and fix, and I'd especially like to have the exhaust fan operating properly. But really there's not much else I want to do - she's setup the way I need her to be setup, she functioned fantastically on the Nullarbor, and for the next 4 weeks I'm going to be splitting my time between living in her and being at home. So my VW Transporter is in good shape, and not something I need to see as "baggage".

Closer to home though, there are real questions about the things that you have in the workshop. Some of that is gear for my van, and I should really pull that out and put it to one side to reveal what is *not* useful or important to keep. Probably the biggest sense of "excess" that you get from the workshop is the stuff left over from doing the modifications to the van, as well as the collection of dive gear that is no longer useful or required. The 200W solar panel that didn't fit on the van roof is the easiest and most obvious leftover, but there is plenty of other stuff in there that can be sorted through too, and it really just requires a keen and clinical eye to go through what's there and let anything extra go. 

Probably the most obvious diving related thing you can think of is the equipment that is specific to the X4 camera. It's clearly gone now, lost to the depths of [Tommy Graham's Cave - 6N56]({{< relref "posts/Tommy Graham's Cave - 6N56.md" >}}), and you won't replace it with another one - you'll either stick with the X5 you have, or look at an X6 down the road. So there is no need to hang onto the X4-specific parts you have - bin them. 

The biggest barrier to getting rid of a lot of the stuff you still have is it's *potential*. You have a lot of SRT rope, and it'd be great to be dropping into places and exploring them using it. The rope is perfect, and you have the SRT gear to use it too, but you haven't and it seems wasted. Keep track of things like that, because they become a millstone around your neck if you don't use or release them. Right now the ropes are fine sitting in the tub doing nothing, but they are yet another *thing* that you have that could be used and isn't. Your Rode microphones are the same, as is the Yeti mic, but those all have a clear and obvious future use - your Podcast plans. But you need to ensure that the things you have all have clear and upcoming plans for their use. If it's not being used, doesn't have an obvious future use, and can't be used by someone else in the near future, then it probably needs to be moved on. 

Part of me misses the days where everything I owned could be fit in a backpack, but I also remember how ludicrous that backpack looked when I really did load everything up. Keep working on minimising your load and your physical footprint while staying capable and ready for action. Let go of the things that don't help you achieve your goals, and share the things you have with others so that they can put them to use towards their goals instead.
