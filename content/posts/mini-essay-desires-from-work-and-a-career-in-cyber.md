To determine what you want from work and your career in cybersecurity more generally, you really need to go back to why you got into cyber in the first place. This whole journey started because you were looking for work that was IT-related, because you haven't never worked in IT have an aptitude for it. Your initial investigations were leading you down the Gaming pathway - that's why you spoke to Martin about his work as much as you did, and why you bought a couple of cheap courses in modelling/gaming design in Unity. The interest in working with computers came first, and then the specialisation.

Ultimately you decided to pursue cybersecurity because it's IT-related and you quickly realised you have the right kind of mindset to work in cyber rather than IT. Your background with the military and working intelligence-related fields has helped accelerate your path considerably, and helped guide you into this career too. Your experience in writing has drawn you towards GRC and policy work too, as well as it being a point of difference from others who want to pursue more technical paths.

So what do you want from cyber and your current job? You got involved in cyber to earn good money (resources), doing something interesting that you're good at (fulfilment), that provides you the flexibility to do the travel and organise the expeditions that are important to you. In your work's case they offer good money doing work that is adjacent to your interests (threat intelligence) and which draws on your experience (risk management). They also offer the opportunity to develop both certifications ([CISSP](/posts/cissp/)) and vital experience that can be applied in a range of different roles.

So what do you want from cyber? A **flexible and fully remote role doing threat intelligence, risk reporting, and/or GRC** - something an IRAP Assessor would have, or someone doing Threat Intelligence remotely.

And what do you want from your current role? My **[CISSP](/posts/cissp/) certification**, gaining more **experience in Threat Intelligence and cyber**, and as much **flexibility** as I can have to plan my future outside of the organisation - plan my next move while still being paid a lot where I am.
