IT’S SHAPED LIKE THOR’S HAMMER!

For those of you following on [Twitter](https://web.archive.org/web/20140322170505/http://twitter.com/Mighty_Ginge "Trust me, you'll regret this"), you’re probably well aware I’ve been in Scotland for the last week or so visiting Edinburgh for the Fringe Festival. And I can honestly say I love Edinburgh and what I’ve seen of Glasgow – there’s always something going on (though that’s got a lot to do with the festival), the city is beautiful, and the view from Arthur’s seat is one of the most incredible things I’ve ever seen. And to top it all off the people are so friendly, incredibly generous and open-hearted.

I just wish I understood a word they said.

I want to make it clear that have a lot of time for the Scots – they seem like a really genuine and happy people. But I don’t give a shit who you are, there is no way you can convince me they’re speaking English. In the major towns like Edinburgh and Glasgow it’s really not that bad, but I’ve just returned from a few days out into the country-side: I had to buy another book to read, because I had NO IDEA what they were saying to each other. And when you’re spending 4 days in a tiny [south-western Scottish town](https://web.archive.org/web/20140322170505/http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girvan) with guys all from the local area, this presents a problem.

![que-pasa](https://web.archive.org/web/20140322170505im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/08/que-pasa-192x300.png "que-pasa")It seems the further into the country-side you go, the less you seem to need the 21 letters of the alphabet we like to refer to as consonants. In all my travels I’ve never come across a place where the primary-school rhyme for learning vowels “A, E, I, O, U” could actually be confused for a sentence. Saturday night apparently was to involve a trip “int eh Eyyeh fur eh nai weh eh gulls” – roughly translated as “into Ayr for a night with the girls”. So we drove into Ayr with “Gehrreh” aka “Garry”, with his horrendous in music – although I will admit it was one of the few times I could ever appreciate Linkin Park, since it was a relief to actually comprehend what someone was saying. After getting knocked back from half a dozen bars in Ayr (seems the concept that someone might only have a foreign drivers license was too much for the rocket-scientists working the doors to the bars), we finally got into “Cactus Jacks” and met up with the “gulls”.

Bouncing – for when even the cops think you’re a meathead

Now being the token foreigner, it became obvious that all four of the girls only wanted to talk to yours truly (much to the annoyance of the guys who’d brought me out). And while the communication problem was tolerable in a bar with music at a reasonable level, when we drifted into a club later things rapidly degenerated into hand signals and sms-ing from 3 feet away. What could easily have turned into a free-range Scottish sex fiesta (sheep optional) instead had everyone going home alone, convinced the other had said “Gets mack tah I base untah I hed”

![The ones that fight back get turned into hot-water bottle covers](https://web.archive.org/web/20140322170505im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2009/08/bedwarmer.jpg "bedwarmer")

The ones that fight back get turned into hot-water bottle covers

What I don’t understand is how the Scot’s accent got so bad so quickly. I mean, I’ve seen Braveheart – I had NO trouble understanding what William Wallace was saying. So it’s clear that some sort massive educational collapse has swept through Scotland in the last 15 years, producing a generation of relatively normal Scottish kids with the literacy skills of a Stegosaurus. And in keeping with this, there really is only one way around this problem: to have Mel Gibson take over the teaching of English to all Scottish school children – immediately.
