# Domain 1A - Information Systems Auditing Process Planning

**The IS Auditor's Role**
- Strategic partner to the enterprise
- Core Mission: ***Evaluate and improve** risk management, control and governance processes
- Auditor's Mindset
	- Objectivity & Independence
	- Focus on Business Risk & Value
	- Systematic & Disciplined Approach

# 1 - IS Audit Standards, Guidelines, and Codes of Ethics
## Planning is Paramount
- Ensures audit efficiency & effectiveness
- Directs resources to areas of greatest risk
- Maximises the value and impact of the audit
## Cost of Poor Planning
- Wasted resources & time
- Overlooked critical risks
- Audit that fails to provide meaningful assurance
## Is Audit Standards, Guidelines and Code of Ethics
- Key Framework: ***ISACA's IT Assurance Framework (ITAF)***
- Purpose: Ensure consistency, quality and professionalism in IS auiting

*Exam Tip: Adherence to ISACA Standards is mandatory for CISA holders

## Audit Charter - Authority to Audit
- Purpose: Outlines overall authority, scope, and responsibilities of the audit function
- Content: Describes the mission, independence, and access rights of the audit team
- Approval: Must be approved by the highest level of governance (eg. Audit Committee, Board of Directors, or senior management)

*Exam Tip: Audit Charter is the foundational document that grants the audit function its power & legitimacy*

## IT Assurance Framework (ITAF) Hierarchy
- ***Standards***: Mandatory requirements - minimum level of acceptable performance
- ***Guidelines***: Non-mandatory (optional) assistance. How to implement the standards
- ***Tools & Techniques***: Practical "How-To" examples (checklists, audit programs, ect)

*Exam Tip: You MUST follow Standards. Consider Guidelines but deviate with professional judgement and justification*

## ITAF Standards Catagories
- ***General Standards***: The "Who" - Attributes of the auditor and audit function
	- Ethics, independence, competence
- ***Performance Standards***: The "How" - Define the nature of the audit work itself
	- Planning, Risk assessment, evidence collection
- ***Reporting Stanards***: The "What" - Define the nature and content of the audit report
	- Communication of results, findings, recommendations

## ISACA Code of Professional Ethics
- Non-negotiable requirement for all CISA holders
- Functions as a primary administrative control for the profession
- Mitigates the risk of audit failure and protects the profession's reputation
- Key Principles
	- Support standards
	- Objectivity & Due Care
	- Serve Stakeholders Lawfully
	- Maintain Confidentiality
	- Maintain Competency
	- Informal Appropriate Parties
	- Support Professional Education
- Failure to comply can result in an investigation and disciplinary measures
- Sanctions include remrimand, suspension, revocation of certification

*Exam Tip: Code of Ethics has real teeth, not just a guideline*

## Management of the IS Audit Function
- *Leadership*: The IS audit function must be managed to fulfill its objectives while preserving independence and competence
- *Resource Management*
	- Staffing - Auditors must maintain competency through continuous training on new technologies and audit techniques
	- Tools: Management must provide necessary resources like **Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (CAATs)** for specialised audits
- *Using Experts*: Auditors may engage external experts for highly specialised areas
	- *Responsibility*: Auditor must assess the experts competence and independence and supervise their work
	- ***Ultimate liability for the audit opinion is NOT delegated - auditor is accountable***

# 2 - Types of Audits, Assessments, and Reviews

## Assurance
- The level of ***confidence and trust*** that an organisation's systems, processes, and controls are effective in protecting information assets, managing risks, and ensuring compliance with relevant standards and policies. 
- Who, how and what of an audit affects confidence
## Selecting the Right Lens
- Audits: *Highest assurance* with formal opinion against established criteria
- Assessments: *Less formal, internal focus* on identifying risks and gaps
- Reviews: *Limited assurance* with less extensive testing than a full audit

*Exam Tip: The choice depends on the level of assurance required by stakeholders*

## Taxonomy of Audit Engagements
- *Compliance Audit*: Adherence to laws, regulations, policies (GDPR, PCI DSS) 
- *Financial Audit*: Accuracy of financial reporting and the supporting IT controls
- *Operational Audit*: Efficiency and effectiveness of operations
- *Integrated Audit*: Combines Financial and IS/Operational audit objectives (Sarbane-Oxley "SOX" requirement)
- *IS Audit*: Focus on CIA triad of information assets
- *Third-Party Service Audit*: Audits outsourced processes, resulting in a service auditors report (SOC report) that the organisation's auditor can rely on
- *Computer Forensic Audit*: Specialised investigation of electronic devices to gather and preserve evidence for legal proceedings
- *Functional Audit*: Independent evaluation of a software product to verify that it's functionality and performance meet specified requirements
- *Readiness Assessment*: Review of an organisation's current state of compliance against a standard, focusing on control design to identify gaps before a formal audit

## Control Self-Assessment (CSA) 
- A management technique where business units assess their own control environments
- *Purpose*
	- Increases control awareness and ownership within the business
	- Identifies risks and control gaps early
	- Serves as a valuable input for the internal audit function
- Auditor's Role: Acts as a facilitator for CSA workshops and processes, not as the one performing the assessment

# 3 - Risk Based Audit Planning
- *Core Principle*: Concentrate audit resources on areas that pose the greatest risk to the organisation's objectives
- Benefits
	- Efficiency: Avoids wasting time on low-risk areas
	- Effectiveness: Provides the most relevant assurance to leadership

*Exam Tip: The first step in any risk-based audit is to understand the business*

## Short Term VS Long-term Audit Planning
- *Short-term (Annual) Planning*
	- Considers audit issues to be covered during the current year
	- Forms the basis for the annual audit plan
- *Long-term Planning*
	- Considers the strategic changes in the organisation's IT environment and direction
	- Aligns the audit function with the long-term goals of the business
- Process: This analysis should occur at least annually and be updated for significant changes (eg. Acquisitions, new regulations)
	- The plan must be approved by the audit committee

## Effects of Laws and Regulations on Planning
- *Auditor's Responsibility*: Is auditor must identify all applicable laws, regulations, and contractual requirements relevant to the audit
- *Impact on Scope*: External requirements that directly impact the audit scope and objectives
- Audit Steps
	- **Identify** and document applicable laws and regulations (eg. Data privacy, commerce, industry-specific rules)
	- **Assess** if management has incorporated these requirements into policies and procedures
	- **Review** internal documents and controls for adherence

*Exam Tip: You won't be asked about specific laws, but you will be tested on how you would audit for compliance with them*

## Risk-Based Audit Planning Lifecycle
- ***Audit Planning Lifecycle***
	- *Understand the Business*: Define the "audit universe" (all potential audit able entities)
	- *Perform a Risk Assessment*: Identify threats and vulnerabilities, prioritise high-risk areas
	- *Identify Existing Controls*: What has management done to mitigate the high risks? 
	- *Determine Residual Risk*: How much risk is left after controls are considered? 
	- *Develop the audit plan*: Define scope, objectives, and resources based on residual risk
		- Approved by the audit committee

## Audit Risk and Materiality
- *Audit Risk*: Risk of issuing an incorrect audit opinion (eg. Saying controls are effective when they are not)
- *Materiality*: Importance or significance of an item in the context of the enterprise as a whole. An error is material if it could influence the decisions of stakeholders
- *Formula*: 
	`Audit Risk (AR) = Inherent Risk (IR) x Control Risk (CR) x Detection Risk (DR)`
	- *Inherent Risk*: Risk before control implementation
	- *Control Risk*: Risk a material error that would not be prevented/detected in a time lying basis by the system of internal controls
	- *Detection Risk*: Risk that material errors or misstatements will not be detected by an IS auditor
	- *Residual Risk*: Risk that remains after controls have been implemented
- ***Key Relationship***: Inverse relationship between materiality and audit risk
	- If an area is highly material (very important) then the acceptable level of audit risk is low, meaning the auditor must perform more rigorous testing

# 4 - Types of Controls and Considerations
- What is a Control? 
	- A policy, procedure, practice, or organisational structure that provides reasonable assurance that business objectives will be achieved
	- Prevents, detects, or corrects undesirable events
- *Control Objctive*: The specific goal a control is designed to achieve

## COBIT and Others
- ISACA's Primary Framework is "COBIT"
	- *COBIT*: Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies
- Other Prescriptive Frameworks
	- *CIS*: Center for Internet Security Controls (Prioritised set of best practices for cyber security)
	- *OWASP*: Open framework for software security
	- *PCI DSS*: Requirements for protecting payment card data
	- *CSA Cloud Controls Matrix (CCM)*: Framework for cloud computing security

## General Controls and Application Controls
- *General Controls (ITGCs)*: Applies to the entire IT environment (data centers, networks, operating systems, ect) and provides foundation for all overall controlled structure
	- Change management, logical access policies, disaster recovery
- *Application Controls*: Specific to particular software application or business process. Ensures data is complete, accurate, and valid as it is processed

## Classification by Function
- *Preventative*: Stops it occurring (eg. firewall, encryption)
- *Detective*: Identifies an incident after occurance (eg. System logs, IDS alerts)
- *Corrective*: Remediates the impact of an incident (eg. Incident response, backups)
- *Deterrent*: Discourages an individual from causing an incident (eg. Warning sings, barriers)
- *Directive*: Mandates (directs, steers) desired behaviour (eg. Policies, laws)
- *Compensating*: Alternate control when a primary control is not feasible

*Exam Tip: Preventative controls are the most effective and desirable type*

## Classification by Implmentation
- *Administrative (Managerial)*: "Soft controls" focused on people and processes (eg. Policies, procedures, security awareness training)
- *Technical (Logical)*: Controls implemented in hardware or software (ACLs, encryption, antivirus)
- *Physical*: Controls that protect the physical environment (eg. Fences, locks, security guards, fire suppression)

## Management Control Monitoring
- ***What is it?*** Management's own process for monitoring the effectiveness of controls, separate from a formal audit
- *Purpose*
	- Identifies control deviations in a timely manner
	- Allows management to take proactive corrective action
	- Drives continuous improvement of the security program
- *Auditor's Role*: The IS auditor can review the results of management's monitoring efforts as part of their evaluation of the control environment

## CISA Mindset
- ***Think the "ISACA Way"***: **ALWAYS prioritise business risk (over purely technical issues)**
- *Auditor's Role*: Provide **assurance**, not implement controls
- ***First Steps is ALWAYS understand the business context***

*Exam Tip: Practice, practice, practice. Use scenario-based questions to master the application of these concepts*
