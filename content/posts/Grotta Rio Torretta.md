(Translated from Italian)
RIO TURRET 2010

09-03-10 I am with the Pied Piper and G i Bi at the house where we will be staying, that is, in a hamlet of Pedemonte. Fanni, the local muse, organized everything: a different house than last year but, like that one, uninhabited in the winter, even if in perfect working order, apart from the internal temperature which is certainly not the warmest. This is a detail of little importance because in the afternoon I will receive, procured by Simone, a new sleeping bag which I hope will do the job for him. We reach the cave, after having unloaded the car and reloaded the necessary equipment, at noon or almost. We carry all the Pied Piper and I, while G i B i
he dedicates himself to outdoor shots. Around 13.30 Simone arrives, while we are still arranging the regulators on the cylinders; while chatting, we mount my hybrid (Copis-Meg) then, given the time of 2.30 pm and hunger, we go to eat something. For today the work is finished and tomorrow morning we will dive.

10-03-10 Morning wake up after a freezing night with 5cm of fresh snow that makes the landscape white. The snow brings happiness but the problems will come from the Pied Piper who will take three times longer than necessary to get up here. Boaria and Giuseppe arrive punctually, at the only bar in the area where we wait in the warmth, to get everyone together. Finally full, we reach the cave at 11.00. When all the equipment, including Copis, is ready, shortly after 12.00, we start transporting the cylinders in the siphon. Since the level is very low, it will be more difficult than usual to carry everything up to the third siphon, but the visibility is good and the desire to continue is excellent. The first to leave are Boaria and I: the others will follow closely behind us. Once passed the first siphon, we stack the cylinders on a stone that seems made for it and we reach the second siphon with the diving equipment on our shoulders; I stay there and wait while the brave companions, once they have unloaded the bibo, make a couple of trips each to finish the transport. Once the equipment has been put back on, we leave for the third siphon. It is here that we realize how low the level is because, to get over the neck of debris, we have to go up it for a couple of meters in dry conditions. Four of us, the handrail is fast but, when we go down the other side, we realize we have to continue for a few more meters before finding water; the only positive thing is that with the level so low, the room between the second and third siphon is not under pressure and therefore there will be no Co2 stagnation problems. Again Boaria and I at the start, the Pied Piper and Franco will leave after about ten minutes so as not to block the passage through the bottlenecks. My Copis is normal and I'm convinced I can pass easily even without the changes I made last time. Boaria goes down first while from above I pass the reel, the three 12lt cylinders. and one of 10lt; once the bottleneck has been released, with a 12lt cylinder. I slip in with decision and in a moment I come out on the other side. I load myself with three 12lt cylinders. and I proceed while Boaria, with the reel, a 12lt cylinder. and a 10lt. follows me closely. In the siphon there are arrows indicating the exit direction of the super-ego of the person who placed them who, not being able to explore, gratified himself by filling the walls of the siphons with his name.
At -21m I find the skein of thread which, broken about fifteen meters further on and carried back by the current, has accumulated treacherous in a corner like a trap. I proceed by pulling it to extend it again, and when it stops, from behind Boaria who has noticed, solves the problem. I continue to the end of the garment and then tie it with the following garment. I tell Boaria to go ahead, because he is in an open circuit and these stops only contribute to consuming gas. We are at -28m, thirds on his 12lt bibo. they are finished so Boaria leaves me the cylinders and begins the return.
I remain alone as I like, free from tying myself or tying my partner: I reach the bottleneck which at these levels is at -32m, I leave the 10lt tank and go through the hole with my 12lt load. The path is easy, almost too easy and incredible if I think of last year's work, but the news we had was dramatic.
After the bottleneck, I think there might be something on the left, but the thread continues to the right and so I follow it. At -40m I deposit two cylinders and the exploration reel and after about twenty meters down to -44m, I leave the cylinder which will remain there as an emergency, while the other two I will carry further on until the next passage. I go back calmly, arranging the thread as best I can in some places where it is damaged and too loose. I returned to my companions after exactly one hour and all together we quickly returned to the second siphon. Having recovered from that too, we leave part of the equipment and we just have to overcome the last obstacle. Outside at 16.00, we find 10cm of beautiful fresh snow which requires us to be very careful in traveling along the hyper-slippery river bed. It's already late for me because I have to hold a screening at the Lthe ons of Bassano. Considering the abundant snowfall that is coming down and knowing that the forecasts announce good weather only starting from Thursday afternoon we decide to skip the dive the next day.
I run to get ready, going to take a hot shower in a freezing bathroom.

12-03-10 We leave from the cave entrance at 10.00, we are Boa, Franco, Moses, Giuseppe and I determined to carry out a perfect team work to achieve a good exploratory result.

Perfect collaboration to transport the equipment up to the third siphon, few cylinders but between lights and camera, the weight should not be underestimated. Moses and I enter the third siphon, the only ones with the rebreather. I swim slowly to allow Moses to shoot, with the shallow depth playing in my favor and even if my concentration for exploration suffers, I stop at various points to allow Moses to reach shooting areas in front of me. We reach the -32m bottleneck together, then we separate. I greet, as I enter the little hole with the 12lt relays and starting to walk the already paved tunnel, I recover my concentration for exploration. After the deep part, I start the decompressive stages, until I reach the limit of the last exploration. I attach the thread and, as I progress, what I see does not seem like the right continuation: after only 30m I reach a surface, and while the tunnel is filled with mud, I am more and more convinced that it cannot be the right route. Anyway I'm here and I decide to remove the rebreather to go and see the dry gallery. After another 20m of dry trail, I stop on impassable bottlenecks. I go back to the water covered in mud, I dress for the dive and I go back into the water which, in an instant, becomes completely cloudy so that I have to hold the line in my hand up to a depth of -3/4m then, as I descend into the room, I reflect that there must be a continuation and it must be here. At -10m the visibility has definitely improved so I start looking. Maybe we're here: a tunnel seems to open up on my right; I'm at -15m, I plug in my reel and start unwinding the line. I go up to -10m then I go down almost immediately to -20m, the tunnel is 5m wide by 3m high, there are no mud deposits, the rocks are white: this is not just the continuation but something more. I progress through amusing ups and downs until I stop for having reached the limit allowed by the emergency open circuit at -30m and after having pulled a further 240m of line. Not bad I tell myself, I kicked for over a kilometer and now I still have to go back through the gallery in reverse and, what's more, recovering all the tanks. I leave the bottles at the -32m bottleneck, thinking about the filming we will do tomorrow and return, making the few necessary decompression stops before resurfacing. I exchange my impressions with friends who have patiently waited for me: curiosity is obviously great and my revelations make us dream of incredible explorations even on this side of the Asiago plateau massif. We go back together and, between the third siphon and the exit, I think that if I had a piglet, I could try to continue on Sunday. Dreams, only dreams, the piglet is in Lecco and I don't have time to go get it.

13-03-10 The night was spent mulling over how to solve the problem of exploration crippled by the lack of the piggy. With everything prepared as we did, I don't like the idea of ​​precluding a good advance, so in the morning, while having breakfast, the Pied Piper suggests that a piglet could lend him G i B i . As soon as G i B i joins us, we immediately ask for their availability. Boa, Franco are also there on Sunday and the Thing can then be done.
For today, Moses dedicates himself to the video camera filming me while I'm going to reposition the cylinders in the siphon, one more task but which I carry out with great happiness; Boa, Franco, Nadia and the Pied Piper help in the air transport after which they will go to see something on their own. This time Moses waits for me at the -7m bottleneck to film even a little bit of the return and, passing it on my way back, I find visibility reduced to just over a metre. When we re-emerge, we find the others waiting for us, all happy to inform us that they have explored a 95m lateral gallery at a depth of about -3m, which continues. This spring is a real surprise.

14-03-10 At the "breakfast bar", Francesco, Laura and Nadia arrive with a tray of fresh pastries and Boa is no less with the brioches. Although this binge is welcome, the most awaited by me is the G i B i piglet which turns out to be a pig due to its size and weight.
With the arrival of Giuseppe and the Pied Piper, the team restarts the work. The last to appear is the Proteus who will join us beyond the siphons later to help. Between one siphon and another I try not to sweat but that doesn't seem possible, even if I only have to carry the rebreather on my back; the others, Boa, Giuseppe, Nadia and the Pied Piper will carry the maialone up to the third siphon. Boa accompanies me up to the narrow passage at -7m to help me pass the thruster because given the dimensions I have some doubts about the possibility of succeeding. The beast is perfectly balanced in the water but once challenged, its speed disappoints me a lot; used to my little ones this is slow, so much so that even Boa manages to follow me finning, without even too much effort. Anyway, with the big beast I won't bear the effort of finning. The bottleneck is easily overcome and I can be towed to the exploration point. I pass the deep part -55m, I go up observing the few minutes of decompression which force me to forced stops then, finally I can spin in the tunnel explored the day before yesterday: I recognize the point where I cut the line, I stop, attach the new reel and I begin to pull a new thread making me pull from the piggy.
The meters of new wire flow, the altitude varies with some ups and downs until it reaches -40m after about 150m of progression, the tunnel continues to descend and this is a limit for me again due to the open emergency circuit. At -50m, when I decided to stop, I laid out 235m of new line, and I'm happy even if I would have liked to go a little further, but that's it for this time.
The return time passes quickly between a few deco stops and the recovery of the relay cylinders. I pass the bottleneck at -32m with four 12lt and one 10lt cylinders in addition to the maialone; but the funniest part is overcoming the bottleneck at -7m, the most selective, with two other cylinders , one 7lt and one 10lt .The decompression is so short that I don't even realize I'm doing it and I resurface carrying everything with me. Now the heavy part of the work remains, that is the transport of the material in the dry stretches that will be done by the other members of the group. I re-emerge greeted by the lights and the laughter of the members of the group: I can't get out of the water if I don't speak first. Sounds like a full-blown interrogation. The first piece of information I give is that it is not necessary to go to the bottleneck to recover the cylinders because, feeling fit, I recovered them all by myself, the second, the most awaited, is a quick description of the exploration and the possibility of continuing. Few words that nevertheless give an important charge of energy for the work still to be done. I go alone with my Copis-Meg on my shoulder and the emergency tank while Boa, Giuseppe, Nadia and the Pied Piper take care of the transport between the third and second siphon, Proteus takes a tank and goes towards the exit . I re-emerge in the open air first after 220', while the efficient quartet re-emerges carrying practically all the material, after just over four hours.
We get one last big help carrying the material from the cave to the car from Iste, Laura and Enniosan.
Rio Torretta remained a question mark for many years, now it is becoming one of the longest explorations in northern Italy, with over 1500m of planimetric development.
