# 3-Day Trip from Nelson (ChatGPT)
**Why Nelson works**
- Legitimate, commonly used **longer-term parking** (town, campground, hire operators)
- You can paddle **upriver into the gorge country**, camp progressively, then return
- No tidal commitment unless you deliberately enter the estuary
- No shuttle, no mid-trip road access required
- Your car is waiting where food, fuel, and reception exist

The alternative (parking at Dartmoor) is workable, but Nelson gives you **better scenery density** per kilometre and more flexibility if weather turns.


This plan lets you:
- Camp away from the car
- Experience the **best limestone gorge sections**
- Spend **2–3 hours near McLennan’s Punt** on Day 2 without rushing
- Paddle fresh _against_ current first, then enjoy an easier return
### **Day 1 – Nelson → Patterson Canoe Camp**
- **Distance:** ~20–22 km upstream
- **Time:** ~5–6 hours at a steady solo pace
- **Overnight:** **Patterson Canoe Camp** 

**Why this works**
- You clear the wider lower river early
- Enter the more enclosed gorge sections by afternoon
- Patterson is a logical psychological and physical breakpoint
- Sets you up perfectly for McLennan’s Punt on Day 2

Expect calm water, slow current, and progressively tightening bends with good limestone walls.
### **Day 2 – Patterson → McLennan’s Punt (cave stop) → upstream camp**
- **Distance:** ~12–16 km total (depending how far you continue)
- **Key feature:** **2–3 hour stop near McLennan’s Punt**
- **Overnight:** **Skipworth Springs Canoe Camp** _or_ nearby upstream site

**Structure**
- Paddle upstream from Patterson in the morning
- Arrive at the McLennan’s Punt area late morning
- Secure kayak well above waterline
- Spend your **2–3 hours on the cave investigation**
- Continue **a few km further upstream** to camp

This keeps the cave stop unhurried and avoids camping right on the Punt (which you’ve already ruled out).

### **Day 3 – Return paddle: upstream camp → Nelson**
- **Distance:** ~28–32 km downstream
- **Time:** ~6–7 hours including breaks
- **Finish:** Back at Nelson, car waiting

This is a **long but friendly day**:
- Downstream assistance
- Familiar water
- Motivation of a clean finish and easy pack-down

If wind builds in the afternoon, you’ll already be moving with the river rather than fighting it.

## Distances at a glance (approx.)
- **Day 1:** 20–22 km
- **Day 2:** 12–16 km
- **Day 3:** 28–32 km  
    **Total:** ~60–65 km of river experienced

That’s a _lot_ of Glenelg in three days without ever touching a shuttle.

## Why this is the best fit for your constraints
- Single parking location ✔
- No daily returns to car ✔
- Proper multi-night canoe-camping ✔
- Mid-river cave time without stress ✔
- Conservative solo-friendly risk profile ✔

## Two important practical notes

### 1. Wind & timing
- Paddle **upstream early in the day**
- Aim to be back into Nelson **before late afternoon**
- Avoid committing into the estuary unless conditions are glassy
### 2. Campsite bookings
- Glenelg canoe camps **must be booked**
- You can only stay **one night per site**
- Build in a backup upstream camp in case your cave stop runs long
