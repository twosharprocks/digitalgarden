## Line Survey and Photogrammetry

Although the first line survey produced by the Wombats using the MNemo goes back as early as 2020, Allendale was surveyed only in 2022. The photogrammetry model produced by the Wombats in the same year was the first full cave model we produced and helped shape the equipment settings and configurations we used for future photogrammetry projects.

![](https://soggywombats.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/image-2.jpeg)

One of the great features of the MNemo it to give you a quick general idea of the direction the cave is heading. Grubby Wombat was the first Wombat to play with the first version of the equipment back in 2019. Most recently the wombats are using the MNemov2 in their projects. Check more information regarding how to learn and use MNemo in your projects in this [article](https://soggywombats.com.au/2024/04/15/making-sense-with-mnemo/) in our page.

Photogrammetry is a powerful tool, however a little complex to dominate in an dark, underground water filled environment. The Wombats used Allendale as the â€˜playgroundâ€™ to firstly understand and later develop different techniques of data gathering and how the available software deals with them.

Out of the first model produced by the Wombats, they have produce their first digital contour line map through [CloudCompare](https://www.danielgm.net/cc/). As divers, producing a map that can give you precise information about depth is fundamental for dive planning.

Later, using the Professional version of [Agisoft Metashape](https://www.agisoft.com/) and multiple trial and error attempts, the Wombats were able to merge surface and underwater scans. The accuracy of the model scale has been confirmed through measurements of objects inside the cave (drum barrel) as well as depth and overlaying geo-referenced maps (via [GoogleMaps](http://maps.google.com.au/)).
