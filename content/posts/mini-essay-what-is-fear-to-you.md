This morning you spent a lot of time thinking during your walk about what fear means to you. There were a lot of examples, but actually pinning it down felt really hard, and much of that comes down to it being similar to distinctively different to anxiety.

For me, fear is cold followed by force. Fear is something you tend to run at, something you feel and immediately react to with action. Fear isn't the every day concerns of things not working out or of something hypothetically happening - that's anxiety. Fear is trying to hold a door closed as three sailors smash into it from the other side to kill you and rape the two women in the room with you. Fear is grabbing the fire extinguisher off the wall and breaking the toes in the foot jammed in the same door. Fear is holding onto a plastic sheet carrying a friend as they're dying of hypothermia, as you run through the mud to get him into the back of an ambulance, then someone to help peel your fingers from the plastic because your hand has cramped onto it. Fear is going back to camp afterwards and finding two more, and knowing there's no one left to help but you. Fear is talking your Mum through all the things she needs to put in a Go-Bag, because you're sure that your father is going to try and kill her. Fear is begging her not to wait until he breaks in one night, to leave and get out now. Fear is him finding out where she's been hiding, and having him threaten to fly across the country to kill her as well as my sister and her family too. Fear is knowing exactly what you'd to him do if he ever did.

I'm not sure Dune got it quite right with "Fear is the mind killer" - I think anxiety is the real mind killer. Fear sharpens everything to down a point, and it's at that point that you find out if you fight, flee, or freeze. It's a point that gives you a decision to act or not act. Fear isn't about being overwhelmed either - that's panic, and you've known that too. Fear is a very distinct experience, where you know exactly what you need to do to survive (or keep someone else alive) and you just need to do it. 

So for you, anxiety is that lurking concern that erodes your wellbeing - it's a poison that seeps in and makes you overthink all the "what ifs" of your situation. Anxiety is worrying about falling off a rope when you're already on it. Panic is a loss of control, where your monkey brain takes over and screams a single overwhelming thought like "GET OUT!" or "BREATHE!". But fear is something else altogether - it's clear, cold, and unforgiving. Fear says "Do this, or you'll die". And you simply do it... or you die.
