At the end of January subscribers to Mars One’s Astronaut Selection Newsletter received an email asking for feedback on “Why would you like to go to Mars?”, with the idea being Mars One could use quotes from applicants in response to media requests.

Naturally like I suspect most subscribers had, I’d already come up with a few reasons in my head as to why being part of Mars One’s mission to establish a permanent human colony on Mars is so important to me. But here was an opportunity to put it all down in writing in 100 to 1000 words (this came to 953), and as such I’d like to share my full response with you all here.

Why would you like to go to Mars?

*Name: Josh Richards*  
 *Age: 27*  
 *Sex: Male*  
 *Nationality: Australian*  
 *Why would you like to go to Mars? (please use more than 100 and less than 1000 words)*

My eagerness\* to go to Mars comes from a deep-seated desire to be a part of something bigger than humanity has ever attempted before, and to be a source of inspiration for people everywhere that we can work together to create an amazing universe – today and long into the future.

Colonising Mars offers the opportunity to take our next “giant leap” forward on our journey as a species. Mars One may initially be a tiny outpost on another planet, but it represents something so much bigger – a diverse team of individuals all dedicated to the pursuit of discovering and sharing the wonders of the universe. Mars One acts as a beacon of hope, calling out to the rest of humanity “Here we are – there’s something bigger than the issues of politics, economics and war on Earth. We’ve worked together to leave all of that behind, and we’d like you to join us on our journey to explore the universe in peace”.

While there are obvious scientific benefits, a colony on Mars is ultimately a physical representation that our a species that has broken free of the bonds of our home planet. By putting aside arbitrary national boundaries and differences, we have spread to another planet not as any one country, but as fellow humans. Going to Mars offers the single greatest opportunity to put my heart on my sleeve and dedicate my life to inspiring ALL of humanity to believe it itself – to emotionally, psychologically and spiritually evolve into an interplanetary species.

But the journey to establish a colony on Mars will take a true explorer’s spirit – open-hearted people who will go to extraordinary physical, mental and emotional lengths to go where no human has gone before. And I want to be one of the souls to says “Yes – I will suffer the personal inconveniences, the dire isolation and the huge personal risks to do this”. Because I believe the inconveniences are only “inconvenient” if you refuse to evolve to the demands of your journey. Because the “isolation” is an illusion hiding the opportunity to work directly with 3 people I’ll have trained with for 10 years and trust implicitly, and be remotely supported in every sense by the most highly-skilled Earth-bound experts, as well as those preparing to follow us to Mars. And because I don’t believe there is anything more noble than service to others in the face of personal risk.

True explorers assess risk objectively and reduce it where practical, but ultimately accept that to step into the unknown unexpected hazards will come up. By expecting the unexpected, an explorer is prepared to adapt, adjust and evolve to achieve their goal. My goal is that people all over Earth will look up at night and see the red hue of Mars in the sky and think with pride “We’re up there.” Not pride of a single nation or race or culture – pride in humanity as a whole.

Part of accepting the risks and inconveniences of this journey is the burning desire to stand on the frontier of our species, acting as a trailblazer for those to come. And I hope the first Mars One crew would more than just survive under the personal hardships of that first manned trip – those challenging first two years of building, testing and adjusting the habitat to it’s unique Martian environment.

I believe the first crew have the chance to flourish in this alien environment – to see the challenges Mars brings and turn them into opportunities. To truly evolve into Earth-born Martians, loving their new and unique life and being there to welcome new arrivals not to a habitat, but to a home. A home that with time will itself grow into a community based on flexibility, adaptability and compassion for all of our species.

By being the first to go to Mars, a tremendous opportunity is available to serve those who come after – to learn those early and hard lessons, so that others need not. I’d like to go to Mars to light the path for the rest of humanity into the solar system – to follow in the footsteps of Shackleton; Cousteau; Columbus; the Pilgrims on the Mayflower; Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins; to go where no human has gone before and spread our horizons. To see the larger picture of our existence. And I believe Mars is the greatest opportunity we currently have to make our next giant leap into the darkness with the torch of humanity.

And if I can’t be first, I’ll go second. Or third. Or one hundredth. Or one thousandth. Or whatever number. And I will strive to support those who are selected to go with me – before, after or otherwise – because I believe if we stop seeking answers to the universe, stop believing in our potential as a species, and stop trying to discover who we are by pushing our comfort zone; we stagnate as a species. We implode and become consumed with ourselves – with our looks, our technology, or our place in a fabricated social hierarchy  But if just a few brave souls are willing to step up and push beyond the “norm”, and if others declare “I will follow you on this journey into the universe”, together we can inspire and give hope to those who doubt our species capacity for compassion and courage.

Why would I like to go to Mars? I want to give hope to those who doubt the power of collective & personal courage, inspire others to action through the far-reaching effects of selfless service, and to share with humanity the simple joy of compassion for others.

*\*Note: My original email used “desire” instead of “eagerness”. It has been changed to be less repetitive, as I use the phrase “deep-seated desire” later in the sentence.*
