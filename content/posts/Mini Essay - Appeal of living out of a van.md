One of the ideas that has stuck with me for decades now is the idea of living alone out of a van. I had the opportunity to do it when I went to Iceland in 2016, and it's always stuck with me as a wonderful but slightly dislocated existence - like I knew I enjoyed having everything in my car and being free to go anywhere at any time, but not feeling like I could really stop anywhere either.

The idea of living out of a van - specifically a VW Transporter - first crystalised when I was in the Marines, and became fully formed as soon as I left. I remember wandering around Plymouth taking photos of vans I liked the shape and colour of, and was already dreaming then about what it would be like to live in one. Looking back on it now, it's pretty clear that my desire to live in a van really comes from being self-contained and having the freedom to leave whenever I like and be able to stay safe and secure by staying on the road.

I've always been drawn to a minimalist approach, and for many years I said that if I couldn't carry it on my back then I couldn't own it. That form of travel was pretty extreme, but it suited the way I lived at the time - no fixed address, ready to load up and leave whenever I needed to, and really only carrying what I needed to get to the next place. The idea of living out of van is really a gentler extension of that mindset, that I could have a little more than a backpack provided everything could fit in my car and I could drive away at at time. I still didn't want to account for anything more than I could pack up and bolt from if I needed to.

Maybe that's the key here difference between a desire for a van and actual van-life: I get a sense of security from being able to pack up all my stuff and take it wherever I want at a moments notice, whereas I haven't yet figured out how to "live" from a van. I haven't yet figured out what to do when I get wherever I've gone to, I just feel safer knowing that I can go *somewhere* if my current environment turns bad. The van (and the backpack) were all about security through mobility, and what I'd really like is to reach a point where I want to spend time in my van not to *get* somewhere but to simply *be* somewhere with it. 

Part of me also thinks that I don't need to be ready to run the way I used to, but I'm not sure I'm over that yet - the impulse remains. The desire for remote work comes from doing what I need to do for an income but being able to do it on the move from anywhere. I really struggle with the idea of being stuck somewhere, and remote work from a van absolutely offers that unlimited mobility I'm hoping for - the flexibility that makes me feel safer, knowing that I'm staying somewhere because I want to be there, and I can be anywhere else if I want.

Right now I'm okay where I am, and part of me wants to stay in a house and be "stable" for awhile to train myself that I don't need to run. But another part of me still wants to have the option to pack up and go anytime, and knowing I have as little stuff as necessary (and that it could all fit in a car or van) is going to continue to reassure me. So I'm not being romantic about the idea of living out of a van - my interest is very much based in a deep sense of insecurity around my home life, which started long before I ever moved to Adelaide.
