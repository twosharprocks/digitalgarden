# Additional Notes

Location
The outlet of the karst spring is not far from the village of Arogno at the foot of Monte Generoso, right next to the road. Along with "Cà del Feree", "Bucco della Sovaglia" and "Sorgente del Paolaccio", it is one of the most important sources of Monte Generosso. This limestone massif, which rises to an altitude of 1701m, is to the north and west of Lake Lugano , bordered on the east by Lago di Como and on the SSW by the Capolago-Chiasso zone, the main massif covers an area of ​​about 50km 2 , the whole area of ​​Monte Generoso includes more than 100km 2

For a few years the source outlet has been fenced and the Access is only possible with a key, which can be obtained upon presentation of the cave diving certificate and proof of insurance on the Municipal administration of Arogno. Caution: Restricted opening times!
We can recommend the Hotel Pignatta in Arogno (German-speaking) as a good place to stay.

Description
The spring can usually be dived all year round. Only large thunderstorms or melt water in spring impair visibility or even make diving impossible.
Except for a few narrow passages that are easy to pass, the cave passage extends for the first 20m, spacious and almost free of sediments or percolation, down to a depth of -60m. Here the cave branches out. On the left the corridor follows a narrow fissure back to the surface. This passage is very narrow in parts and extremely cloudy in places due to heavy clay deposits.

After passing an elliptical corridor filled with clay, you come to a surface just below the mountain flank. Here the short, dry cave passage leads to a filled shaft that used to lead to the surface.
If you follow the cave passage at the junction to the right, it leads continuously to a bottleneck at -89m. If you pass this bottleneck, the corridor now climbs steeply upwards. Here, too, visibility is greatly reduced by percolation. The passage branches several times but only one leads to the emergence point which is deep in Monte Generoso. Here follows a widely ramified, dry cave system that can only be entered with shaft technology.

Story
The Sorgente Bossi, called "Böss" by the locals, was first explored by Primo Meli from Rovio in 1974 to a depth of -47m. In 1985, Olivier Isler and Walter Keusen were the first to dive through the bottleneck at -89m and followed the ascending cave passage to -40m. In 1992,  Luigi Cassati (Gigi) was the first to reach the surface inside Monte Generoso. From 2005 to 2006, Luigi Cassati and Jean Jacques Bolanz (†07) explored the dry cave system discovered behind the siphon (post-siphon) several times and explored it.

Unfortunately, in 2006, during a preparatory dive, an  accident occurred in which an Italian diver died. 
Due to the tragic death of his friend and diving buddy Jean Jacques Bolanz shortly afterwards, Gigi put further exploration of the Bossi on hold.

Present
We have been diving the Sorgente Bossi regularly for years. Diving through the deep siphon with an open system was always a big and expensive (gas costs) battle of materials. Since the use of the rebreather, the effort and the high gas costs have now been massively reduced. With the consent of Gigi and the local speleo club, we began further exploration of the cave in the winter of 2008/09.
At first we concentrated on the left siphon. Unfortunately, this part soon had no more potential for possible further exploration. However, since diving this side arm is very dangerous, we decided after the end of the project to lay a completely new line from the branch to the surface for the safety of subsequent cave divers.

So now that this part has been completed, we have concentrated on further exploration of the dry cave system behind the right siphon. Unfortunately, at the moment it looks like the whole previously known part will have to be completely re-measured. In addition, large chimneys must be opened up with the help of an aluminum mast. This takes a lot of time and is not without risk. We shudder every time we think of a rescue through a -89m deep siphon.
Luckily, since autumn 2009, our colleagues Sebastian Kuster & Hubert Zistler have been supporting us in further exploration of the cave.
