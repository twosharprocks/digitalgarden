[Insta - ADHD + Autism masking](https://www.instagram.com/p/DNtMk1F3GVm/?img_index=1) - AuDHD
- Outgoing ADHD traits can mask autistic struggles with social rules.
- How it can show up:
	- Using humour or storytelling to connect (ADHD)
	- Taking things literally and missing hidden meanings (Autism)
	- Struggling with sarcasm or vague hints (Autism)
	- Oversharing or interrupting without realizing (ADHD)
	- Socializing feels like work, not rest (Autism)
	- Being the "life of the party," then suddenly ghosting without explanation (Overlap)
	- Talking over others out of excitement (ADHD)
	- Flat or monotone tone even when engaged (Autism)
	- Switching topics quickly (ADHD)
	- Using very precise or literal language (Autism)
	- Talking for hours about a passion... but monotone delivery hides how much they care (Overlap)
	- Overcommitting when energized (ADHD)
	- Forgetting personal needs while hyperfocusing on tasks (ADHD)
	- Sudden withdrawal from overload (Autism)
	- Needing long downtime to recover (Autism)
	- Looking unstoppable one week, unreachable the next (Overlap)
	- Burnout cycles repeating across months or years (Overlap)
	- Heightened sensitivity to lights, sounds, or textures (Autism)
	- Repetitive movements like shifting side to side when standing or rotating objects like pens or coins (Autism)
	- Zoning out mid-conversation or task (ADHD)
	- Jumping between tasks when overwhelmed by the environment (ADHD)
	- Using fidgets or restless movement to stay regulated (Overlap)
	- Forgetting everyday tasks (ADHD)
	- Relying on rigid routines to cope (Autism)
	- Starting many projects without finishing (ADHD)
	- Struggling with transitions or unexpected changes (Autism)
	- Planners, apps, sticky notes everywhere... yet still forgetting the one thing that mattered (Overlap)
	- Intense excitement or frustration (ADHD)
	- Crying or anger that feels uncontrollable (ADHD)
	- Shutting down or going quiet under stress (Autism)
	- Emotional reactions triggered by sensory overload (Autism)
	- Feelings that hit fast and linger longer than expected (Overlap)
	- Hyperfocus on special interests (ADHD)
	- Racing through work at the last minute with bursts of energy (ADHD)
	- Deep knowledge in narrow areas (Autism)
	- Needing structure and clear steps to function (Autism)
	- Teachers or bosses see the talent, miss the struggle (Overlap)
	- Peaks of brilliance alongside sudden crashes (Overlap)
	- Making friends quickly (ADHD)
	- Saying yes to plans impulsively, then regretting later (ADHD)
	- Struggling to sustain long-term connections (Autism)
	- Missing subtle social cues (Autism)
	- Friendships feel all-or-nothing, too intense or too distant (Overlap)
	- AuDHD
		- Being seen as "too capable" to need support
		- Burnout cycles mistaken for laziness
		- Sensory or emotional sensitivity dismissed as weakness
		- Inconsistency mistaken for lack of effort
		- AuDHD doesn't look like one or the other. It looks like both, and neither, all at once.

[Insta - Exhausted by dumbness, mediocrity](https://www.instagram.com/p/DGwZCydO6hE/)
- "That's what's exhausting about it, because *no one is actually saying anything*. When you and I have conversations I'm not exhausted by it because we're talking about things, but when you have these stupid casual, ridiculous, mundane social interactions with the average person every day wherever you're at, they're not saying anything that matters. So that's the part that's exhausting. *There's probably some kind of energy being spent tolerating boring, mediocre, dumb conversations - that's more what it feels like. Fighting that off expends energy, so it's exhausting fighting off dumbness.* Dumb mediocrity. Whereas *if I'm having an intellectually stimulating conversation and learning things and having differing opinions on important topics, I don't find that exhausting, I find that energising*. I really think that's part of it - whatever energy is being spent blocking the stupid, which is a *lot*."

[Insta - Trevor Noah on saying "No"](https://www.instagram.com/p/DRi3wK1jcB9/)
- "*Start with no*. Changed my whole life. Everything people ask you, start with no, and you'll be shocked at how it changes. Because that's what I realised happens to ADHD people. Our brains are moving so quickly, someone says something, we love being stimulated, and so Christiana says 'Hey, can you babysit my kid this week' 'Yeah I'd love to' I said it immediately. And then I leave the conversation, I pull out my phone, and I go 'Oh, this weekend I have a gig that I have to do, and I forgot about this thing' but you send me a text going 'Thank you so much for agreeing to babysit' and I go 'Ahh damn what do I do now? Wait, I think I can do this - what time is the actual babysitting? From 4 to 6, my gig is at 6:30 so I could, it's a 27min drive - I think I can'. And now I'm back in that loop. And then what happens is I disappoint you on the day because I get overwhelmed. Because the day comes, my times aren't lining up, I can't get to you on time and then what happens to a lot of people with ADHD I know is there's the shame that comes with it. Because you were trying to do the thing for everyone. What happens is you fail everyone. And because, rightfully so on their side, they don't know what you were going through, and so they just go 'Why didn't you tell me, why did you agree to do this?'

[Insta - Chaotic Discipline](https://www.instagram.com/p/DLBxeaHshba/)
- "You could have something called chaotic discipline. Some days you going to be a big old couch potato. You ain't going to want to do nothing, you barely want to breathe. You don't even want to get out of bed to eat, you just want to lay in the bed. But some days your ass is going to be on fire. When you have those on fire days you get a week's worth of stuff done in a day. When you on fire, you on FIRE! So having ADHD you're not going to have regular discipline like a normal person. You're NOT neurotypical, you're neurodivergent. Your discipline is different from everyone else's. Neurotypical discipline is more consistent. Your type of discipline is actually more productive. Three days of the week you're just a couch potato. You don't want to do nothing, you don't want to talk to nobody, you're just laying there. But those other four days of the week you're getting a month's worth of work done. So if you got ADHD, don't feel bad about yourself on those couch potato days. You need those! It's literally part of your disciplinary action. Because when you get back to those on-fire days, you're going to be getting everything and anything done."
