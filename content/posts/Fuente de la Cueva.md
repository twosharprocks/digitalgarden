GENERAL INFORMATION
Fuente de la Cueva is a permanent spring that gives birth to the Mundilla River . This source opens under a small ridge with a clear retreat of the limestone cliff, the waters forming a beautiful succession of waterfalls and cascades adorned with some tuff deposits. The emergency point, captured for the drinking water supply of several towns, is a narrow horizontal crack that appears impenetrable. Due to the use of the waters that flow from this cavity, it is advisable to have the corresponding authorization to access it.

The cavity is located on the SE slope of Lora Alta, a small plateau half Burgos and half Palencia, close to another larger one with the shape of a croissant ., called Lora de Valdivia. In both Loras, separated from each other by a small transversal valley, several areas of lapiáz stand out as well as its numerous sinkholes, which dot the entire surface without apparent order. La Lora de Valdivia, hydrologically related to Fuente de la Cueva, takes its name from the picturesque Valley of the Ivia River, a river that has its source on the central-western border of said plateau or páramo through Covalagua, an important karstic upwelling that at its This time gives its name to the Natural Area that extends from Pomar de Valdivia (Palencia) towards the East to the border with the provinces of Burgos and Cantabria.

Covalagua UpwellingGEOGRAPHICAL AND GEOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK
La Lora de Valdivia and Lora Alta are located to the south of the Cantabrian mountain range and form a small part of a large sector of páramos (Lora de la Lora structural platform ) that with a WNW-ESE orientation extend from the northeast of the province of Palencia to the northwestern area of ​​that of Burgos.

Its main morphological characteristic is that of an extensive elevated platform, practically horizontal and that structurally constitutes an inverted relief as it is formed by a hanging syncline. The materials on which it develops are predominantly calcareous and belong to the Cretaceous series, being made up of sandy limestones, oolitic limestones and limestones with rudists (Middle - Upper Turonian - Coniacian) having as impermeable base level, hydrologically speaking, a layer of sandy marls of the lower Turonense cave fountain.

HYDROGEOLOGY
The surface of both Loras is approximately 14 square km and constitutes the main recharge zone of this aquifer, as the limestone materials that emerge show intense fracturing as well as a notable profusion of sinkholes, which together favor rapid infiltration. The subterranean circulation of water is fundamentally conditioned by the underlying layer of marls and by the syncline structure, whose axis crosses Las Loras from WNW to ESE. This syncline structure also presents small transverse anticlinal undulations in the SSO-NNE direction, which possibly determines that in Lora de Valdivia there is, broadly speaking, a drainage hydrogeological divide, with a part of the waters flowing to the W (valley of the Ivia) and another towards the SE.

Thus, the two main discharge points of this aquifer are, to the WEST, the Covalagua Upwelling -Source of the Ivia River- and to the EAST Fuente de la Cueva  -Source of the Mundilla River-. The first pours its waters into the Duero hydrographic network, while the second does so into the Ebro. In addition, there are several intermittent springs bordering both Loras that are located at similar levels, as they also emerge at the contact between the limestone and the section marl bottom.

DISCOVERY, HISTORY OF EXPLORATIONS AND BRIEF DESCRIPTION .
This exploration has been carried out by the Madrid interclub FLASH, GEODA, MINAS and GAEM and has had the support of CEFME as well as the collaboration of members of GET, GUIAS and ISER .
Year 2009;
At the end of 2009 , a team made up of members of the NUBIS , MINAS, GEODA , FLASH and GAEM clubs carried out a visit to the Covalagua Upwelling (Palencia) to begin the underwater exploration work of the cavity. During this first stay in Revilla de Pomar, he contacted several residents of the A. de Amigos de Revilla , gathering information on possible resurgent caves or nearby springs .

Siberian Environment in the PáramoThe result is the location of several points, a priori very interesting, and which would require a detailed inspection to assess their possible speleological and exploration interest. One of them, located in nearby Lora Alta ( Villaescobedo , Burgos ) was of special interest and despite the short time available, it was decided to pay a brief visit. The upwelling was the Nacimiento del Mundilla .

With the help of Tom, a resident of Villaescobedo, the cavity is located, noting that it is a narrow horizontal crack from which abundant water flows. A quick inspection through diving goggles surprisingly revealed that a small submerged conduit opened up behind the narrow crevice. Ten days later, with all snow covered and -16ºC, he returned with immersion equipment, managing to cross the submerged section of the entrance, but what is better, exploring after him 340m of virgin conduit (TOM Gallery) until finally coming across a pass impenetrable (El Paso de Genaro) Participants : Alfonso Gutiérrez, Cecilio López, Roberto Cano, JL Izquierdo . _____________________________________________ 2010. January;

Accessing the cavity on foot from Villaescobedo

At the beginning of the year, and coinciding with an intense snowfall to the north, they turned to try to clear the Paso de Genaro, convinced of the great potential that this recently discovered cavity hides. The attempt was a success, being able to resume exploration through a conduit similar to the previous one, characterized by its small dimensions and by the curious covering of its walls and ceilings. The real surprise came after traveling 170 new meters to connect perpendicularly (El Cruce) with a larger gallery with the appearance of a collector. Baptized with the unoriginal name of Galería del Río,This appeared as a notable conduit, probably related to the axis of the syncline, through which an important underground river circulates. In general, it develops in favor of stratification planes and tom's galleryan important vadose engagement stands out in its final zone.

On this glorious day, more than 1 km of pipes are explored upstream, finally reaching a wide set of rooms re-excavated by water, located after a narrow and uncomfortable meander developed in favor of a joint lattice (Meandro de las Dolinas). At the end from one of these rooms the first final siphon of the cavity appears. On the way back, downstream, the first meters of the Quarry Gallery are examined.

2010. February-March;
In the following months, and despite the terrible weather conditions, visits to the new cavity continue, focusing the exploration and topography, upstream, on two lateral branches with a significant water supply: El Meandro del Siphon and La Galería de los bridges . The first appears to the right at the end of the G. del Río and is generally a narrow and long meander heading NNW that, like the Meandro de las Dolinas, ended in a siphon. About 100m from the beginning of this meander, there is a short hanging branch with a modest contribution of water ( Ramal de la Cascada )

. For its part, the Gallery of BridgesIt opens at the end of the G. del Río on its left side, and its name comes from the numerous rock bridges that appear in its initial section. This gallery is hung with respect to the G. del Río and an important flow circulates through it. 2010. April-May-July; The exploration of the Gallery of the Quarry begins from the Crossing. This gallery, which is nothing more than the continuation of the G. del Río , develops preferably in a SE direction. Currently, its operation as an active gallery has been relegated only to periods of large avenues because Tom's G. has taken away its prominence, draining from El Cruce, the main watercourse of the cavity. The most notable characteristic of this gallery are the mud deposits that cover the walls and floors in the first section.

Its dimensions, with a predominance of height over width, make transit through it very comfortable, at least until it reaches its middle, from which the heights begin to decrease and the widths increase. In the final area of ​​this gallery the presence of water is reduced and numerous collapses and landslides can be seen. The point of exploration in this gallery is in fact in a collapse in which the fallen flakes make it impossible to pass. At that time it is thought that this gallery, beyond the end reached, could have an "unlimited" Syncline continuation down, since being the "old" main drainage course, it could channel the water towards some ancient and distant unknown upwelling. Later it would be seen that we were wrong.

2010. August-September;
In the G. de la Cantera it is possible to force its end, confirmed after a quick incursion the important continuation of it. Likewise, exploration work continues in other areas, but above all in topography. It is precisely while completing the topography of the entrance area when it is discovered that the small right side branch, which had been considered impenetrable, has a continuation. The surprise is great when verifying that the direction and morphology of this conduit unequivocally relate it to the end of the Galería de la Cantera. Finally the connection is achieved, concluding that this was the old main drainage route.

On a new visit to the Clay Branch they advance about 100m up to a narrow pass with water current. The last visits to the cavity are devoted to completing and checking the topography as well as taking sections.
