A new week – a new venue! Week 3 of SHSSP16 kicked off away from the usual Mawson Centre lecture theatre, and instead we led the participants over to the [Institute for Telecommunication Research](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/https://www.itr.unisa.edu.au/) (ITR). Professor Bill Cowley (head of ITR’s academic staff) has been working with SHSSP16’s stratospheric balloon payload team since the start of the program, but now had the chance to formally discuss the challenges of data & spectrum management in space.

![Physics, but not as we know it - I honestly don't understand half the things Bill says](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/bill-1024x576.jpg)

Electromagnetic wave physics, but not as we know it – I honestly don’t understand half of what Bill says

After a full day of having their minds blown at ITR, the course were invited to a closed-session on the future of Australia in Space that evening chaired by Co-Director Michael Davis. Alongside [Jeff Kasparian](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/http://www.unisanet.unisa.edu.au/Staff/Homepage.asp?Name=jeff.kasparian), [Peter Nikoloff](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/http://www.novasystems.com/management/index.html#2), and Alex Senata from the [Australian Space Policy Unit](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/http://www.industry.gov.au/INDUSTRY/IndustrySectors/SPACE/Pages/default.aspx); there was a very frank discussion about the future of Australian space activities & government policy. Nerves got a little frayed, but with course’s stratospheric balloon launch the next morning everyone was understandably pretty stressed.

Not that they had anything to worry about. Our stratospheric balloon launch on Australia Day was a complete success, which was surely down to the meticulous planning that went into it.

![Serious Balloon Planning Is Serious](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516im_/http://www.themightyginge.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Balloon.jpg)

With live updates coming from the balloon tracking team, it wasn’t long before we got word it had burst at a maximum altitude of 36.4km and was returning to Earth under a parachute. The recovery teams tracked it’s decent through the bush for hours, before finding the payload intact on the ground (and not strung up in a tree).

After a HUGE day though we all headed back to Mawson Lakes for a BBQ and the Australian Day culture night. The university had lost all power while we’d been gone, but our illustrious Aussies pushed on with their culture night anyway, and still managed to include aboriginal art, eating vegemite sandwiches, and playing Goon of Fortune despite the power outage.

With the balloon launch now complete though, the course started to shift their focus on to the food & water security white paper they had just 2 weeks left to produce. Between lectures from Juan De Dalmau (Head of ESTEC Communications), Will Reid (Australian Center for Field Robotics), and Charley Lineweaver this week; the participants have started to really get their teeth into the structure and content of what will be the program’s final product.

With stress over the white paper and the final 4 exam building though, we held a particularly tense class meeting on Friday to resolve some of the participant concerns. After a tense few hours the course elected a new project management team to keep things on track, and between the teaching associates and the participants we found a way to deal with many of the issues. Things like this are never pleasant, but ISU is all about overcoming challenges to do something extraordinary – SHSSP16 certainly overcame one of their biggest hurdles of the entire course that day, and should be exceptionally proud of themselves for doing it relatively painlessly.

With a renewed sense of direction and purpose though, a sense of relief washed over everyone – staff and participants alike. Which is just as well, because after the management change we went pretty much straight into the Canadian and Chinese culture night! Needless to say the whole course had a lot of steam to blow off, so the less said about Friday night the better…

Looking ahead to week 4 we’re starting to get down to the pointy end of the course: we’ll see the end of lectures, the final exam, and then *almost* complete focus on getting the white paper deliverables done. I say *almost*, because among all this our SHSSP16 course director [John Connolly](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/http://mmp.planetary.org/astro/connj/connj70.htm) will be sharing his life work of designing missions to Mars, the world’s only space ethicist [Jacques Arnould](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Arnould) will share **his**life’s work, and then on Thursday night I’ll be on a panel about [Space and Popular Culture](https://web.archive.org/web/20160205151516/http://www.unisa.edu.au/Calendar/Southern-Hemisphere-Space-Studies---Space-and-Popular-Culture/) where I’ll be sharing the ridiculous series of events that led me to signing up for a one-way mission to Mars and writing comedy shows about it.
