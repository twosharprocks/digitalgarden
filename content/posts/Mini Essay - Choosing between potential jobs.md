Right now you're looking at two different job opportunities, and both of them offer different elements of what you're looking for in a career in cyber. It's also important to remember that you don't *need* either of these roles - they're the first to come back to you, and they've both provided you with 2nd interviews today, but that doesn't mean that either of them is the right fit for you - only that they're the things that are being offered this week.

On one side we have a junior analyst role with a really renowned MSSP - a company that I've seen as the best around since before I started working in this industry, and still see as the best organisation in the business. They have made a solid offer, and the perks of working there are pretty incredible, however the role and the income are both a step backwards from where I've been. On the plus side, the job will be incredibly easy compared to what I've been doing, it'll allow me to develop skills and work on my own things (I'll almost certainly get a book published next year if I accept their role), and the money is still very good (just not as good as I've been on, or what I could get elsewhere). The role may be a step backwards, and it'll be a whole lot less interesting than what I might do elsewhere, but I'd be joining an organisation that is there for the long-haul - if I'm serious about working in cybersecurity in Australia for the next few years, then this would be the place to be. Likewise, they'd be making a significant investment in me - training, support, mentoring, security clearance, and other opportunities that I simply wouldn't get elsewhere.

On the other side, I have the high probability (but not an offer) to work in a fully remote role on more money than I currently am, and do it as a senior threat intelligence analyst with an emerging organisation that is directly supporting critical infrastructure. If I stop and think about it, my concerns here are bigger - I'd be getting the well-paying remote role that I've been seeking, but I'd be doing it by stepping into a role that I'm not 100% sure I'm ready for, which will involve a great deal of extra work and study to keep my head above water before I became confident enough to hold it. I'd be earning good money, but I'd be potentially pidgeon-holing myself into threat intelligence (something that is already quite niche) while pushing to stay on top of everything that's going on in the world. Yes, I'd be earning good money and I'd be working from home, but I'd also have nowhere to go to when I wanted to work out of the house. 

A lot of this comes down to what I was asked in my interview with the threat intel CEO today - where do I want to be in 3-5 years? There are lots of things on the horizon, and I will have a lot of flexibility to wherever I go, so I need to have a sense of why I'm doing this and what I want to be doing when I'm in my mid 40's. Do I want to push myself with cyber and invest more time and energy into developing it, or do I want to step back slightly and do something much easier for a year? Would the threat intel role really be pushing myself, or would it be an opportunity to learn more about something I'm interested in while being paid good money?

Right now I only have one offer, and that's for a well-paying (but not exceptional) role at the same level that I'm currently at. The MSSP is well-renowned, and it would be without a doubt a wonderful place to work - I just need to decide if taking a pay cut and a career regression for a year is worth it to join them. And if I get offered the senior threat intel role, I need to ask myself if that's the direction I really want to take myself at the expense of an opportunity at the MSSP. Right now I don't know what I should do, and I'm a little scared to ask my gut which direction I should be heading either...

My gut is saying I should go with the MSSP, and that I shouldn't wait around to hear what other offers come through. I have a coffee catchup with the MSSP's SOC Manager on Tuesday morning, so I'll ask myself again before then... but based on what I'm feeling now with a clear head and open heart, it feels like the right role to accept is the one that's being offered to me.
