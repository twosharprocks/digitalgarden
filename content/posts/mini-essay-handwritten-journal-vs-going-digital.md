There's no question that writing has played an absolutely vital role in your development since you first discovered Mars One in September 2012. It took awhile for you to figure out how you wanted to do this, but handwritten journals have formed the mainstay for more than a decade. In the last few years though, you've noticed your journaling drop right off, with regular gaps ever since 2020 and the COVID nightmare. You were working so hard on Cosmic Nomad that you poured out your heart and mind into that book, leaving no desire or need to then write by hand in a journal. And the reality is you've never really picked that up properly since. 

The question in this essay is whether you return to handwritten journals, whether your mini essays and daily notes can replace the journal, or if there's something in-between. You know that typing doesn't always work - you have your little Bluetooth keyboard for typing on your phone, but there is still an appeal in writing without a screen. Most of what you do is typing, and the most meaningful writing you do is typed into a keyboard like this. But curling up in bed and pouring your heart out into a handwritten note still holds huge appeal - it's still a vital part of your expression. If you want to photograph your journals and use OCR to transcribe them, then that's still an option. But you do need to write by hand occasionally, so it's important to keep some sort of journal around. Does it need to be a moleskin? No. And will you still keep looking for digital alternatives? Of course - the move to digital seems very natural, just as you've looked a the Remarkable e-paper devices so you can write a journal without it going on paper. 

But the devices can't replace the pure simplicity of paper *just* yet. There'll come a time in your future that they will, and so it's good that you're consolidating everything into Obsidian the way you are - eventually everything you write and think about will be available here. So for now, keep your green journal around, but definitely don't feel bad for not writing in it every day - you're shifting your writing more fully into the digital realm, and you're becoming a more consistent writer for it. 

Think about things, take notes on things, and write up little essays on things the way you are here. By typing and thinking about the world you live in, you're clearing your mind the same way you do with a handwritten journal, AND you're producing an easily referenced digital product that you can index, copy/paste from, and edit without it needing to be some museum piece. By typing this stuff and putting it all into Obsidian, you're slowly creating the digital garden that you so desperately want to share with people - soon it'll be ready, but not if you writer everything into a green journal no one (not even you) will really read.
