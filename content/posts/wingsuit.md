- *Complete AFF (Accelerated Freefall)*
      - Typically **8 instructor-led jumps**
      - You demonstrate stability, altitude awareness, safe deployment, and landing control
	- Outcome: you are cleared to jump **solo under supervision**.

- *Certificate A (Initial Solo Licence)*
- [Learn to Skydive - Adelaide Skydiving Centre](https://www.adelaideskydivingcentre.com.au/learn-to-skydive) 
	- Typical requirements:
		- ~25 stable freefall jumps
		- Demonstrated safe landings
	    - Canopy accuracy basics
	    - Written exam + instructor sign-off
	- Outcome: you are a fully licensed solo skydiver.

- *Certificate B (Intermediate jumper)
	- Typical requirements:
		- **50 stable freefall jumps**
		- Improved landing accuracy
		- Better air awareness and group discipline
		- Participation in coached jumps
	- Outcome: you’re no longer a beginner and can join more complex skydives.

- *Certificate C (Advanced jumper)*
	- Typical requirements:
		- **100 stable freefall jumps**
		- Reliable tracking skills
		- Consistent, accurate landings
		- Aircraft spotting and exit judgement
	- Outcome: you are trusted with more autonomy and responsibility in the air.

- *Certificate D (Expert jumper – mandatory for wingsuit)*
	- This is the **gatekeeper certificate** for wingsuiting.
	- Requirements include:
		- **200 stable freefall jumps**
		- **20 witnessed accuracy landings**
		- Within 5 m of target centre (or 10 × 5 m runway)
		- Demonstrated ability to guide the aircraft to the exit point
		- Strong freefall separation and deployment discipline
	- Outcome: you are legally eligible to begin wingsuit training in Australia.

- *Wingsuit training (formal endorsement)*
	- Training must be conducted by a **recognised Wingsuit Coach**
	- You follow the APF **Wingsuit Training Table (WSTT)**
	- Core elements:
		- Ground instruction specific to wingsuits
		- Suit fitting and emergency procedures
		- Exit technique
		- Flight control (pitch, roll, glide)
		- Separation and deployment planning

### Step 9: Wingsuit Training Table (Parts A, B, C)

You progress only when signed off by your coach and DZSO.

- **Part A** – first wingsuit flights
    
    - Solo, conservative flights
        
    - Focus on stability, altitude awareness, safe deployment
        
- **Part B** – controlled flight and navigation
    
    - Improved glide control
        
    - Defined flight plans
        
    - Limited, approved wingsuit relative work
        
- **Part C** – consolidation and competency
    
    - Demonstrates full situational awareness
        
    - Safe integration with other jumpers
        
    - Reliable emergency handling
        

Additional requirements:

- Audible altimeter (mandatory)
    
- Minimum exit height of **10,000 ft AGL** for training jumps
    

---

### Step 10: Award of the APF Wingsuit Crest

Once you successfully complete **WSTT Part C**, the APF issues the **Wingsuit Crest**.

Outcome:  
You are now **formally wingsuit-qualified** and may fly wingsuits at APF-approved drop zones, within DZ rules.

## What this looks like in real numbers

From zero experience, expect approximately:
- **200–220 skydives**  
- **12–24 months** depending on jump frequency
- Significant coaching time (especially canopy accuracy and tracking)

Wingsuit flying sits at the **very top** of the sport because it magnifies:
- Poor separation
- Weak altitude discipline
- Sloppy deployments
- Inconsistent canopy control
