There’s a great scene early in The Life Aquatic with Steve Zissou - at the film screening on the night before the expedition begins – where Eleanor Zissou asks Steve “How are you feeling?”. His response has always resonated with me: “I’m right on the edge. I don’t know what comes next.”

Since 2008, every March and April has been pretty turbulent for me to say the least. Often January and February are challenging too, but March is usually when every plan I’ve made turns to pus and April is notoriously when my life does a back flip in a new direction. Weirdly enough, April 18th to the 25th (today) is without fail the week it all suddenly turns around. From bottoming out in frustration at jobs in March then quitting in the April, to recognising doomed relationships in March then moving on in April – for the last 6 years these two months have signalled major life upheavals, if not several of them. I always come out the other side in late April/early May in a much better place than I was the year before… but it’s still a pretty scary experience while you’re in the middle of it.

This year has been no different.

I had high (misguided) hopes in February that the 5 weeks at ISU SHSSP14 in Adelaide would be an overnight launch pad into the space industry. Don’t get me wrong – SHSSP14 will forever be a time I look back too and think “THAT is when it started”. But it was only a start. Deorbit after the course was rough, but I’ve had rougher. And in retrospect there was always going to be an incubation period afterwards too – I’d just hoped I could skip the usual March/April challenge this year.

What has been different though was knowing the upheaval and uncertainty was probably coming, knowing I’d eventually come out the other side if it did, and that life becomes significantly brighter after than it ever has been before. It always gets better.

Mars One announced on December 30th last year that 1058 astronaut candidates had been selected to undergo medicals, with another announcement about selection interviews expected “in April”. So much of the last 4 months has been a mixture of preparing and waiting – I passed my medical without any issue, but after that there was nothing else I could do to accelerate the selection process. As far as I know, most candidates have just gone about their regular day jobs, keeping their ears open for updates but otherwise keeping calm and carrying on with their regular lives. But after I came back to Australia to promote Mars One, this is my life now.

As soon as I recognised in late Feb that I wasn’t being immediately launched back out of Perth to work in the space industry, I decided I needed to bring space here. Western Australia is one of the best places in the world if you want to LOOK at stars – my old university is home to one of the most respected radio astronomy research groups in the world. But people look at you weird if you say you want to EXPLORE stars. Or other planets. Or even just visit space.

So for the last few months I’ve been changing that – generating a grassroots interest in space science here in Perth. I had a vague idea of doing the same thing last year, but neither Perth or I were really ready for it – I still needed to learn more about Mars, how space agencies & science education centres work, and I needed to plant the seeds of inspiration in a few key science educators last year before they were ready to get fully onboard. I’ve been busy rebranding myself as a science speaker instead of just a comedian, and my book about the Mars One mission is taking shape too… although but if it’s anything like writing my comedy shows, I’ll spend another 3 months conceptualising and trialing out ideas, then right the WHOLE THING is about 3 weeks.

However by far the biggest focus since February has been running Perth’s first “Yuri’s Night” event - celebrating the anniversary of Yuri Gagarin becoming the first human in space. I coordinated with the folks at Scitech and the Horizon planetarium to run Q&A sessions about Mars One on April 11 for school kids, and managed to get two other Mars One candidates along too – Korum Ellis & Kerry McLeod.

In the planetarium with Korum & Kerry

The event was huge, with literally hundreds of kids coming through to ask us every question imaginable about Mars One. The Q&A was paired with a Horizon “Live” show taking the kids on a virtual trip from Earth to Mars on the planetarium screen, as well as a short iMax film. Everyone had an absolute blast, and we also had a chance to do a special “adults only” session with the Scitech staff, answering all those really curly questions best not raised in front of primary school kids.

I’ve also been busy organising plenty of school visits – the press has started to show a more serious interest since the 1058 astronaut candidates were announced, so I’ve been organising school visits to talk more to Year 6-12 classes about the Mars One mission. I’ve also started offering in-class workshops to teach kids about Leadership and Risk-Management, as well as working with Scitech to organise science/space/comedy events during National Science Week (August 16-24).

There’s also been plenty changes online to fit all this – you’ve probably noticed the new “Speaker” section on the website describing the school programs and corporate presentations I’m doing now. I’ve also been promoting major space events on Twitter and Facebook – things like SpaceX’s recent CRS-3 resupply mission to the International Space Station, and NASA’s #GlobalSelfie project for Earth Day on April 22nd.

Chipping into the #GlobalSelfie project

Even Keith the Anger Management Koala has been contributing, and getting his mischief shared on Twitter by the next crew of the ISS.

And threatening the royal couple in the process…

But really, it’s been mostly waiting. I’ve done what I could, when I could. But every one of the 1058 who passed their medical has been waiting. The April announcement is expected to tell us how many have passed the medical, and what the process for the televised interviews will be, but we don’t know much else yet. How will the interviews be conducted? Where? When? How will people respond to the next step up in media attention? For those of us that have passed we know we’re on the precipice of something incredible, but not how it will unfold.

I’m Steve Zissou the night before setting out to find the Jaguar shark – anxious & focussed, like a coiled spring. I’m right on the edge. I don’t know what comes next… but I know I’ve done everything I can to be ready for it.

Bring on May
