Dear Furrys,

A few recent incidents on social networking have made me realise I need to clarify a few things about my stance towards you all. Many of you may believe I look down on you for dressing up in furry suits and taking on animal personas – this is not true. You’ll also note this post is listed as “Random” rather than the usual “Menstrual Mondays” – it’s because I’d like to take the time now to fully explain the situation to you all, so that are no future misunderstandings.

In late 2010 I performed a 7 minute stand up set in a cramped and ridiculously hot room above a pub in London. The crowd was hot and tired, and it was hardly my finest work – I hadn’t been onstage for months, and my personal life was a total fucking wreck (to put it politely). But during the set I did a bit over a minute on going to high school with a girl who’s now a furry. It was lazy slander about a sub-culture I knew little about, but I liked the idea I could jump off into some absurdist humour with a semi-fictionalised story about high school. The video of the full set is below (furry material between 2:45 and 4:05)

Watch archived video

Needless to say I’d never used any of the furry material before, and haven’t used any of it since – with the exception of the bit about having sex with a crocheted ewok I don’t think it’s particularly funny. But fast forward a few months: I’m setting up a YouTube channel, and start uploading videos I’ve been keeping for years. Not knowing exactly how it all worked, I started favouriting my own videos so they’d show up how I wanted. It wasn’t until a day or so later I discovered YouTube was posting the favourited videos on my personal Facebook page – cue Furry-Girl from high school seeing video’s description, watching it because of the furry reference, and taking mild offense.

When she commented saying I’d “confused (furrys) with fetishists” - I have to admit, I could have handled it better. I could have told her I was being lazy, that I was in a bad mood, or simply that I was sorry. Instead I told her she’d “confused me with someone who gives a shit”. The comments stopped, and a while later one of us (not sure who) un-friended the other from Facebook. Not the end of the world.

Fast track again to just before Christmas 2011: I’d ordered a massive panda costume so I could be Sexual Harassment Panda for my work Christmas party, but it arrives a day late. Instead I wear the panda costume to a friend’s party in London, and make some smartass comment (again, on Facebook) about how the only people at the rail station are a dwarf, a guy muttering to himself, and a ginger weirdo in a panda suit. Several friends comment to make jokes. Cue Furry-Girl seeing comments through mutual friends, making her own comment along the lines of “see, told you dressing up in fur suits is fun”, me responding with “shut up, you’re still a fucking furry”, and it all spiralling out of control.

Things were clearly a little heated on her end – I was just trolling. After a few single line responses designed purely to piss her off further, I simply deleted everything she’d written rather than really read it. Because the honest truth is she’d once again confused me for someone who gives a shit.

I don’t give a damn what any of you do in your spare time. Or at your “fur conventions”. Or whatever the fuck else you people do. And by “you people”, I mean “You people this letter is written for - fucking furrys”. Sure, I did a set making fun of you – saying “all you do is dress up in fur suits and fuck each other” is pretty uninspired stuff. It wasn’t till this recent problem that I actually decided to do the right thing and research furrys properly. Only now have I learnt all the details behind your proud culture and tradition of dressing up in animal suits and fucking each other.

I know you believe it’s an ill-informed stereotype that stems from ignorance about “fur culture” – frankly I think you’ve deserve it. If you don’t, feel free to type “furry” into google image search with safe search OFF. If you’re confident you could show the first 25 images to a 10-year-old without having to explain what BDSM or Nazism are, I promise I’ll start taking you seriously.

No amount of scrubbing will ever get the 2 pictures that google finds BEFORE this off your retina

Furry girl pointed out I have a panda costume. I also have a lemur as a logo for this website, more soft toys and puppets than any guy in his mid 20′s should ever have, and just used a de-motivational picture of a honey badger to emphasise a point. And without giving away too many details, I’ll be spending most of this year’s Edinburgh Fringe Festival in a massive grey koala suit.

But I am nothing like you.

I do these things because I think animals are hilarious, not because I get a kick of pretending to be an anthropomorphic fox.  Most of my puppets are deliberately cute but with terrifying personalities – except Gerald the Baboon, who’s just creepy all round. My panda costume uses the fact that while everyone thinks pandas are adorable, reminding the public that they are actually merciless killing machines is hysterical.

Comedy. Build one expectation, deliver another, get laugh. The Mighty Ginge logo is a ginger lemur wearing a cape - it’s deliberately bizarre (and awesome). I’ve also spent an excessive amount of time in women’s clothing – when I dressed up as a German beer maid, called myself Frueline and flounced around campus to advertise the university Oktoberfest, it didn’t mean I’m part of some sub-culture of beer-maid cross-dressers. I did it because it was hilarious and because I wanted to bend girls over a beer keg and plough them at Oktoberfest while we bothwore dirndls – radically different.

Yup, TOTALLY different

I don’t have to dress up and pretend to be someone else to have a good time though – I like me all the time. I dress up purely because I’m an attention whore, it hides my identity, and I get laughs from breaking expectations. Anyone who has to viciously defend a group/sub-culture they are part of either believes deep down the group isn’t strong enough to ignore criticism, or they need to get a sense of humour – usually both.

So in conclusion, I don’t give a shit what you do. You could be dressed as Dolly the Sheep and get bummed by the PG Tips sock puppet monkey for all I care – just don’t tell me about it. And if you do, don’t be surprised if I judge you for it. I’ve got friends from all walks of life who don’t tell me about their weird interests – so stop trying to talk to me about your fetish for fur and pretending you’re an animal. It’s not funny weird, it’s weird weird. Get a sense of humour, and if you don’t want me to call you a furry in public STOP TELLING ME YOUR A FUCKING FURRY.

Best Regards,

Josh Richards – The Mighty Ginge
