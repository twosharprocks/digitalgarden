It was a crisp, clear morning driving down to the medical centre. Blood tests tend to freak me out a little: I’m fine with needles and blood, but it’s revisiting the memories of having pints drained out of my arms while the Royal Marines were desperately trying to diagnose my Lymes Disease back in 2010 that isn’t pleasant.  This morning felt very different though. Traffic was light, the sun shining, and the radio playing some of the best new music I’d heard for some time. As I pulled up out the front, I saw the centre was already open – earlier than the doctor had said it would be – but I stayed in the car as a live recording of The Smith Street Band finished.

Wandering in, the centre initially looked abandoned. Just a single receptionist manning the front desk and a lady reading in the nearby waiting area. The receptionist asked me to take a number, and unsurprisingly I was the first there for a blood test, so it was number 1. As I sat down however, part of me started to worry about the lady in the waiting area. I was certain she was watching me over her gossip magazine – chewing on a ragged fingernail with a frantic look in her eye, as if she was just seconds away from running screaming out of the centre, away from what ever series of events had led her to sitting in a medical centre at 9am on a Saturday morning.

The moment shattered, as a lilting Irish accent behind me called “Number 1?”. I stood and turned towards her voice, but she was already gone again in a blur of pale green scrubs and… was that red hair? For most of my adult life, red-haired women have always shown up at my most critical turning points. From meeting Serena just weeks after I’d completely committed to finishing my physics degree, to meeting Amanda literally the day I’d realised I’d evolved into an artist; they’ve always shown up when something major is happening. Amanda would also be the driving force behind my trip through the US in 2012, a tour that would introduce me to the very peak of Australian comedy and change my entire perspective on life.

While Amanda may have flaked out on helping me at the 2012 Edinburgh Fringe, Rosie turned up right when I was questioning going there at all. Once up at the Fringe, Laura Levites‘s madness helped keep my own sanity in check, and writing the review of Disney’s “Brave” for Ginger Parrot was a healthy distraction as everything else at the festival fell apart. And straight after it, the  surreal combination of coincidences at the Redhead Days in the Netherlands was the precursor to starting my journey with Mars One. There’s been so many more – there were two the night I decided to perform “Josh Richards – Mars Needs Guitars!” at National Science Week, another 2 the night I arrived back in the Netherlands to perform the show at the Living On Mars Conference, just to name a few.

And while they are all without exception gorgeous women, it’s rare that I’m romantically involved with them any of them – some times they’re there to teach me something, often they’re a catalyst for a change I was already starting to make, but usually they’re just guides. Signposts to simply say “You’re going the right way”.

And now this.

I spotted the bun of vibrant red hair in the second examination room, but checked the first in case I’d dreamt the flash of colour – empty. She was busy checking my paperwork as I walked in, flashed a smile and asked why I was being privately billed. I explained the blood test was for an “overseas job application” and had been told Medicare wouldn’t cover it, but she took my insurance details anyway “just in case”.

The next minute I was in the chair, compression strap around my arm, and the needle was in a vein first go. Sample vial change, and seconds later the second sample finished. “Good veins” she said as the needle came out again. “Only two?” I asked, almost disappointed. “Only testing lipids and cholesterol today, so all done!” as she tore off a tiny piece cotton wool and taped it over the puncture wound.

I thanked her, sorted a few details with the receptionist, then waltzed out the door to my car – my first round of medical testing as an astronaut candidate was done, and fate had thrown another gorgeous red-haired woman into the process as a reminder that I’m going the right way.

*******************

**Update:**Since this was first written last Saturday, I can now proudly tell you that my medical results have come back with a clear pass, they’ve been submitted to Mars One and accepted. So I’m through the initial medical phase, and next up is the candidate interviews & aptitude testing some time in April – can’t wait!
