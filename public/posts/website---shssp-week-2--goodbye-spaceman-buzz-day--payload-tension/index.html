Week 2 of the Southern Hemisphere Space Studies Program got off to a roaring (if early) start, with many of the participants waking up for 5am to watch SpaceX launch the Jason-3 satellite aboard their final Falcon 9 v1.1 rocket (all future launches are the updated 1.2 version).

The launch went perfectly, the satellite deployed cleanly, and the first stage booster landed softly on the drone ship. But then… well…

Watch archived video

Canadian astronaut Dr Bob Thirsk gave his final lecture to the program on “Expeditionary Behaviour” – sharing critical insights into the attitude required to work as an effective team on long-duration spaceflight missions. We couldn’t let our two visiting astronauts leave without gathering our participants together for a group photo though.

Dr Bob Thirsk & Professor Jean-Jacques Favier (holding signed class photos) with SHSSP16

And Icouldn’t let Bob Thirsk escape without getting a photo together… …aaaaaaaand signing one of his glamour shots for me.

With his lectures over Bob Thirsk wrote about his week with SHSSP16, before joining our first participant debate. Participant debates are a staple of all ISU courses, where everyone on the course argue for or against a critical idea relevant to space – the trick being they can’t choose which side they had to argue. Our participants were given the challenge of arguing whether humanity should go direct to Mars or return to the moon first – something our course director John Connolly is deeply interested in.

After a lot of laughs and a lot learnt though, it was time to say goodbye to our visiting astronauts…

Watch archived video

Wednesday was also the birthday for one of the biggest names in space history – Apollo 11 Lunar Module Pilot (and ISU’s Chancellor) Buzz Aldrin!

I also had the chance to help run a couple of workshops this week – working with Robert DeBelle to present ISU’s “Crisis Communications” workshop, as well as organising some team building activities that had some frankly hilarious results…

Building spaghetti & marshmallow towers for team building… just seconds from disaster

Week 2 saw our participants complete their first academic quiz, as well as a huge ramp up in activity on the stratospheric balloon payload, with many of the participants working some very long hours to ensure everything worked during the Australia Day launch. But among the hive of activity they also found some time to embrace some culture, whether it’s learning about space culture with Kerry Dougherty;

Kerrie Dougherty on the how existing culture shapes the way we see space

… or learning about our participant’s culture through our first Culture Night…

Melissa trying to convince us all that Italians are not just stereotypes

…or relaxing over a BBQ dinner & pavlova/lamingtons/tims tams for dessert, all while watching a sunset from a patio with a koala sitting in the front yard’s gum tree.

Arguably the most Australian cultural experience ever

After a full-on Week 2, SHSSP16 seriously has their work cut out for them in Week 3 – a full day at the Institute for Telecommunications Research, the launch of their stratospheric balloon payload on Australia Day, another two culture nights, as well as the white paper project ramping up… all alongside their usual load of lectures from world-class space specialists like Juan De Dalmau (Head of ESA’s ESTEC Communications Office) and Dr Charley Lineweaver (Convener of the Australian National University’s Planetary Science Institute).

It’s going to be one hell of a week… like every week with ISU.
