Let’s be honest here – a lot of people are really quite dull. They work, they eat, they sleep. Occasionally they watch A Current Affair to feel real emotions about how much they’re spending on dish-washing liquid, or if their 3 bedroom home in Ellenbrooke will be safe from ISIS.

Which is a shame, because life can be pretty amazing if you get off your ass, try new things, and stop being so damn scared of making mistakes. Some of you may already think you’re making a mistake reading this – this is entirely possible. “Hey, the title said something about Fringe World – I wonder if he’s got some show suggesti…”

Shut your face, I’m having a moment here.

There’s been a bunch guides recommending different Fringe World shows this last week, and almost all of them have recommended shows that have already sold out or will do soon. Don’t go to them. I’m not saying they’re bad shows – many of them are very, very good. But they don’t need you to see them – they’ve already got plenty of people who follow safe recommendations going, because “Hey, why would you want to see what might be the next big thing, when you can play it safe and see the current big thing?”.

Fringe festivals are supposed to be a chance for the less conventional, the less well-known, and frankly the flat-out weird performers out there to showcase what they do though. It’s not about loading up on more of the same safe crap you already know: “Oooooh, let’s go see him! I loved his TV special on Channel…”.

Seriously, I’m going to ask you to stop reading this if you keep interrupting with your beige-coloured, vanilla-flavoured opinions.

As I spent four years at the Edinburgh fringe and used to regularly dress up as a ukulele-playing koala that screamed at people for a living, I think I’ve got the authority to tell you what is “fringe” and what isn’t. Almost certainly more authority than anyone from the Sunday Times. If anyone from the Sunday Times would like to question that, please get in touch so I can arrange a meeting for you with my alter-ego Keith The Anger Management Koala, and he can politely inform you as to why you’re wrong.

And also explain why Christmas has been cancelled

The Fringe World UV Rating is reasonably accurate for finding weirdness, but there are some VERY notable exceptions. So I’m also going to give you a list of amazing shows to see to avoid being that boring wanker who turns up on Monday morning to work telling everyone he “saw Will & Woody doing stand-up“. Because while they’re nice guys, YOU CAN LISTEN TO THEM EVERY DAMN WEEKDAY MORNING FROM 6.

I’m seeing a lot of shows, but this is the proper fringe stuff I’m going to and you should too. Feel free to push your comfort zone at…

A Young Man Dressed As A Gorilla Dressed As An Old Man Sits In A Rocking Chair For Fifty-Six Minutes And Then Leaves. (Jan 29 – ONE NIGHT ONLY)

This is exactly what it says on the tin. It is also the ULTIMATE fringe experience. It’s so fringe it’s not even listed in the Fringe World guide, nor is it on their website. I saw “A Young Man…” in Edinburgh 2011, and it remains hands down the absolute best show I have ever seen at any festival anywhere in the world. It’s free, and I’ll be rocking up two hours early to make sure I can get in.

I say this without irony, or as part of any kind of trick: Please. For the love of God. See this show.

Mac The Comedy Computer’s Variety 52-Minute Spectacular! (Jan 24-Feb 21)

Ever watched a MacBook sit inanimate on a stage for nearly an hour, while the pre-recorded electronic voice of a sociopath plays over the sound system? It’s hilarious and haunting. Mac is doing his first full fringe show, being joined on stage by Steve the Horribly un-PC PC as well as a variety of the weirdest and most confronting performers at Fringe World. This will probably destroy you.

Dr. Professor Neal Portenza performs his own autopsy live on stage. One night only. (Obviously). (Jan 28-Feb 12)

Whatever lunatic gave this a Fringe World UV Rating of “Medium” clearly didn’t even read the show title, let alone see the show. This show IS also running over multiple nights, because Neal Portenza is a mad man who clearly needs to be stopped.

Roaring Accordion (Feb 11-20)

Mimes cause a rather primal desire for violence to well up inside me, almost as much accordions do. That I’m still keen to see this should be an indication of how highly recommended the show is. I will however remain both skeptical and possibly armed.

Sarah Young’s Magical Murdery Mystery Tour (Feb 11-14)

Sarah Young is a ball of borderline-psychotic energy both on and off stage, who just won the Blue Room’s 2014 Judge’s Award for Best Writing for her play “Giving Up The Ghosts” where two people eat McDonald’s and then kill themselves. She also knows way to much about Big Foot, and will argue he’s real until she has an aneurysm. This show is not about sasquatch – it’s a whimsical show about serial killers. With hand puppets.

Pro-Choyce (Feb 18-21)

This is the full-size poster of Sian Choyce’s first solo show “Pro-Choyce”. Before you go to her show about how much she hates kids (I’ll be in the front row on opening night), you should click the image above and have a really good look her poster. Zoom in and read it. Look that – it’s an excellent poster. It’s also a poster you’re unlikely to see on walls anywhere at the festival, because Fringe World enlarged Sian’s A6 flyer to poster size and distributed THAT all over town instead.

Because it wouldn’t be a true fringe experience unless hard-working and talented performers were overcharged for incompetence.

Jeff Hewitt: V For Vagina (Feb 18-21)

I like Jeff. He’s a fun guy, and undoubtedly one of the best stand-up comedians in Perth. But he was also the kind of comedian that I’d never take a girlfriend to see unless I was trying to break up with her. Until now. From the trial material I’ve seen and the article Jeff recently wrote on why he’s doing a show about women called “V For Vagina”, I’m willing to take the risk.

But Jeff I swear to God, if I bring a date to this and afterwards she never wants to have sex for the rest of her life, you’re coming off the Christmas card list.

BRUCE (Feb 13 & Feb 14)

Bruce is a puppet made from a hacked apart block of foam, and needs two guys to make him talk. Those two guys however happen to be two of the most talented puppeteers in the world, and Bruce is one of the weirdest/funniest things you’ll ever see. Sammy J and Randy are great, but with my artist pass this is one of the only shows I’ll be paying to make sure I can see.

BLUES AND BURLESQUE:HOTTER THAN HELL (Jan 29-Feb 15)

I genuinely don’t get burlesque. Someone took me to see Dita Von Teese and her burlesque showcase in Seattle, and while it was great… I still didn’t get it. The women who do burlesque are unfailingly incredible, but the sexiness is usually all a little too serious for me. So unless it’s something hilariously warped – like a woman awkwardly trying to get out of a bee keeper’s outfit on stage – I’ll usually try to miss it. Maybe I’m just wired wrong.

There’s no sexy, angry bees in Vicious and Scarlett’s show, but they do have a hysterically warped sense of humour however, and Pete Saunders is absolutely exceptional providing the bluesy accompaniment.

If you enjoy regular burlesque shows, then Things That Go Bump…and Grind in the Night is definitely the place to be – all of the most entertaining burlesque dancers I know will be on, if they’re not actually behind the scenes running it.

Tomás Ford Is The End Of The Fringe (Feb 14 & Feb 21)

Everyone’s favourite, creepy cabaret fringe uncle/urban legend is back – bigger, badder, bulgier than ever. Besides coordinating all of the Noodle Palace shows and running the Late Night Gala, Tomás is also playing a valentine’s day mega show as well as ENDING THE ENTIRE FRINGE FESTIVAL with Tomás Ford Is The End Of The Fringe.

Keith the Anger Management Koala will be playing both the Valentines Day and the End of Fringe shows for Tomás, as well as a raft of other gigs throughout the whole fringe that I’ll put on the “Upcoming” page shortly. In the meantime go out, step beyond your comfort zone, and support some incredible talent by witnessing their abject on-stage madness.
