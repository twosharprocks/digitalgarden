Mini Essay - If Mars One rebooted

For some time you’ve been aware that the shutdown of Mars One in 2020 has left you very adrift and directionless in the world, and one of the questions that’s come up repeatedly is what you’d do if Mars One suddenly rebooted. Bas floated the idea when the Ridley Scott documentary was first being discussed, but we’ve heard nothing on that for well beyond the 12 month exclusivity contract. But the question remains - what would you do if Mars One rebooted tomorrow?

The most obvious thing is you’d be looking to shed all your gear again. Anything excess or outstanding would be immediately trimmed out - physically, emotionally, mentally, and probably spiritually too. So what would that look like?

Physically I’d be selling the compressor immediately, and everything in the storage unit would be getting a very hard look to see if it’s worth keeping. I’d go through all my clothes and figure out what I really want to keep, what I want to replace, and then ditch anything that doesn’t suit. I’d be stocktaking everything I have in the house and asking really tough questions about why I have it. I’ve already gone through the car, but I’d be asking if I keep it either. Physically I’d also be trying to shed weight and get up to a substantial level of fitness too, because I’d have a substantial motivation to push on when it’d be easier to quit or treat myself. I’d be driving hard every single day towards that goal, and that would be all I’d need to be a whole lot fitter and lighter.

Mentally is probably where the biggest shift would occur, because I’d be slipping back into that highly optimised backpack lifestyle, ready to travel and speak about Mars One all over again. I’d want to read all the book on my bookshelf and then give them away, and I’d be reading everything I could find in my digital library that might help me with preparation for Mars One selection. Cave diving would immediately drop to the background, because as much as I love it there is nothing that compares with a one-way mission to Mars. Interestingly though, I know how vital it’d be to write out all the notes and mini essays I already have planned, because I’d want to cut all my attachments and clear the decks as much as possible so I’d be approaching selection as cleanly as possible. So I’d still be grateful for all the time I have coming up, and I’d be pushing to write as much as possible so that I had my emotions and mental state as clear as possible.

The emotional impact is very hard to predict, and I suspect it would follow the actions I’d take in the physical and mental realm when I got news that Mars One was rebooting. The response from my partner is really hard to predict - I know she’d be supportive in her way, but Ialso know she’d hate the changes that would follow. I’ve know for some time that I shouldn’t (and really can’t afford) live in fear of upsetting her, but I also know that Mars is bigger than her, bigger than our relationship, and bigger than any of the relationships I have. So I know I’d do what I needed to do to be emotionally ready for selection, regardless of the consequences. It sounds brutal, but the opportunity to be part of a Mars mission is bigger than any one person.

The one area where I’m certain the impact of a Mars One reboot would be immediately and undeniably positive is in my spiritual health. This is the one area that has lapsed most in the last 5 years, and my trip to Antarctica is the first time since 2020 that I’ve felt my spirit really ignite. I don’t want the rest of my life to be simply pursuing different experiences and writing about them - I want to feel unbridled joy seeing, doing, and creating extraordinary things. Paddling away from that leopard seal, I knew I’d had one of the most incredible experiences of my life so far, and I know that the life I’m currently living really lacks that. Discovering new underwater caves are one example from the last 5 years, but I can’t think of many others and certainly not the way I’d feel getting an update from Mars One. Every morning I’d wake up hopeful for an email, and while that anticipation was exhausting over time it was still an incredible rush when I did see an official Mars One email arrive with an update.

So what would I do if Mars One rebooted? I’d cut back a lot of the things that have crept into my life over the last 5 years that I don’t like. I’d regain a clear sense of purpose, and I’d be absolutely brutal in my dealing with people because I’d know I was on a mission that was bigger than any of us. And the next question obviously becomes “Why not live my life like that anyway?”. You asked yourself the question of what you’d do if you had 10 years left on Earth, and you took a potential Mars mission and stretched it into a question around mortality.

So why not ask yourself what you’d do if you knew you were going to die in the next 10 years? Because you might. What would you regret if you were paliating in hospital tomorrow? Would you wish you’d done the safe thing? Or would you want to go out doing something that boring people would say “He deserved it”? You’ve always joked about that, but it’s never really been a joke at all - I’d much rather die doing something fucking awesome but stupid than die in an old and broken body. Life is for living, and it’s not the years in your life but the life in your years that counts. So go and do extraordinary things. Make trouble doing the right thing, and die because you deserved it not because you were old or unlucky. Mars One gave you the focus you needed to see life that way, but there’s no reason why you can’t live life to the fullest now that the prospect of being sent to another plant has become more remote. Every day you must remember death, and sleep in a coffin incase you don’t wake up.
