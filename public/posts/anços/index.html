The Anços River rises from the interior of the Sicó massif in a marginal area. It is a Vauclian spring, in the northern sector of the west western Meso-Cenozoic border. With an estimated annual flow rate of 50 Mm3, this is one of the most important springs for the hydrological functioning of the Dogger limestones in this massif. Although the spring is permanent, you can only dive there during the dry season. It is possible to cover 400mts of galleries and a difference of -63mts. In the rainy season, it is impossible to penetrate the system at the source due to the strong current and flow rate. (Luís, R. 2018) – Spot description by Rui Luis

WATER TEMPERATURE: 12º-18º DIVE SITE: Cave (spring water)

  • CaveAtlas.com: Easy access, you park your car on the main road just 20 or so meters from the entrance. No entrance fee.

This cave is entered through a tight, shallow and snaky section. Dropping through a well into the main tunnel it starts opening up and the initial flow from the entrance is very much reduced. The walls are lighter in color than the Alviela or Almonda, and drop down steeply to a restriction at 30m and then drops further down to its explored limit at 80m (João Neves, 2007).
