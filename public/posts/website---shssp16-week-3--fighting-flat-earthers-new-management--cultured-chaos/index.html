A new week – a new venue! Week 3 of SHSSP16 kicked off away from the usual Mawson Centre lecture theatre, and instead we led the participants over to the Institute for Telecommunication Research (ITR). Professor Bill Cowley (head of ITR’s academic staff) has been working with SHSSP16’s stratospheric balloon payload team since the start of the program, but now had the chance to formally discuss the challenges of data & spectrum management in space.

Electromagnetic wave physics, but not as we know it – I honestly don’t understand half of what Bill says

After a full day of having their minds blown at ITR, the course were invited to a closed-session on the future of Australia in Space that evening chaired by Co-Director Michael Davis. Alongside Jeff Kasparian, Peter Nikoloff, and Alex Senata from the Australian Space Policy Unit; there was a very frank discussion about the future of Australian space activities & government policy. Nerves got a little frayed, but with course’s stratospheric balloon launch the next morning everyone was understandably pretty stressed.

Not that they had anything to worry about. Our stratospheric balloon launch on Australia Day was a complete success, which was surely down to the meticulous planning that went into it.

Serious Balloon Planning Is Serious

The morning of the launch there was tension as the payload team were forced to make some last-minute changes…

“I’m not freaking out – YOU’RE FREAKING OUT!”

…and the webcast team were triple checking that everything was plugged in where it was meant to be.

The files are INSIDE the computer you say?

It was still organised chaos as the balloon started to fill and the launch window approached.

SPOM waiting for this entire thing to go horribly, horribly wrong

And even after the payload and webcast were fixed we thought we’d lose the balloon when the wind started to gust, and the balloon handlers started planning a “running release”.

“I’ll just pretend there’s a Big Mac on the other side of the oval and let go halfway across!”

Just as the launch window opened the wind dropped to zero though, and we had a perfectly clean release – the entire course cheering as our balloon and payload sailed into the sky!

Left – BRRRRRRUUUUUUUUCCCCCE!!! Right – The stratospheric balloon being chased by our drone

Then the media interviews began…

Left – Co Director Michael Davis. Center – Course Director John Connolly. Right – Participant Dominic Hardy

Watch archived video

While the recovery team started tracking the rapidly ascending balloon, the rest of the participants loaded onto the coach and headed to a winery… for a GPS exercise.

What do you mean we have to “collect data” before wine tasting?!

With live updates coming from the balloon tracking team, it wasn’t long before we got word it had burst at a maximum altitude of 36.4km and was returning to Earth under a parachute. The recovery teams tracked it’s decent through the bush for hours, before finding the payload intact on the ground (and not strung up in a tree).

Lisa’s visible relief at having this polystyrene monster back on planet Earth

Which meant we could recover photos from the payload like this…

So while Neil DeGrasse Tyson & B.O.B fought over the Earth being flat, we sent a balloon up to 36.4km to check

Which instantly brought out the flat-earth brigade on Instagram…

This is why aliens fly straight passed our planet…

But while the recovery team were bashing through bush land looking for a polystyrene box, and the flat-Earthers started having a meltdown; the rest of SHSSP16 went to Cleland Wilflife Park to hang out with koalas…

After being ripped from his favourite eucalyptus tree, Gary the koala was just one hug away from becoming a drop bear…

… and to see if someone’s kid would fall into an enclosure and be eaten by a Tasmanian Devil.

We were all cheering for the devil

After a HUGE day though we all headed back to Mawson Lakes for a BBQ and the Australian Day culture night. The university had lost all power while we’d been gone, but our illustrious Aussies pushed on with their culture night anyway, and still managed to include aboriginal art, eating vegemite sandwiches, and playing Goon of Fortune despite the power outage.

With the balloon launch now complete though, the course started to shift their focus on to the food & water security white paper they had just 2 weeks left to produce. Between lectures from Juan De Dalmau (Head of ESTEC Communications), Will Reid (Australian Center for Field Robotics), and Charley Lineweaver this week; the participants have started to really get their teeth into the structure and content of what will be the program’s final product.

With stress over the white paper and the final 4 exam building though, we held a particularly tense class meeting on Friday to resolve some of the participant concerns. After a tense few hours the course elected a new project management team to keep things on track, and between the teaching associates and the participants we found a way to deal with many of the issues. Things like this are never pleasant, but ISU is all about overcoming challenges to do something extraordinary – SHSSP16 certainly overcame one of their biggest hurdles of the entire course that day, and should be exceptionally proud of themselves for doing it relatively painlessly.

With a renewed sense of direction and purpose though, a sense of relief washed over everyone – staff and participants alike. Which is just as well, because after the management change we went pretty much straight into the Canadian and Chinese culture night! Needless to say the whole course had a lot of steam to blow off, so the less said about Friday night the better…

Lets just say there was poutine, pandas, and one very unfortunate salmon…

Looking ahead to week 4 we’re starting to get down to the pointy end of the course: we’ll see the end of lectures, the final exam, and then almost complete focus on getting the white paper deliverables done. I say almost, because among all this our SHSSP16 course director John Connolly will be sharing his life work of designing missions to Mars, the world’s only space ethicist Jacques Arnould will share hislife’s work, and then on Thursday night I’ll be on a panel about Space and Popular Culture where I’ll be sharing the ridiculous series of events that led me to signing up for a one-way mission to Mars and writing comedy shows about it.

We’ll probably discuss why this appeared on breakfast television too…

Bring on the penultimate week of SHSSP16!
