Algeria

#caving  #diving 

About

Algeria features a diverse landscape that includes mountain ranges, vast deserts, and karstic plateaus, making it highly relevant from a speleological and geological perspective.

🪨 Limestone Karst in Algeria

Yes, Algeria has significant limestone karst formations, especially in the northern and northeastern regions of the country. These include:

  • Djurdjura Mountains (part of the Tell Atlas): This region has well-developed karst plateaus with numerous caves and sinkholes, formed in Jurassic and Cretaceous limestones.
  • **Constantine and Setif regions**: Known for complex karst systems and underground hydrology.
  • Tassili n’Ajjer (southeastern Algeria - DO NOT TRAVEL): While primarily known for its sandstone formations and prehistoric rock art, some karst-like erosion features occur in isolated carbonate outcrops.

Allendale - 5L11

#diving  #caving  #mount-gambier 

From CaveDB

Known as the ‘hole in the middle of the road’, Allendale is one the of the first CDAA Cave Rated sites that divers have access in the earlier steps of their cave training. The cave consists of a main straight tunnel on a 45 degrees slope reaching 26m depth at the bottom and a total 60m length (from surface to deepest part). A small surface lake can host divers comfortably with water level usually at below waist level. The Northwest corner of the lake will grant access to the cave’s main tunnel which is reasonably large until you reach 12m depth after a 25m swim. Attentive divers will notice on their right hand side that some sort of collapse happened there. The main tunnel branches East at the 12 m restriction, and one can navigate through a narrow passage until hitting a full stop after a roughly 15 metres swim. Back to the main tunnel and once cleared the 12m depth mark, divers will face a large room spanning 30m in length (to the deepest part of the cave) and 20m in width. Behind the black silt pile on the Northwest corner a fissure on the rock has been object of many divers work. She doesn’t go, yet…

Allendale East Cave – Soggy Wombats

#diving  #caving 

Allendale East Cave

Introduction

Known as the hole in the middle of the road or just ‘the Dale’, Allendale East Cave is a very popular site among the CDAA Cave rated divers in the Mount Gambier region. As it presents itself as a non-complex cave to dive in, the Soggy Wombats Team has been training, developing skills and accumulating experience in this cave since 2019. Although geographically far from the most developed cave systems in the region, ‘the Dale’ has a small ‘tunnel’ at it’s deepest end that has been in most of Australian’s Cave Divers dreams to break into something big. There hasn’t been any breakthrough in this cave in the last 20 years or so, but with the advances in exploration techniques, we might be surprised with what the future will tell us.
