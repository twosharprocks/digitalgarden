Cyprus

#diving 

Sub-regions

Caves

About

Geological Context

  • Large parts of western and southern Cyprus, especially the Akamas Peninsula, Paphos District, and Troodos foothills, are composed of:

    • Limestone
    • Chalk
    • Marl and dolomite

  • Karst processes have created solutional caves, sinkholes, and underground streams, though most documented caves are dry or semi-dry.

Known Cave Systems

  • Choirokoitia Cave, Ayios Georgios Cave, and Moutti tou Avak are known dry caves.
  • Sea caves (e.g. in Cape Greco and Akamas) are abundant, formed in soft limestone and often accessible by snorkeling or shallow diving.
  • Submerged inland cave systems are not well-documented, but there are multiple springs and karstic aquifers that hint at the possibility of underwater conduits or sumps.

Cave Diving Status

  • There is no significant record of cave diving exploration in Cyprus’s inland karst.
  • Some technical diving occurs in marine environments like the Zenobia wreck, but not in caves.
  • The lack of documentation may indicate unexplored opportunities, especially in springs and limestone basins inland.

Potential for Exploration

  • Unexplored resurgences or aquifer-fed springs in limestone zones may host short submerged systems.
  • Winter and spring seasons may reveal temporarily flooded caves or sumps suitable for reconnaissance dives.
  • There’s also potential for sea-level phreatic caves along limestone cliffs to extend underwater.

Challenges

  • Limited speleological mapping outside known dry caves.
  • Land access and conservation restrictions in some areas (especially Akamas Peninsula).
  • Water quality may vary depending on season and agricultural runoff.

Verdict: Moderate Potential
