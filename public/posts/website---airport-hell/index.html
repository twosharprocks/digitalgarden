While I was waiting to board my flight from Cape Town to Addis Ababa a few weeks back, a strange thing kept happening on the gate information screen: in clear white letters was “Addis Ababa” with the correct gate and boarding time, but every now and then “Addis Ababa” would change into “Gaborone” and go a dark yellow colour. All the other flight details matched up though, and I boarded the flight with no issues, so I just put it down to being an alternative name for Addis Ababa.

It wasn’t. It’s the capital of Botswana. They flew us to Botswana. Do you know where Botswana is Because I sure as hell didn’t til I landed there.

This looks… inviting?

Turns out we needed to stop and refuel to make the distance to Addis Ababa, but Ethiopian Airlines figured we didn’t really need to know this until we were taxiing to the runway. To be honest stopping for an hour to refuel wasn’t really an issue for most of the passengers, but it was a fairly major issue for me, because I sat on a window just behind the wing & had to watch the plane being refueled by the loosest man alive – hence forth known as “LMA”.

Picture this: The fuel truck drove up, LMA leaps out and places a rickety wooden ladder under the wing. He runs back, grabs a wire off a reel and connects it to the plane’s wing as an earth. Then he wanders back, grabs the fuel line, drags it over to the rickety wooden ladder, connects it to the wing, then wanders back to the tanker and turns the pump on.

All pretty standard stuff, except for the bit where LMA then sits next to the pump pushing hundreds of liters of jet fuel a minute and answers a couple of calls on his handheld radio, makes a couple of calls on his mobile, and then because he’s bored decides to have a smoke…

He’s facing away from us because cool guys don’t watch explosions

LMA managed to refuel the plane without incinerating himself or the ~150 people on-board, and we left to begin a whole new circus in Addis Ababa. The only person I know who’s spent any time in Ethiopia is an old girlfriend I’ve only just started speaking to again (honestly, 5 years almost felt too soon… but we’re okay now), so a few weeks before the flight I asked her if it was worth leaving the airport. She warned me that Bole International Airport was a “hellhole” and kindly organised for one of her old teaching buddies to pick me up and take me out for dinner….

…which would have been amazing if I hadn’t arrived to discover Ethiopian immigration only let people out of the airport if their stopover is more than 8 hours – mine was 7 & a half.

We’d arrived half an hour early (probably because of LMA’s efficient refueling), so after braving one of the toilets I rushed around trying to find wifi to message the ex’s friend and say I couldn’t get out of the airport. Turns out the “Free Wifi” at Bole International is only “free” if you know the wifi password… which you can only get if you pay one of the dodgy shop owners. So after paying $10US to use an internet cafe that wasn’t connected to the internet (“no refund!”), and another $10US to have a shop owner type the wifi password into my phone (“you no look at code!” as she grabbed my phone and turned away from me), I managed to get through the ex’s friend to say I wouldn’t be able to leave the airport, just as she was about to leave her house to pick me up.

I’d had a fairly big night fueled by nachos and margarita-slushies in Cape Town before I left, and there’d been next to nothing to drink on the flight, so with “Escape from Bole” aborted the next challenge was to try to find water. This would have been easy if I could drink what was leaking from the roof, but “Drink Ethiopian airport roof water” isn’t on my “101 Things To Do Before I Die” list.

This is just like the “Free Wifi”, except the buckets are advertised as “Shower Facilities”

I couldn’t even pay to use one of the Ethiopian Airline Lounges (“Are you business or first class Then no”) so after a futile search for water fountains I just curled up in a ball on one of lounges, clutching all my worldly possession to my chest for 6 hours and dreaming of bottled water.

Welcome to WHAT?

After some next-level Orwellian insanity at the boarding gate (3 x-ray luggage scans without leaving the security area of the airport) at 1am local time, I was back on my way to the UK. It’s not that I hated every second at Bole International Airport… it’s just that if I ever go there again in this lifetime it will be too soon.

Accurate photo of me flying out of Addis Ababa

Back on-board things were a little more sane – Ethiopian Airlines isn’t great, but atleast this flight the sound to my entertainment system worked (as opposed to my flight into Addis Ababa where I watched “Edge of Tomorrow” on mute) and there was water: I think I went through half a dozen of those little bottles of water before they cut me off.

I landed at Heathrow around 7:30am, walked straight off the plane to Customs & Immigration (joys of being a registered traveller with no checked luggage) was on the Heathrow Express by 8:00, Paddington station by 8:15, and walked into the Space Innovation Congress (SIC) in St Paul’s at just after 9:00. Because apparently I work on ISU time all the time now.

SIC wasn’t really aimed at me (more for space entrepreneurs trying to start commercial space ventures) but it was fantastic catching up with Dr Alice Gorman over from Adelaide to talk about cultural heritage in space, as well as finally meeting Professor Chris Welch (who run’s ISU’s Masters Program) after emailing & tweeting the last 2 years in since I finished SHSSP14. There were claims of Buzz Aldrin making an appearance that never materialised, and I knew there was an astronaut panel organised after lunch… but it was still a fantastic surprise to see Dr Bob Thirsk again! I’d only met Bob in January at SHSSP16, but that already feels like a lifetime ago.

I’ve been operating my phone without a SIM card since I left Australia (carrier doesn’t allow roaming), so anywhere with wifi has become an oasis in a communications desert. The conference had solid wifi, so I ducked out of the presentations a little early to place a couple of VoIP calls to my boss for SSP16 and some TV folks who want to talk science & comedy when I get to LA in mid-June.

Didn’t have long to chat though, because I needed to get out to Luton airport to meet an ex-girlfriend (Eli) who was flying in from Amsterdam. So I jumped on the nearest tube and bolted out to Luton… to discover you can’t use an Oyster card out to Luton – you have to buy a separate ticket. Just as well there were two inspectors waiting by the gate to issue fines, because it’s almost as if this is something that people do accidentally all the time…

After waiting for Eli at Luton (still no wifi, but atleast the roof wasn’t leaking like Bole International) we headed back into London… when I realised in amongst fighting train ticket inspectors and picking up an ex from the airport, I’d missed one of the most historic moments in the modern space age…

I’m retroactively making this the reason we broke up

With all the chaos going on I’d completely blanked on the fact that SpaceX were launching CRS8. But even if had remembered, there’s no way I could have watched it travelling through the wifi desert of Luton bus/train transfers. Still, mucho disappointo.

After a night in a dingy hostel in London (because I’d somehow forgotten just how generally awful London hostels are) we met up with old comedy friends Robbotron & Jo Jo Bellini for breakfast, then Eli & I headed down to Brighton with Megan – who’s garage I was living in when I found out about Mars One. First stop The best Mexican in the known universe…

Burrito fun times at La Choza with Eli & Meg

By the time we got home I was reaching burn out – the reality is I hadn’t truly stopped in the 3 months since SHSSP16 started, I’ve been carrying the ghost of a throat infection for the last 2 months, and emotionally really just needed to not see or speak to anyone for a solid few days. We struck a compromise by staying in so I could curl up on the couch, I introduced Eli & Meg to Rick & Morty, and Meg went and picked up fish & chips from down the road for dinner. Eli & Meg stayed up drinking wine and chatting, while I passed out on the couch in the lounge.

No no – just leave me here

At about 3am something stirred me, but I just lay there being angry at the world for disturbing my slumber – I found out the next morning someone was killed 50m down the road around that time, when a bar fight spilled out into the road and in front of a truck. But to be honest that was the last thing on my mind, because as I rolled over to go back to sleep my stomach suddenly turned… then turned again… and about 12 seconds later I was diving for a sink as the fish and chips made a surprise return to the world.

Food poisoning is a barrel of laughs if you’ve ever had it – fantastic way to lose weight. After hurling my body weight up, I managed to sleep through till about midday and also managed to pull off “being a pathetic mess” for the rest of the day too. So while Eli & Meg embraced the pancake love, I sipped on a flat coke. We ended up staying in again and I only started to get part of my appetite back nibbling on a pizza and watching the LEGO movie.

After the great purging of April 9th, atleast I woke up the next morning not wanting to die. Eli & I headed back to London, she flew back out to Amsterdam (after a weekend for us to catch up where I’d spent 3/4 of the time being a tragic, miserable asshole) and I headed out to dinner with a friend and to see the UK premiere of “Last Man On the Moon”.

Eugene Cernan – Gemini 9 pilot, Apollo 10 Lunar Module Pilot, and Apollo 17 commander – was literally the last person to stand on the Moon, and “Last Man On The Moon” is a documentary based on Cernan’s autobiography that’s been getting a lot of traction in the US. Cernan had been supposed to conduct a Q&A after the documentary screening, but health issues prevented him making the flight to London for the UK premiere.

The documentary was… I don’t know. It’s hard to be disappointed with a documentary about the last human to have walked on another world. But I really struggled, and the non-space friend who came with me was less than polite about it. There were some truly memorable & emotional parts (the interview with Roger Chaffee’s widow about the Apollo 1 fire comes to mind) and I’d still recommend people see it, but by the time we got to Cernan going to the Moon with Apollo 17 I was… bored. And to be honest it felt like it kind of ruined the otherwise great night I had with an old friend.

The next night in London was definitely an improvement though, because I was getting to meet 3 more of the Mars 100 had come all the way down from Durham, with Ryan MacDonald joining us from Cambridge and Clare Weedon too.

L-R: Ryan, some ginger skinhead, Hannah, Clare

It was incredible meeting Hannah, Clare & Ryan – besides it being a great get-together of folks all dedicated to the human colonisation of Mars, it’s also been fascinating seeing how my preconceived ideas about different candidates (gleaned from watching & reading their interviews) marry up with what they’re actually like in person. I’m sure given how many ridiculous interviews I’ve done over the last few years I’m sure many of the Mars 100 have a fairly skewed image of what I’m like, but on a selfish note it’s been really interesting to have those some of my preconceptions of the others challenged (and some confirmed) by meeting in person. I’m really grateful for the flexibility in this trip to meet as many of them as I can.

London town grows pretty old if you’ve lived their before though, so the next day it was time to head out to Bristol. It’s clearly been awhile since I took a train around the UK, because this one had on-board wifi all fancy like, and apparently it’s standard on a lot of the lines now. Getting into Bristol I had enough time to walk from the station to the hostel, drop my bags at the hostel in town, then walk back to the station and head back to Bath for dinner with some old friends from my time working for Damien Hirst! 100% worth it though – Vicky has always been my pseudo-sister in the UK, her boyfriend Olly is a fantastic guy, and the three of us had a lot to catch up on. The take home line for the night came from Vicky: “Josh, next time you’re planning on changing your whole life for some woman, just call me – I’ll tell you not to”.

I never listen though…

There were a half dozen people I could have caught up with in Bristol, but given I’d been bed-ridden by food poisoning a few days earlier I figured a rest day might be in order (sorry Ollie & Roxie). I managed to catch up with one other friend from Damien Hirst – Hugh, our carpenter who was one of the guys I worked closest with – but otherwise most of the day was spent trying to find a decent place for breakfast, writing, and booking accommodation for the rest of the trip.

As you’ve probably figured by now, there’s a fairly serious mis-match at the moment between my instagram feed (which shows where I am in the world at the same time I’m there) and these posts (this one’s about a week behind). Doing things is great, but catching up with people has certainly taken it out of me the last few months. Atleast the “bat shit crazy” time schedule has eased to “hectic” now though, so I’m starting to catch up.

Next stop was Dublin to catch up with good friend, science-communicator extraordinaire and ISU-buddy Niamh Shaw and her hilarious partner Peter (he ought to be funny, the man’s been a comedian/MC forever). There wasn’t much time to chat to Niamh & Peter though, because the mighty Barry was driving from Derry to pick me up for so we could wreak havoc on Cork for the weekend!

Two of Cleo’s 2016 Most Eligible Bachelors

We had grand intentions for our Saturday night in Cork, but the reality is after the best pizzas in the region at the Franciscan Well Barry and I kind of stumbled around a bit like lost sheep. We ended up befriending a couple of German girls who looked interesting but equally lost, found our way to Crane Lane (which I remembered from my last visit), mingled about, and then just kind of bailed: everywhere was wall-to-wall with people, and against our initial hopes of a big night I don’t think Barry or I were really feeling it.

Cork is also home to ISU’s 2017 Summer Space Program too, so Barry and I went and caused mischief for a morning at Blackrock Castle – which is playing a major role in SSP17 with it’s observatory and space science outreach program.

And spotted Niamh’s “To Space” show poster behind the front desk!

After Blackrock the only things left were to meet up for a coffee with another friend (which I may write about separately some other time), and drive back to Dublin! I waved goodbye to the mighty Barry from Niamh’s place, and spent the next few days trying to get my life in order by booking more flights, catching up on sleep, and getting some much missed running in – if I don’t get to stretch my legs every few days I get… weird.

Before you ask, yes – weirder than usual

Besides having some incredible evening chats with Niamh about her SSP15 experience which will directly affect the way I operate as a Teaching Associate on SSP16 in Haifa; the only thing of serious note from my days in Dublin was meeting Joseph Roche. Joe is a former Mars One candidate who earned a certain notoriety in early 2015 when he was quoted in a series of highly critical articles written by a journalist I know quite well. As an assistant professor in STEM education at Dublin’s Trinity College though, I’d wanted to meet Joe well before “Rochegate” (as it became known), and have always questioned how much was said “by Joe” was actually him, and how much was the journalist twisting what he was saying to suit her very clear agenda against Mars One (and space exploration generally).

I’d read Joe’s article in the Guardian which seemed to carry a much more reasonable message, but again it was important to meet him in person to get the real story. And the reality is he’s an absolute gentleman, a huge proponent of exploring and colonising Mars, but as time went on he decided Mars One didn’t have what it takes to succeed – and that is entirely okay. Where it all went wrong for him was expressing that to a journalist who had zero qualms twisting what he was saying into articles with titles like “Mars One Finalist Explains Exactly How It’s Ripping Off It’s Supporters” (I’m not linking it – look it up yourself if you must).

So really, the only one who “won” out of the entire episode was the journalist, and she’s not worth saying anything about anymore…

…but Joseph Roche is a great guy, and deserved to be treated a lot better by the world’s media than he was.

Overall though, it was great being able to stop for a good few days somewhere safe to rest properly (thank you so much again Niamh & Peter for welcoming me into your home) and time/headspace to plan the rest of my travels… including a last minute detour to Iceland that I’ll write about in the next post 😉
