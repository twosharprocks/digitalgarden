Mini Essay - Why I’m writing a book about cave diving

#mini-essay  #writing 

Writing a book about cave diving isn’t just the “right thing to do”, it’s also something I genuinely love the idea of doing. Writing is a huge part of my life and how I process things over time, and Diving is equally important to me having been part of my life for nearly 30 of my 40 years now. Cave diving is something that I’m fiercely passionate about and incredibly invested in, so it makes sense that I’d be drawn to the idea of combining these interests into writing a book about cave diving, especially if I put it in the context of my first 500 cave dives.

Beyond the most obvious elements though, I know that writing a book about cave diving is important to both the sport and myself. I’m a good and engaging writer, and I know deep down that writing is where I excel - the artform that I know I’m strongest at, where I can make the greatest impact. Likewise, I am an exceptional cave diver with a natural instinct for finding new tunnel where others couldn’t. Discovering and sharing new tunnel with people, being able to show non-divers why we do the dives we do, and embracing that raw sense of exploration all help me be a better version of myself.

There’s also no doubt that a lot has happened in the last 6 years too, as much above water as below. Right now I have a lot of strong feelings about the Australian cave diving community and the way I’ve been treated by specific parts of it over the last couple of years. There’s no question I’m angry and hurt about some of it, but that’s not my motivation for writing this book - if it were, I’d have thrown the idea out months ago. But I know this is a worthwhile project to pursue, specifically because I’m worried that my anger over the vindictive and petty politics will spill out into a book that might encourage others to engage with cave diving. I genuinely love this sport, and the last thing I’d want would be to write something that put people off it because of the old-boy politics that goes on.

I have numerous things that I could write, and probably plenty of other things that in some ways I should write before a book on cave diving… but I know this is the right thing to do. If I were to switch my daily Mini Essays over to writing about various dives I’ve done, I have no doubt I’d be able to create this book in a matter of months. Better yet, I’d love to turn my daily Mini Essays into a project to write up the 101 Things that I’ve completed, and then allocate a separate hour each day to writing about my 500 cave dives.

Part of me worries that I’ll need more time to write these things than I’m currently willing to give, or that I won’t write them properly because I’m trying to make them fit into the #mini-essay format. But those are both poor excuses for procrastination - ultimately this is just about showing up and writing the next thing on the list. The Book - 101 Things will be incredibly straight forward because you just need to write the next entry on the 101 Things - Complete List list. Likewise with your cave diving book, you just need to write about the next dive. That may mean putting together a list of your cave diving experiences so you can simply pick the next entry and start writing about it, but creating that list should be fairly straight-forward (especially if you add “Make a list of cave dives” to your Tasks).

So why am I writing a book about cave diving? Because I have some incredible experiences to share and stories to tell - I saw that first hand talking to Mitch and Keith at the bottom of Murra El Elevyn when I was causally dropping stories while sorting out gear that was blowing the minds of them both. I’m writing this book because people are interested in what I do, the cave diving stories are particularly great, and I love sharing experiences with people who are interested.

As I write this book, just remember that “interesting” is the key. My goal with Cosmic Nomad was to make it as funny as possible, but that’s not the aim this time around. In the wise words of Ivor Dembina, “You don’t always have to be funny, but you do have to be interesting.”
