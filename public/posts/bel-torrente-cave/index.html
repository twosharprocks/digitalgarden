Additional Notes#

The entrance is richly decorated with oysters and mussels. Hydrogen sulfide released by posidonia clouds the water. Polished limestone rounds off the mystical cavern environment. At the 50meter mark we ascend to the surface to explore further and admire huge formations and grand chambers.

MAX. DEPTH: 12m

MAX. PENETRATION: 60 Meters

MAX. MEMBERS: 4 Divers

CAVERN LINE? None present

NECESSARY EQUIPMENT: Scuba unit Primary and back up torch

STUDENT PRE-REQUISITES: Open Water Diver Certification

DURATION OF BOAT RIDE: 18 Minutes

  • Protec Sardinia - Cave: https://protecsardinia.com/bel-torrente/?lang=en

The entrance is richly decorated with oysters and mussels. Hydrogen sulfide released by posidonia clouds the water. Polished limestone rounds off the mystical cavern environment.

There are several changes in salinity marked by haloclines, changes in the rock formations, and increased visibility. Once you reach the fresh water, huge halls appear in the beam of your primary torch and the cave takes on its full form.

It as a great ride for scooters.

EQUIPMENT CONFIGURATION: Backmount und Sidemount

GASES: Compressed air and nitrox

EXPLORATION: New routes continue to be found and new line continues to be laid.

  • Cave Atlas: A wonderful cave system, the inner walls of the entrance are covered with oysters and small mussels. Large rocks lay in front of the entrance, one dives over them and right after you recognise the large entrée with a diameter of approximately 4 meters. Some 20 minutes after placing the primary reel in open water we reach the main line on the right side of the cave and we just follow it further inside the system. The water is a bit milky in the beginning, sea grass grows on the bottom and eroded lime stones complete the picture to a somewhat mystic environment.

Soon you will see fresh water flows out of the cave and after a short while so called haloclines start to form, a blurry mix of fresh and salt water. After 25 minutes we dive over a small rock formation and glide in a tunnel leading to a depth of 4 to 5 meters. One is now in fresh water only, the view is magnificent and the visibility is crystal clear. From this point the actual cave starts to appear, Enormous halls caught in the glow of your primary light and the cave shows its full shape. One can recognise how old these systems actually are by the different shapes of stone and stalactites and they tell you a bit of the evolution this system has gone through. After some 40 minutes the cave continues to go deeper, up to about 21 meters and continues to go further and further.

Equipment configuration: Back mount and Sidemount for the Sumps.

  • SKEP: https://protecsardinia.com/project-bel-torrente/

The mountain massif called “Supramonte” is located on the east coast of Sardinia. With a summit height of 1463 m and an extension of approx. 50 square km the largest limestone area in Sardinia. Due to the strong karstification of this region, all surface water disappears in rugged mountain gorges to finally collect in the numerous underground cave systems and exit again in karst springs or flow into the sea. The “Bel Torrente” cave is part of this subterranean system and it can be assumed that, due to its enormous size, it plays an essential role in the water balance of the Supramonte.

Since the entrance to the cave is under water directly in front of the cliffs in the Mediterranean Sea, the entire logistics can only be carried out by water or by boat. By exploring the course of the “Bel Torrente”, this exploration can provide an important building block in the exploration of the mighty Supramonte karst system and contribute significantly to the understanding of the underwater household and the history of the formation of the “Supramonte” mountains.

TIME FRAME: The next exploration will take place at the end of August 2020 with the further exploration of Sifon 5 which will require a stay of at least 7 days. A team of 4 people will go to camp at S2, there will be 2 porters who will carry material up to S4. Then the Push Team will try to dive into S5 after S4 (which is known) to see how the cave will proceed…

PAST EXPLORATIONS: – 1990: Leo Fancello, Axel Mahler, Mario Mazzoli, Wolfgang Morlock explored the cave and were able to explore up to 900m. – 2003 – 2006, HFGOK (cave research group Ostalb Kirchheim Tek) (Germany) further explored the cave up to 2906m and after S1 found the Vadosen part of the cave.

  • 2005 Exploration of the system and discovery of the so-called “Bone Passage” with a number of bone finds from monk seals
  • 2006 Exploration of the “Ramo del Bue” passage to the surface and further exploration of + 250m of new course
  • 2008 Exploration of the dry passages to S1 and first orientation in the Speleo area and also consideration of the S2 entrance -2009 First dive with Rick Stanton in S2 and exploration of + 700m of new cave Phreatic and emerge in a vadose zone, also exploration of this course of more than + 500m in length and discovery of the Sifon3 - 2012 surveying of the S2 +540m and first exploration (phreatic) of the S3
  • 2013 Further exploration (phreatic) in S2: + 500 m (approx.)
  • 2013 Resurvey after S1 in Vadosen part of the cave and new discovery of further + 300m cave
  • 2014 Survey of S3: + 120 m
  • 2014 Further exploration after S3 + 207 m and discovery of the S4
  • 2015 Further exploration (phreatic) of S4 (Neoprene) and survey of + 241 m – 2015 rediscovery of S5 and first dive done, but sidemount rebreathers have to be used due to the fast, deeper profile. Enormous passage sizes of approx. 15m diameter discovered
  • 2016 new discovery after S1 + 978 m
  • 2017 new discovery after S1 + 1578 m
  • 2019 new discovery after S1 + 840m “Lo Smilzo” The cave has now been explored over 8.6km and further research is planned for 2020.
