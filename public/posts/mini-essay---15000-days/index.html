Mini Essay - 15000 days

Created: 2026-05-26
Updated: 2026-09-13
Status: seed
Tags: #writing #mini-essay 
Related: Mini Essays Writing

On Friday I hit the 15,000 day mark - 15,000 days that I’ve been alive and living on this planet. It’s strange to think that I started tracking my days alive when I first discovered Mars One, and stranger still to discover that I hadn’t created a markdown file for Mars One until just now. It still feels like it’s a thing that I’m trying to forget or let go, and yet it was one of the things that lit me up the most in the world. I think the hurt I experienced with Mars One and the criticism it (and I in turn) received really left a major mark on my psyche, and in many ways I’m really just recovering from it and finding my feet again all these years after the program officially shutdown. I love that I track my days on Earth, and especially enjoyed that I might one day be able to countdown the number of days I’d have left - not because I was dying, but because I was leaving for another world. Seeing my time on Earth as being finite and measurable provided an enormous sense of purpose - that days are never to be wasted, and that I am capable of doing so very much with what I have that each day on Earth is an opportunity to make things better for myself and for others.

15000 days seems fairly arbitrary, and I’m really only paying attention to it because it’s a significant number. It doesn’t correspond to years in any real manner, but it’s still a big number and a reminder that I have already had a lot of days to do a lot of different an incredible things. I don’t think I’ll get another 15,000 days, so I need to make the absolute most out of however many days I do get on Earth. This makes me re-evaluate my Bucket List and ask if there are things on there that don’t really matter to me, or if there are things that mean a lot that are not on that list. Hitting 15,000 days makes me ask if the life I’m living right now is the one I want to continue to live, and how I’d like to shape the things that I’m not happy about. Writing about all of this also reminds me that this is something I absolutely love to do - to write about concepts and things that i love, and to feel that satisfying clack of the keyboard as I type out my thoughts about things I love. So much of the last few years has been about “surviving”, and yet I have always know that I thrive right on the edge of life and death - that I can and should do incredibly challenging things and if I die, then at least boring people will say “He deserved it” while never even glimpsing the absolute awe I experience through my existence.

I have spent so many days working to serve others, and I continue to believe that it is the right thing to do. What I’ve learned from the 2750 days since I started my first cave diving course is that the Australian cave diving community is incredibly toxic and populated by predators who will take what you offer and spit on you for it. But that’s no reason to give up on people, and you’ve met plenty of incredible people cave diving too. You met plenty of amazing people in the space industry too, and that took up a similar chunk of about 1/6th of your life so far. You have so much to give, and you’ve done so much with the time you’ve had already - continue to be extraordinary, continue to prove the shit-heels wrong, and see the absolute best in yourself. You don’t owe anyone anything, and you’ve given far more than you’ve ever taken, so keep celebrating your days on Earth and keep aspiring to create, love, and celebrate this existence each and every day you can access it.
