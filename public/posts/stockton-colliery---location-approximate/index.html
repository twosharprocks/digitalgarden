Additional Notes#

Diving so far has seen more than 1000m of line laid in tunnels between 30m & 40m (most at 36-38m) in a typical “Bord and Pillar” design coalmine (colliery) where they extracted 30% of the coal. The 2m thick seam was extracted using a square grid tunnel system of 20m spacing and is found between layers of sandstone. The workings are all shored up with jarrah timber, though several sections of roof collapse have been found. Rebreathers are a definite bonus !! The water in the tunnels is 14C so preservation of the wood can be expected to be good, whereas preservation of divers is not !!

The Stockton Colliery was an underground mine between 1927 & 1960. An open cut was developed and worked between 1943 and 1957. The open cut intersected the underground workings and that is how we access the now flooded tunnels. Stockton Colliery was the only mine in the area completely excavated by hand and the coal removed by pit ponies. The potential to dive in tunnels down to 90m depth exists, though I doubt if we’ll get to 90m, not because of the depth itself, but because the horizontal distance to get there could be as much as three kilometres !! The Dive Profile link below shows a typical dive in the Stockton Colliery on closed circuit.

When I saw the map of the entire Colliery and identified the tunnels we had dived, I realised what a miniscule part of it we had seen – it is staggering !! According to the map, the underground workings extend approx 500m to the North and several km to the West and East from the lake. The original surface entrance (in-filled) is located to the East and when we dive the tunnels in that direction, they do become shallower. The October 02 Map shows what we dived during the first push into the tunnel system and the November 02 Map shows more. [map to come: The Stockton Colliery Site Plan shows the full extent of the flooded tunnels and where we have dived is highlighted in red.] It should be noted that Stockton is just one of many collieries in the area !!

Plenty more fun and good training dives to be done at Stockton Lake. The opportunity to do massive circuit dives in the mines and back into the open pit is limitless. Just choose your bottom time !! Diving the Stockton Colliery tunnels is NOT recommended for open circuit divers due to the reduction in viz caused by exhaust gas as well as disturbance of loose rock slabs in collapse areas increasing the risk of further collapse.
