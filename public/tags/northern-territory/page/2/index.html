Pine Creek

#diving  #caving  #northern-territory 

References

  • Trimix Divers - https://www.trimixdivers.com/trimix5/?p=4893: OK, it’s out of the bag. NT has a new freshwater dive site located a few weeks ago by Dr Jeff Swann from Darwin. The info he gathered from the Mines Department confirmed a max depth of 135m in the old goldmine open cut shaft of Pine Creek, 224km south of Darwin. It is a large (250m x 700m) site, blue in appearance with good access via the approach road on the South East corner of the lake. Water quality is confirmed as good with plans to make it a public recreation area in the near future. It is certainly considerably healthier than Rum Jungle! On Saturday, Dr Natasha Mitchell, Dr Jeff Swann and I dived the site. Jeff and Tash dived to 56m finding 5m viz in the first 20m opening out to 8m below that, temperature a cool (for NT) 24 degrees. I dived to 72m, finding the step cut mine to continue downwards, confirming the information previously given. Dim daylight was still present at 72m, although lights were required. In our surface interval we used a zodiac, portable sounder and compass to plot the rough layout of the site. Unfortunately the sounder (rated to 200m) would not give us readings past 79m, but we did manage to plot the area it doesn’t read, giving us a good indication of where the depth is. Future efforts will hopefully reveal official plans of the site otherwise a decent survey will be conducted.

On Monday, Jack Martins, Claire Elliott and I returned, Jack and Claire doing an air dive familiarisation to 45m whilst I did a drop to 100m. I conducted this dive on open circuit using trimix 13/44 in backmounted steel 120’s, with air and nitrox in 55 cuft sling bottles. The dive went well although visibility dropped again to 2-3m past 70m, with the step cut almost vearing to vertical for the final 25m, making for a rapid drop and increased narcosis. A bounce profile confirmed that the site continues further downwards. The return to the surface during the 71 minute dive included Pyle deepstops commencing at 60m, air between 60m and 15m stops, and nitrox 60 stops commencing at 12m. Dive planning software utilised was Z-Plan 103, turnaround at the max depth was commenced 7 minutes into the dive, 18 minutes extra deco time was added to the stops for safety.

References

  • Trimix Divers - https://www.trimixdivers.com/trimix5/?p=4895: This flooded, old mining pit is located in the Litchfield National Park and has been rated by the CDAA as a Sinkhole site. The notoriety of this site stems from its alleged previous life as an open cut uranium mine. The truth is that whilst trace elements were found in the ore extracted, it was not in sufficient concentrations to warrant its mining, however Copper Sulphate abounded and this is the real origin of the site. Whilst the former?s radioactivity remains at very safe levels, the latter remains of some concern from it?s formation of a layer of hydrogen sulphide, which has been known to affect some divers. The site is deemed to be safe by the local council and indeed is a popular swimming hole for locals (2 legged not 4, and not the big chomping types !).

This site makes a superb training venue for depth and low visibility, and drops to 56m near the middle of the lake, beneath the permanent buoy. The sides of the site drop evenly and there is little evidence of any undercuts or horizontal drives from the mining activity. There is a distinct halo- and thermo-cline at around 7-9m above which the water is warmish (27 degrees) and green with low viz, below which the light cannot penetrate and viz opens to 2m, 3m on a good day. The floor is thick fluffy silt with virtually no features bar the occasional 44 gallon drum.
