Mini Essay - Why you didn’t think you’d make it to 30

#mini-essay  #personal  #101-things  #philosophy  #writing 

Throughout most of my 20’s I was certain I wouldn’t be alive to turn 30. It’s not like I can pinpoint exactly where that idea came from, but there’s no question it was a recurring belief that shaped the way I thought and planned my life. If I try to trace it back, I suspect I’ll wind up back at April 19th, 2008. I made a choice that night to live, and it was certainly a close-call, but I’ve used that night as a reference for most of my life since - no matter what awful shit I’ve been through since then, I know that I will never allow myself to be trapped in that mindset ever again, where suicide felt like the only way out.

Mini Essay - Your evil secret plan

#mini-essay  #philosophy  #personal  #writing 

Ask yourself “What is your evil secret plan?”. Ultimately this is a question about how you’re going to use the system to do the things that YOU want to do and live the life you want to live. We’re not talking about blindly trying to “Live your dreams” - we’re talking about gaming the system so it looks like you’re playing along with everyone else, but you’re hacking the game so you do exactly what you want regardless of what others may expect or even demand of you. So you’re not playing by any rules, but you are faking compliance while you put all the pieces into place to subvert expectations.

Note - Mini Essays

#knowledge-management  #writing 

Original: https://youtu.be/N4YjXJVzoZY

Short piece of writing on some thing you find interesting All about retention, learning & reflection Write fast and often - over time it provides depth Mini-essays make learning more addictive by being novel Like a sketchbook for an artist

Three rules of Mini-essays

  1. Only cover one idea
  2. Keep them short (fit on monitor without scrolling)
  3. Keep them organised (Obsidian)

Elements of mini-essay

  • 100-300 words (no scrolling within Obsidian, focus on being brief)
  • Storytelling approach? Prose? Make sure this serves your goals
  • Write it as if you’re teaching it (Feynmann technique)
  • Restrict to single concept (one distinct idea) or idea (save connection/blending for larger writing)
