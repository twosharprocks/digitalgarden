Kongorong Sinkhole - Location TBC

#diving  #caving  #south-australia 

About

“Behind Kongorong Hall”

Details

Identification

  • Feature type: Sinkhole
  • Source: LSECR

Cave and exploration data

  • Vertical access: No
  • Goes to water: No

Additional Notes

Kongorong Sinkhole is an almost featureless doline which is basically a simple 30 metre diameter doline some 5 metres or so deep. It is not exactly within “spitting distance” of the small township of Kongorong from which its name was derived, but someone nevertheless thought it was a suitable name at the time, and it can only be seen from a relatively remote dirt track. An August 1971 CEGSA Trip Report by caver Ian D. Lewis described the feature as follows: “It is on the south-west side of a ridge which runs at 338 degrees. The sinkhole is 100-120 feet long (major axis) and 90-100 feet long (minor axis); it is therefore oval-shaped and degraded along the west and south-western sides. “There is a vertical wall 10 feet high on the east and south-eastern sides. Depth is 15 feet (on the) west side and 20 feet on the eastern side. “The major axis runs north-south, and there is a small rubbish pile on the south side which does not appear to cover a shaft or similar feature. The inside floor of the sinkhole is a flat grassed area with a few small rocks. Sheep were grazing in it… when I first approached the sinkhole”. The feature does not have any accessible underground extensions, and a visit by the author in 1984 found that a car had also been dumped in it. It is obviously not really worth the effort of a visit by keen cavers!
