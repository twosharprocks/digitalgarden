Akiyoshidai

#diving 

About

From https://tech-diving.jp/akiyoshidai

What is Akiyoshidai? Akiyoshidai is Japan’s largest karst plateau , located in the central and eastern parts of Mine City, Yamaguchi Prefecture. Its limestone distribution covers approximately 93 km², a scale unparalleled anywhere else in Japan.

More than 400 limestone caves have been identified in Akiyoshidai, including Akiyoshido Cave , one of Japan’s three largest limestone caves and a designated special natural monument.

Akiyoshidai is divided into two plateaus, Higashidai and Nishidai, by the Higashikawa River . Higashidai (Akiyoshidai in the narrow sense) was designated a national park (Akiyoshidai National Park) in 1955 and a special natural monument in 1964. In addition, individual caves are registered as natural monuments, and meticulous care is taken in their maintenance and management.

Algeria

#caving  #diving 

About

Algeria features a diverse landscape that includes mountain ranges, vast deserts, and karstic plateaus, making it highly relevant from a speleological and geological perspective.

🪨 Limestone Karst in Algeria

Yes, Algeria has significant limestone karst formations, especially in the northern and northeastern regions of the country. These include:

  • Djurdjura Mountains (part of the Tell Atlas): This region has well-developed karst plateaus with numerous caves and sinkholes, formed in Jurassic and Cretaceous limestones.
  • **Constantine and Setif regions**: Known for complex karst systems and underground hydrology.
  • Tassili n’Ajjer (southeastern Algeria - DO NOT TRAVEL): While primarily known for its sandstone formations and prehistoric rock art, some karst-like erosion features occur in isolated carbonate outcrops.
