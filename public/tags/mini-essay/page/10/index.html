Mini Essay - What is fear to you

#mini-essay  #writing 

This morning you spent a lot of time thinking during your walk about what fear means to you. There were a lot of examples, but actually pinning it down felt really hard, and much of that comes down to it being similar to distinctively different to anxiety.

For me, fear is cold followed by force. Fear is something you tend to run at, something you feel and immediately react to with action. Fear isn’t the every day concerns of things not working out or of something hypothetically happening - that’s anxiety. Fear is trying to hold a door closed as three sailors smash into it from the other side to kill you and rape the two women in the room with you. Fear is grabbing the fire extinguisher off the wall and breaking the toes in the foot jammed in the same door. Fear is holding onto a plastic sheet carrying a friend as they’re dying of hypothermia, as you run through the mud to get him into the back of an ambulance, then someone to help peel your fingers from the plastic because your hand has cramped onto it. Fear is going back to camp afterwards and finding two more, and knowing there’s no one left to help but you. Fear is talking your Mum through all the things she needs to put in a Go-Bag, because you’re sure that your father is going to try and kill her. Fear is begging her not to wait until he breaks in one night, to leave and get out now. Fear is him finding out where she’s been hiding, and having him threaten to fly across the country to kill her as well as my sister and her family too. Fear is knowing exactly what you’d to him do if he ever did.

Mini Essay - What were the highlights of Antarctica

#mini-essay  #writing  #antarctica  #bucket-list  #expeditions 

Mini Essay - Why Antarctica is Important to you Mini Essay - PRIVATE - What has changed in you since Antarctica Mini Essay - November 23

Today marks two months since the end of Trip - Antarctica - 2025 Nov, and I feel like it’s well overdue for a reflection on what happened and the highlights of visiting the frozen continent.

The most immediate aspect of visiting Antarctica is that it was my “7th continent”. I still don’t feel like I’ve properly visited South America, as I really just flew through Santiago and Buenos Aires on the way to Ushuaia, then wandered around Ushuaia for a few days before boarding the ship to Antarctica… but I have now set foot on all 7 continents. That was never really that big a deal for me though - I never set out to visit all 7 continents (even though it’s one of the Bucket List) and I’ve always been a little disgusted by folks who count the number of countries they’ve visited. Travel is extraordinary and vital to our emotional growth, but visiting or ticking off places for just the sake of it always felt disrespectful to the places you were visiting. I’ve travelled extensively through North America and Europe, done multiple visits to Africa, and been to a variety of places through Asia too. But this most recent trip is the only time I’ve been to South America, and it felt like it was just a trip in transit to Antarctica. Visiting Antarctica is understandably difficult so a 10 day trip is not to be ignored, but I still don’t feel like I’ve really experienced the continent either - that’s something I think will require an overwinter stay. So ticking off my 6th and 7th continents weren’t really highlights from the trip.
