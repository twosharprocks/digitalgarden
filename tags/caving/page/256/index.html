L110B

#diving  #caving  #south-australia 

About

One of two entrances into a small cave. One entrance is a 1.5m drop and the other about 3m. They both enter the same cave which is connected by a small crawl. There are no obvious leads

Details

Identification

  • Feature type: Cave
  • Reference: L0110
  • Source: Forestry SA

Cave and exploration data

  • Vertical access: Yes
  • Exploration potential: Low

Access and warnings

Forestry

References

  • LSECR: Page 218 Known as “Entrance B when first explored sometime around late 1967 or early 1968 (see 5L109), this small cave was visited in November 1986 by the author where he discovered that it was about 20 metres long, one metre wide and ranged between one and 3 metres in height. Entry is made via a 2 metre diameter collapse window which drops 2m into a small cavity, and the pass- age then heads north-west for a few metres more to the edge of another 2m-deep sandy drop. This is the edge of a small chamber which has a roof window about 5 metres above its lowest point. This chamber lies under the southern edge of a nearby forestry track where a steel stake has been hammered into the ground. A sandy collapsing floor-hole was found during the 1986 visit, but lack of a breeze provided little impetus for farther (risky) exploration, so the north-western crawl- way was pushed to its limit instead. This was found to extend for just another 4 metres or so before it closed down completely and appeared to have no further extensions
  • Forestry SA: L0110, Thin Roof: N, Fence: R. Dimensions: 2m x 2m drop. Features: Some wire; 2 entrances; old fence

Access and warnings

Forestry

References

  • LSECR: Page 220 This feature appears to have originally been known as “Entrance D in the series 5L109-5L111 (refer to texts for these cave numbers), and it was described as being a 6 metre diameter, 2m deep countersunk depression which funnelled down to a one metre diameter tube before dropping some 4 metres to the floor of a north-west to south-east joint-controlled cave. The cave floor reportedly sloped evenly down at an angle of around 30 degrees to the east-south- east, giving a drop of 8-9 metres in the length of the chamber, which was about 2.5 metres wide. The plan view map in-dicates that it terminated about 12 metres from the entrance in two very narrow fissures. Unfortunately, this feature was reportedly completely filled by bulldozing (in preparation for pine planting) within weeks of the CEGSA visit of 7th October 1967.
