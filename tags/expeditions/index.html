Exploring Japan’s Hidden Rivers

An Australian cave diving expedition to the Mine–Akiyoshidai Karst Plateau Geopark
By Josh Richards & Will Passos

Josh Richards and Will Passos walking through a bamboo forest after their first dive in Terayama Cave

The Mine–Akiyoshidai Karst Plateau Geopark in western Japan is famous for its limestone scenery, extensive cave systems and the enormous Akiyoshido tourist cave. However beneath it’s picturesque landscape, the Mine-Akiyoshidai plateau is riddled with submerged passages and an active groundwater system that remains only partially understood.

Azerbaijan

#diving  #expeditions  #exploration 

About

Moderate Potential Areas of Interest: Talysh and Caucasus Foothills

Geological Context

  • Azerbaijan has extensive limestone outcrops in the Greater Caucasus, Lesser Caucasus, and Talysh Mountains.
  • Karst formations are concentrated in Nakhchivan, Ismailli, and Lankaran areas.

Known Caves

  • Dry caves like Ashabi-Kahf, Karst caves of Gobustan, and others have archaeological and geological significance.
  • Thermal karst springs exist in Nakhchivan and other mountainous zones.

Cave Diving Status

  • No documented cave diving.
  • However, karst springs near Lankaran and Lerik may contain submerged sections suitable for exploration.

Exploration Potential

  • Greater logistical challenges and less existing infrastructure than Armenia.
  • Some springs in south-eastern Azerbaijan (Talysh Range) appear strong enough to support substantial submerged conduits.
