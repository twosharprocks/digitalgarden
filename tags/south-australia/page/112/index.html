Scott’s

Additional Notes

The descriptive name for this sinkhole-like cave entrance was evidently allocated by Bob Sexton during his April 1962 CEGSA trip to the lower South East, when it contained a vast amount of foul rubbish. The large amount of hard rubbish has only deteriorated a little during the past 25 years or so, and even today it is more of a rubbish tip than an interesting karst feature! It is basically a single large collapse chamber with a roof window about 20 metres in diameter and with overhanging walls all the way around. By dropping a 6 metre long ladder along the wall on the south-western side to the top of the rubbish-pile, access to the 25 metre wide area in the lower reaches of Rubbish Cave can be made with care, but it is not possible to safely walk right around the bottom of the feature these days. Although the April 1962 trip report stated that “on the south-eastern and eastern sides… there is an extremely foul pool of grey water”, preliminary dives by the author and Andrew Cox in May 1990 revealed much clearer conditions and a maximum underwater depth of just 6 metres with no apparent extensions at the south-eastern end. Rubbish Cave has very little to attract cave explorers or divers!

Ryan’s Solution Tube

#diving  #caving  #south-australia 

About

Unusual solution tube under concrete in the Malloney/Kaczkowski’s carport. Hole searched for after report from Simon Carey (previous owner) of large cave under concrete - possibly search for steel plate with a metal detector?

Details

Identification

  • Feature type: SolutionTube

Cave and exploration data

  • Vertical access: Yes
  • Exploration potential: Low
  • Goes to water: No

Additional Notes

“Funnily enough I had breakfast this morning with my best mate who concreted over hole in Molony’s driveway. He remembers the hole and just how big the area is under Commercial St. If you follow the direction of the yellow line on your map, and extend it to the front door of Molonys…That’s the direction of the cave. We dropped rocks and heard nothing, we shone torches and couldn’t see anything. It was massive. If you stand in the middle of the front door and take 3 large steps heading south, your right over the hole. Under the concrete is about 2 inches of bitumen and then nothing! It’s really scary! I lowered myself through the hole, shone a strong torch towards Vansittart Park, and it was just black. That probably explains why Vansittart Park dries out so quickly. You can have 2 inches of rain in the Mount and all the other ovals are under water and Vansittart Park is as dry as a bone.”
