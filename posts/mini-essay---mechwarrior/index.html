Mini Essay - MechWarrior

#mini-essay  #writing 

There’s something really unique about my life-long relationship with the BattleTech / MechWarrior videogame series. The tabletop game look interesting too, but I’ve never really gone in for tabletop wargames except to paint them as models. But the videogames have always held a very special place in my heart, and it goes all the way back to my first exposure through MechWarrior 2.

Being able to stop around in such a huge bipedal robot and shoot things has to be a huge part of this. The idea of controlling a building size walking machine fitted with lasers and rockets is certainly awe inspiring, far more than I’d feel driving a tracked vehicle. In fact the tracked and flying vehicles in MechWarrior 5 often feel out of place when I see them in-game - more like a machine and less like a powerful giant.

The view is certainly an important element in this too, as I don’t enjoy the small cramped view of the cockpit and always immediately opt for the wider 3rd person perspective. Being able to see my mech (and it is my mech) looking cool, shooting shit and hearing the epic sound of my weapons while weaving over terrain and between buildings, and generally dominating the battlefield - it feels incredible.

It’s important to note that the lore of MechWarrior endlessly fascinates me as well. Having churned through over a dozen BattleTech audiobooks, hearing the details of that universe is almost as good as playing the games that recreate it. Even now I want to soak up the stories in a way that WH40k never quite recreated. The idea of the clans is particularly interesting to you, along with the stories of the star league and the Lostech caches left behind. None of it is real - it’s all complete fiction set in an alternate universe 1000 years in the future. And yet reading about it makes me want to go out into the universe and discover these incredible caches of information and knowledge that only exist in the minds of lore writers.

The elements of lore that I really connect with are vital here too. I know I don’t give the slightest shit about the palace intrigue of the inner sphere, but the culture and single-mindedness of the clans and the mercenaries is fascinating. They’re not interested in the petty bullshit - they’re bred for war, armed with refined war-fighting machines, and they’re very fucking good at what they do. That’s the appeal for you - the raw do-or-die attitude couched in a very real capability to do your job. Follow the process, think about how to be the best you can be, and take setbacks and success in your stride - it’s just another day.

This also reminds me of…

  • Being really good at your job and doing it every day because you love it
  • Having a singular focus on what you need to do to survive
  • Being fascinated by the idea of lost knowledge and seeking it out
  • Feeling like a powerful machine being an extension of yourself
  • Looking at changes to humanity and technology 100’s and 1000’s of years into the future.
