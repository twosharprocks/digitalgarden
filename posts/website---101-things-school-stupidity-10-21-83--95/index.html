Website - 101 Things School Stupidity (10, 21, 83 & 95)

2012-09-19[Josh Richards]
#writing 

Just a bit of background – this was written way back in April, but it somehow slipped through the scheduling process. As such the “most recent employer” comment toward the end actually refers to another previous employer.

——————————————————————————————————————————-

This week’s 101 Things entry is all about some of the stupid shit I did during my education. It is far from a comprehensive list of my school stupidity as I’m sure “ Abducting your Year 9 English teacher’s teletubby and blowing it up on camera ” isn’t on the 101 list. But these are the stupid things I did (mostly) at school that relate directly to the list…

10 – Leave Your Mark in Graffiti I’ve never had any interest in graffiti at all. But I did go through a stage in year 8 where I inexplicably started calling my myself “Isbu” and running around with saying (in a mock Indian accent) “I AM ISBU!” for months, driving my parents to despair. Which is weird and seemingly unrelated – until I wrote “ISBU WAZ ERE” on a wall at a holiday camp with a marker pen. And my Dad saw it about 15 minutes later. And I was made to clean it off with a scourer while I cried tears of guilt. And let us never speak of this dark and bizarre period of my life ever again…

21 – Be a Human Guinea Pig For most, university is about experimenting and experiencing new things. For me in the Curtin Physics department, it was about trying new things while messing with people and unsuccessfully chasing girls. And I do mean unsuccessfully – one afternoon in first year three of us went exploring the other common rooms on campus, eventually meeting three girls in the Chemistry common room (we didn’t venture far). And over the next 6 months we all seemed to pair off – one pair went on to date for about a year, the other for over 2. Not me though: I got turned down by my Chemistry girl pairing because the fourth season premiere of The Amazing Race was on…

So it’s probably no surprise that when Keel’s – the only remotely attractive, under 30 and female member of the Physics faculty – mentioned that her equally attractive younger sister (making her our age) was running a research study in the Public Health building, about 6 of us immediately run across campus to take part…

…to find out it was a double-blind study tasting cheese and the only person we actually dealt with was mid-50’s tutor who looked at us in disapproval when we walked in the door. Although in fairness, we were probably looking at him with considerably more disappointment than he was sending at us.

83 – Skinny Dip at Midnight Ahhh, the wonders of year 9… or not. I have no idea how I wind up in these situations, but the facts are these:

  • It was I was at a chick friend’s place with another guy and girl - It was about 11:30pm - I had nil interest being there, and was frankly bored - The other guy was trying to convince the two girls to nude up, so I did it myself – partly to spite him, partly because at least it made the night remotely memorable.

Actually, I do know how I wind up in these situations – a combination of boredom and ADHD. This whole post is turning into a collection of “Josh’s Greatest Fails”…

But wait, there’s still…

95 – Get Revenge Before people start jumping to conclusions here, I’d like to make something very, very clear – I don’t believe in revenge. I do however believe in assisting the universe at fucking over shitty people when the opportunity presents. And besides one fairly nasty ex-girlfriend – who’s cosmic punishment is to wake every single day being her – I’ve really only been forced to deal with two people who’ve seriously pushed my limits. People who, just by being there, turned an otherwise fun and healthy learning/work environment into something I dreaded going to each day. One of them was my most recent Line Manager, the other was my Year 6 teacher. The Year 6 teacher is an easy one – she hated me because I’d just come back from living overseas and she’d been teaching in the same classroom for 15 years. And her way of dealing with the stress of teaching was to be sweet as pie to everyone except the one kid she singled out each year to direct her frustrations at – foolishly she picked me.

She told my parents I might be mentally handicapped (she actually used the word “retarded”), and that I was an attention-seeking troublemaker (at least that wasn’t a lie). Only unlike the kids from previous years she’d picked on, I fought back – told her she was a bully, and that I wanted to discuss all the abuse she’d been directing at me with the Principal. So she had a nervous breakdown. And I do mean a nervous breakdown – when she left the principal’s office she’d been balling her eyes out for an hour and immediately went on 3 months psych leave.

So we got a substitute teacher for the last term of Year 6. But to top it all off, the following year I won an award for being the highest scoring student in Western Australia in a national science competition. To recognise my achievement, the principal presented the award from UNSW to me in front of the entire school at our weekly school assembly. And once he handed it to me and shook my hand, I turned to the crowd, held my award in front of the whole school, then stared straight at her and grinned until the other teachers made me sit down.

“Retarded” you say?
