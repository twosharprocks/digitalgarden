Anyone following the chaos on twitter for the last week is undoubtedly already aware that I started the Southern Hemisphere Summer Space Program with the International Space University (ISU) on Monday. Operating out of the University of South Australia’s Mawson Lakes campus, the ISU course is an intensive 5 week course where people from all over the world have lectures, workshops & meetings with some of the world’s top space professionals.

THIS IS NOT SPACE CAMP

… but it feels a lot like space camp. The biggest outcome from all of this is that the team of 35 students – ranging from high achieving 2nd year engineering students, to Brazilian Air Force Colonels, to Chinese rocket designers and everything inbetween – that need to put together a white paper of recommendations on using space systems to support disaster management.

Which is probably a really long way of saying we’re learning about space and how not to kill each other when you’re working in such a diverse group.

Moments before the massacre

The staff have made suggestions and encouraged a few lines of progress, but for the most part it’s been down to the 35 of us to work out who’s going to make decisions, what our mission statement is, and those awkward fine details like the structure of meetings and how to vote.

Stevie the Slow Loris votes for more rum and hugs in meetings

There’s also been some really entertaining nights out. After everyone arrived last Sunday there was a short “introductions” session so we could put names to faces. Monday kicked off with the class watching the live launch of the Thaicom 6 satellite in SpaceX’s first launch to GEO, then some formal “welcoming”. This welcoming was through and extensive – I don’t think I’ve been welcomed so hard since uni orientation camp. We had flag waving, lecturer meeting, booze drinking, pomp, ceremony and circumstance.

We’ve also had a chance to learn directly from astronauts: Paolo Nespoli from ESA and Andy Thomas from NASA have spent the week with us, giving lectures, conducting public question panels, and even just hanging over lunch. All of us tried to play it pretty cool, but at certain level of fan-girling was inevitable. Both Andy & Paolo were quite accommodating with autographs, and we even managed to convince Paolo to shoot a short video for the ESA’s “Wake Up Rosetta” competition.

Watch archived videoSo we’re having a great time – the unofficial ISU acronym of “Insufficient Sleep University” is well deserved, and it’s sure to get worse as we progress with our white paper on space-based disaster management. But its great being here, working with such an amazingly talented group of people, learning from some of the biggest names in the international space industry, and hopefully contributing something worthwhile to the world space community.
