Mini Essay - Your Cave Diving Future

#mini-essay  #diving  #writing 

Now that you’ve been suspended by the CDAA, you have an amazing opportunity to look ahead and ask what kind of future you want to have in cave diving. You knew the suspension was coming, and you’ve been more than emotionally ready to tackle it - part of you has even relished it finally coming to pass. So the question now is how you want to develop as an exploration cave diver outside of Mt Gambier and the CDAA.

First and foremost you’re heading to Sulawesi to dive and chat to Robin Cuesta, which presents exciting opportunities for both your podcast and to discuss the importance of water security. A major point of interest for your cave exploration is to give it a purpose and scientific legitimacy - you want to explore not just for the sake of exploring, but to also bring back valuable data and to help the people who are responsible for the land you’re diving under.

Your future also involves diving outside of Mt Gambier, and mostly outside of Australia for that matter too. There’s no doubt you want to visit incredible places and to help others see them too - your real interest is in making adventures easier for everyone so they can support the exploration too. At the heart of this however is your own desire to explore, experience and share cave diving with others - to tap into that passion for an “underground Google Maps” that you dreamed of so many years ago. That means having a list of places you want to explore AND to collect publishable data from. It’s not enough to just see these places and take a select few - you really want to share these places with anyone who’s interested, to feed that passion for adventure, and to do good science so we can understand these places better.

So ask yourself - where do you want to cave dive, and how can you collect great data to support the production of incredible maps and models for the places you visit? Even the most touristy caves don’t have 3D models, so don’t just go for the most difficult or remote places you can think of - dives like Mjonar Janos and Gozo are absolutely worth the journey. And don’t worry about how you’ll fit this around work - you have the resources to do these things, so plan them out and make them happen. Whatever you figure out with work, you’ll be able to support yourself and see the places you want to see.

First priority? Figure out how to get Lewy to the Eastern Mediterranean, and make sure you record that journey. Taking Australia’s pre-eminent underwater cave geologist on his dream trip would be an extraordinary thing to do, and there’s only so many years available to do it.
