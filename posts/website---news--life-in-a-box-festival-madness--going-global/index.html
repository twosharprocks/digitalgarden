Word of warning: This post is everything I’ve been up to over the last 3 months, and is about a ~15minute read. If you think that’s going to tax your ADD too much, just imagine how hard it was writing it.

************************************************************************

Some years you just know are going to be out of control. Big projects finish up, even bigger ones kick off, and there’s always a multitude of random chaos blended with it. You can prepare as best as possible, but ultimately you just have to dive in and take it as it comes. Provided you keep pushing yourself and laugh at the ridiculousness of it all though, it every single day gets weirder and more entertaining…

… which is my long-winded way of saying the first 3 months of 2016 have been bat-shit insane.

Many of you followed the weekly adventures of the International Space University’s Southern Hemisphere Summer Space Program in Adelaide I was working at. What you’d have missed though was the day before I left for SHSSP16 I met up with an ex-girlfriend for breakfast, where I told her about my global comedy tour and she joked “Australia was never going to keep you”… to which I replied “Earth can’t keep me”. Then because I had nothing else planned that day, I spent the next 8 hours in a tattoo parlour getting a lightsabre-weilding Carl Sagan riding a velociraptor & Rick Sanchez riding a honey badger etched into my shoulder.

To say my 4 weeks at SHSSP16 was full-on is a massive understatement. After the absurdly long days of trying to keep 31 participants from across the globe vaguely sane and on-track with their white paper, I figured my 5 days in the #BringHimHome habitat would be breeze: a nice change of pace and a chance to catch up on the emails I was behind on, as well as getting on with some writing I’ve been wanting to do for a while. Life in the hab would prove otherwise…

It’s all smiles and happiness outside the hab, isn’t it asshole?

I’d been working with 20th Century Fox for months to design the #BringHimHome activation to promote the DVD release of The Martian… but as soon as I saw the hab the day before the activation started, I knew I was in for a really long week. The activation had been setup right next to the Overseas Passenger Terminal in Sydney’s Circular Quay so that the 10’s of thousands of people coming off the cruise ships would be able to see a ginger idiot live inside a little white box for 5 days pretending to be Mark Watney. Which I had no issues with, except in order for those 10’s of thousands of people to see me living inside a 3x3x9m glass & steel box, they had to face the hab windows DIRECTLY INTO THE AFTERNOON SUN.

So when I arrived on at 5pm the afternoon before the activation started, the air temperature inside was 36 degrees with the doors open and both air-conditioning units running flat-out. With the hab sealed up and the air con working the average temperature for the week ran around 38 degrees. Then on the first afternoon inside the hab the circuit breaker on the generator tripped, the air-conditioning cut out for 2 hours and things got… emotional. I’d tell you what temperature it got to, but the temperature gauge stopped working, along with one of the iPads cooking off and all the glue in the duct tape melting.

Things peaked out on the second afternoon when I was put through a “Martian Training Session” with Dan Connolly from F45 fitness… which was basically an F45 session designed for someone with less space than a prison cell. What it wasn’t designed for was someone with someone with living in a greenhouse with less space than a prison cell AND who’d barely eaten in the previous 24 hours because the extreme heat had suppressed his diet.

Nothing like dark clothes to soak up that summer sun too…

There was never a point where I’d have quit during my 5 days in the hab, but the Tuesday afternoon fitness session was definitely where things got toughest. After that I finally sorted out my food for the week – I’d asked for Army ration packs which would have been perfect (easy to just remember to eat a ration pack per day) but I got an assortment of camping food, and needed to sort through it to allocate exactly what I’d eat/drink and when. For the last two days “Ground Control” outside the hab also organised sunshades that blocked much of the sun in the afternoons, and I blocked the remainder with silver tarps duct taped to the glass. And just to mix things up a little, my hab/deathtrap didn’t have any insulation and would drop to about 7 degrees overnight… but to be honest the freezing nights were just a welcome relief from being baked each afternoon.

There was no shower in there, so I washed myself with wet-wipes standing next the porta-potty, which I’m sure Mum appreciated when I finally left the hab on the Friday and immediately hugged her. Cutting down the sun on the glass didn’t help the heat in the toilet though – not that I wanted to hang out in the toilet the whole time, but it was where I needed to go to take calls from radio stations all over the country for the relentless stream of interviews. So every one of the interviews you can listen to here recorded “Live from the Hab” were all done while I was sitting on the porta-potty, slowly cooking in the hothouse.

The heat may have been oppressive, but truth be told though I adjusted to life in the hab pretty quickly and it felt very much like “home” by the final day. The limited space in the hab never bothered me (I enjoyed knowing everything I needed was just a few steps away), and I really enjoyed the challenge of fixing equipment (even if it was because the electronics had been cooked). People ask me what the worst part about living in the hab was though, and I tell them it was PEOPLE.

After 10pm each night the drunks would turn up, and while security did their best it was still a zoo out there: people banging on the glass, others trying to yell at me inside, and even a few brave ones trying to force open the door into my hab…

“Ground Control” running things outside also suffered from an issue that has plagued human space missions since we first ventured beyond the atmosphere – giving the crew 10 minutes to do a job that needs 20. They thought I was bored in there, but between the challenges and “hab emergencies”, writing online articles, video conferencing schools, the relentless radio interviews, as well as minor things like occasionally eating and bathing; things got pretty hectic in there.

Life didn’t slow down once I left the hab though. After a quick shower (which I’m sure would have felt majestic if I wasn’t so sleep-deprived), one more radio interview (where Kyle Sanderlands asked me if I was planning on having sex with a grasshopper), and breakfast on Bondi Beach with my parents (who’d flown over to surprise me on “release day”), I jumped on a plane straight back to Adelaide! The plan had been to surprise the SHSSP16 participants at their closing ceremony the same day, but delays meant I turned up at the reception just afterwards. The rest of the staff had stayed quiet on my return though, so I still managed to surprise most of the participants and we celebrated their graduation well into the night.

The University of South Australia runs a space studies graduate certificate program immediately after SHSSP each year, so I stuck around to provide a little support for that while catching up on emails, recover from SHSSP16 & the #BringHimHome hab and prepare a little for jam-packed the months ahead. As soon as the 3-day graduate certificate was sorted though, I needed to fly across the country to take part in the World Science Festival’s regional tour to Townsville! WSF had asked me to take part in events in Townsville, Chinchilla and Brisbane, and while Chinchilla didn’t work out the rest certainly did! The turn out for my talks on Mars One was extraordinary, and I had some of the most rewarding experiences I’ve had since I first applied: getting to tell a 16 year-old space geek about “2001 – A Space Odyssey” when she’d never heard of it before, and having an incredible conversation with a 11 year-old with high-functioning Asperger syndrome about him building a teleporter that I could take to Mars so he could visit me.

There wasn’t much time to hang Townsville though – I had to get back to premiere Cosmic Nomad at the Adelaide Fringe and start my Australian tour! After a very shaky first show (that still managed a 3.5 star review) the show went from strength to strength each night as I found my old comedy groove and the crowds got bigger and bigger each night.

The folks at RiAus were an absolute pleasure to work with too, being so incredibly supportive from start to finish. I also got a chance to catch up with ISU-alumn and science communicator extraordinaire Niamh Shaw, was also in town to perform her award-winning show “To Space”!

After the previous 2 months of chaos and my shows in Adelaide I was completely wrecked though, and having just been officially accepted as the Martian Environment Teaching Assistant at this year’s SSP16 in Haifa, I was far FAR behind on the never-ending email stack. It was a tough call, but in the end I knew I needed to cancel my only Sydney show at the Martian Embassy – there’s no way I could have given the audience the show they deserved, or been able to continue on this frankly ridiculous world tour, if I’d pushed ahead with it. So after a slightly quieter few days in Sydney I shot down to Canberra to visit the fine folks at Questacon, and to watch Alan Alda talk about science communication at the Llewellyn Music Hall for an hour alongside 2000 other fascinated audience members…

Alan was in Australia for the same reason I was heading to Brisbane next though – the World Science Festival was leaving New York for the first time in the 6 years since it began, and thousands of science communicators were descending on Brisbane to hear from the likes of Alan Alda, Brian Greene, Silvia Earle… and ME! I’d recommended Niamh to the festival too, so it was fantastic seeing her again and getting to watch her “Storytelling in science” panel, and I performed Cosmic Nomad to a sold out crowd of 200 in the Festival Lab who loved it… even if I WAS feeling sick as a dog.

Although I’ll be honest, the highlight of performing the show was having a gorgeous, heavily-tattooed woman wander up afterwards and ask to take a photo of the bender tattoo on my ass…

… and I immediately dropped my jeans for her.

After catching up with my sister and her boyfriend in Brisbane for the weekend, it was almost time to move on with the tour. There was one last job to do before the next tour stop though – speak to 3000+ primary school kids at the Halogen Youth Leadership Primary event in Brisbane! I’ve presented for Halogen several times in Perth & Melbourne before, although this time I’d just been booked to be on their “Coolest Job In the World” panel. But when after one of the speakers dropped out suddenly they asked if I could step up again, so I ended up speaking to a frankly astounding number of excited primary school kids about space exploration

Melbourne was up next with a full-on week of running workshops, performing and catching up with old friends. The night after I landed I was running a storytelling workshop, and the next night my “How to Become an Astronaut” workshop – both for laneway learning. The Thursday night saw Cosmic Nomad at the Captain Melville and brought all sorts of mischief: I’d bought a HDMI adaptor to use the venue’s projector, but couldn’t for the life of me find it when it came to set up the show! After some manic tram & uber rides back to where I’d been staying, I decided to heed the wise words of Rick Sanchez… 

… by getting a drink and performing the show with zero tech – not even a microphone. And absolutely smashed a raw stand-up version of the show in front of a completely packed house. I’d been concerned about breaking-even in Melbourne, but we sold so many tickets there were serious concerns about blocking the fire exits. It was incredible being able to turn it on for so many folks who’d decided to come out on a Thursday night for a quirky science-comedy show in a hidden away room at the back of a pub

With Melbourne’s show done I had one more talk at Ormond College on the Friday night as a favour to a friend, and then I finally had time to breathe… for the weekend. I’d only been gone 2 months but it’d felt like 2 years, so it was great being able to mentally check out for 48 hours and reconnect with friends in Melbourne I’d genuinely missed in the meantime.

Which was just as well, because my 3 days in Tasmania performing Cosmic Nomad for Inspiring Australia were even more relentless. Half an hour after I landed in Launceston, I was co-hosting “Tasmania Talks” with Brian Carlton (aka Spoonyman). Brian has been regularly checking in with me about Mars One over the last 3 years since I first applied, so it was an absolute pleasure to finally meet the man behind the voice.

My Launceston and Hobart shows were both supported by local comics with me as the headline act, and given the success of my zero-tech show in Melbourne I was keen to keep that raw stand-up vibe going by losing the slideshow and sticking to the tried-and-tested “Ginger leprechaun with a microphone” comedy formula. The sold-out crowds certainly loved it. Ulverstone was a slightly different vibe – with over a 100 turning up along with the local mayor and a couple of members of parliament in the audience, I decided to bring the slides back while also dialing in my tendency to swear like a sailor and describe unconventional romantic situation in more detail than most are comfortable with. I still got my ass tattoo out though.

Not the ass tattoo this venue needed, but the one it deserved

All this running around started to catch up with me, and after an afternoon being confused by the appeal of MONA in Hobart I could feel I was getting run down on the way to being epicly sick. Thankfully I’d have 10 days in Perth to recover… right? Wrong – I flew straight into the Easter weekend with a hailstorm of Perth schools trying to get me to visit. But when they’re Year 2’s, it’s pretty much impossible to say no.

In the end I managed to speak to about 12 of the Year 2’s at Mel Maria, about 120 gifted science students at Lesmurdie Senior High, and over 1200 at Perth Modern School. Seems like it was the right time to be speaking to school students in Perth about Mars One too. A student in Leeming called Dr Karl on ABC720 to ask what he thought would be the biggest health risks to the Mars One settlers, I heard it online and was less than impressed with Dr Karl’s response and his comments regarding Mars One as an organisation. So like everyone who disagrees with something on the internet, I took to Twitter to complain about it with my best “Well actually…” tone. I wasn’t expecting a response, but to Dr Karl’s credit he sent me a direct message with his mobile number, and we chatted as long as we could about the physiological and engineering challenges of keeping people on Mars permanently. I had to cut him off after half an hour though: it was April 1st and I had a science-comedy show to perform

The final Australian performance of Cosmic Nomad was booked at Scitech for April Fools Day, and after touring the show around the country I figured it was finally time to get it filmed. Videographer extraordinaire Levon Polinelli (the guy responsible for filming & creating the intro for Cosmic Nomad AND the final moments of Keith the Anger Management Koala) setup his camera at the back, the staff at Scitech ran through checking all the AV and welcoming the growing crowd outside, and I tried to distract myself from the growing knot in my stomach at the thought of the show being filmed.

After playing one of my favourite space videos of all time…

Watch archived video… and playing the koala intro video, I walked out into the spotlight, greeted the crowd to start the show… and the slideshow immediately crashed.

There’s a certain confidence in talking about a subject you’ve spend the last 3 years completely immersed in, something you’re passionate about dedicating your life to, and knowing the audience are as interested in the details of going to Mars as they are in your jokes about it. Scitech’s AV guy quickly got everything back online and working smoothly, but when the bluescreen of death appeared my first reaction was “Fuck it – we’ll do it without tech then!”

A huge component of living on Mars will be adapting and adjusting to the unexpected, and making a joke out of it when things DO go wrong – from what I’ve heard, my improvising during the slideshow crash was a highlight rather than a disappointment, so you can expect to see it included in the DVD version when it’s released too!

I’d love to say I finally caught a break after the show, but there was a 24 hour scurry to see friends, post gifts, and answer more emails before the jumping on another plane to start the international tour in South Africa. I flew into Cape Town on Monday morning to meet Dr Adriana Marais – Quantum biologist, winner of the L’Oreal-UNESCO award for women in science, and fellow Mars 100 candidate – and we spent 3 days buzzing around the province trying to see all that Cape Town has to offer.

Between Adriana and her sister Ellen trying to convert me to wine at the vineyards…

… staying in the most spectacular villa I’ve ever seen…

… FINDING PENGUINS…

… then and climbing Lion’s Head the night before I left…

… it was a pretty full-on few days. We also managed to squeeze in a visit to the University of Cape Town’s “SpaceLab” and catchup it’s director (and ISU faculty) Professor Peter Martinez, as well as Mexican food & margaritas with Dr Carla Sharp from the Square Kilometer Array too. Probably unsurprisingly though, I’m on a plane out of South Africa already – we’ve just started our descent into Addis Ababa, so I’ll get to hang out in Ethiopia for 7 hours before heading onto London and straight into the Space Innovation Congress!

Like I said: 2016 is going to be bat-shit insane… and I wouldn’t have it any other way.
