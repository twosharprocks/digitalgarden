About#

Description: May have more sumps (See DiverNet article)

Where it is & what it is
On the WA Nullarbor near Cocklebiddy; entrance on the lip of a doline with a long haul down to water. Expect a dry entrance slope through breakdown to a lake, then multiple sumps and at least one intervening airspace.

  • Steep entrance → main chamber → lake → ~190 m sump through a phreatic maze → large dry chamber (~80 m) with another lake at far end. Beyond, Glacier Lake reportedly sumps ~130 m to a ~30 m dia. rock-pile chamber that’s blocked ~35 m above water (suggests a collapsed dome with potential bypasses).

  • Divers consistently report high CO₂ in the intervening air chamber—treat as hazardous; many traverse with regs in.

  • Noted for subaqueous speleothems and a distinct halocline in parts—mixing-zone dissolution/precipitation features are documented across Nullarbor lakes, including at Tommy’s.

  • ACKMA/ASF items: the Tommy Grahams Cave Visitor Book (Buzzacott) has site history, entrance/doline details, and access notes—handy for on-the-ground orientation.

Potential Leads#

  1. Rock-pile chamber above Glacier Lake
    A terminal rock-pile that blocks at ~+35 m above the lake screams collapse-dome with possible bypass tubes or slot chimneys on the margins. Sweep the edges of the talus where wall meets debris; look for clean, eddy-scoured floor patches, perched sediment traps, or faint drafts across the air–water interface if you can access the higher levels. Underwater, check for under-cut scallops heading beneath the pile.

  2. Phreatic-maze junctions on the 190 m sump
    Maze nodes often hide low, tube-mouth “feet” (half-filled phreatic notches) offset from the main line. Swim slow, run raking light along the lower wall 0.5–1.2 m off floor, and watch scallop asymmetry to follow paleo-flow into blind alcoves—those alcoves are classic seed points for “new” side tubes.

  3. Halocline horizons
    Rowling and later Nullarbor syntheses note mixing-zone corrosion above haloclines and calcite deposition below—that interface can amplify lateral porosity. Cruise the halocline with minimal wake; search for horizontal undercuts or solution bevels that parallel the lake level, especially on outer bends of the passage where shear is higher.

  4. Breakdown-shadow voids in the main chamber
    At the base of the entrance slope and first lake, scan behind/under large collapse blocks for inset tubes. Many teams rush past here—slow ceiling/upper-wall passes with oblique light often reveal cupolas or small rising feeders into the chamber roof. (tdisdi.com)

  5. Air-chamber margins (CO₂ caution)
    High CO₂ discourages exploration; that also means less traffic. If you do enter (with proper monitoring), check condensation-corrosion features and roof pockets for perched tubes that reconnect underwater downslope. Keep exposure short and staged. (oxy-doc.com)

  • Photogrammetry Capture: ceilings first, then upper walls, then floors on exit.
