For the last few weeks I’ve been asking myself a question that I’ve never really onside red before - who do I want to be in 20 years time? Chatting to Mark Tozer over drinks in Glenelg, he casually mentioned that he felt he had “3 good years of diving” left before he turned 60 and would need to reign in some of the deeper and higher risk dives he currently likes to do. Given how short term I’ve always seen my existence, it’s really the first time I’ve stopped and asked “Who do I want to be at 60?” or even asked what kind of life I want to be living in 10 years time since I was shortlisted for Mars One. And one way that I’ve managed the tension involved with being in a highly claustrophic office environment for my current job has been to ask this question and then research the lives of people I might consider Role models for when I’m 60.

Jill Heinerth seems almost too obvious as role models given the way she works as a highly visible diving storyteller, and I guess Jacques Cousteau could be categorised in a similar manner. But that doesn’t take away from the fact that they’re both excellent examples of the kind of adventurous, aquaphillic storytellers that I want to aspire to and emulate.

Explorers like George Mallory and Ernest Shackleton feel like they belong in a very different category - one that belongs to men from a very specific time, and men who took risks doing things they knew were dangerous but absolutely worth it. They were also both more cautious than they may appear on the surface… But they were still willing to risk it all for the adventure. Like them, you’re not completely fearless… but you do have the ability to override your fears in the pursuit of something new and extraordinary.

Scientists like Carl Sagan and Richard Feynman inspire me too, but I have already chosen a path away from academia. Both of these men were rogues in their own way, but they each chose to fight their way through the same academic path that I’ve already committed to stay away from. So I aspire to be like the men they eventually turned into, rather than following the paths they took to become the men who inspire me now.

And the likes of Richard Attenborough and Ranaulph Fiennes both intrigue me, but I feel like I need to research both of these remarkable men a lot more than I have. Even skimming the Wikipedia entry for Ranaulph Fiennes just now tells me he’s always been an absolute ratbag, an that I have far more in common with him than I may have initially believed. Richard Attenborough deserves a lot more attention than I’ve probably given him too, but maybe that’s a research project for tomorrow. What’s most important is that these men have utterly thrown themselves into what they’re doing and what they are passionate about, and I have every reason to do the same thing with the utter compulsion I experience with cave diving.

So take what you want to take from each of these amazing people and create your own life over the next 20 years - you may find that 60 is just the beginning, but for someone like you who’s never looked much past a single year they are all excellent targets to aim for over the next 20 years.
