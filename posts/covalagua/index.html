LOCATION : Revilla de Pomar , Palencia.

INTRODUCTION On an extensive area of ​​páramo, an authentic rosary of sinkholes of great size scattered without apparent order throughout its surface calls our attention; Before our eyes opens a singular place known as Lora de Valdivia, a name that it takes from the picturesque Ivia River Valley. This river has its source in the central-western border of said páramo through Covalagua, an important karstic upwelling that in turn gives its name to the Natural Area that from Pomar de Valdivia (Palencia) extends east to the border with the provinces. Burgos and Cantabria.

Along with this great Lora, it is worth noting to the E, another much smaller one called Lora Alta, and which actually forms part of the previous one, although superficially a slight blow of time seems to want to divide them. The West half of this Lora is Palentina, while the East is Burgos.

In Loras Alta and Valdivia, and despite the abundant exokarstic manifestations and therefore favorable conditions for the existence of important underground networks, at a speleological level only three cavities stand out, to which must be added a fourth, recently discovered:

tourist entrance- Upwelling of COVALAGUA (source of the Ivia river) It is an active cavity developed fundamentally on stratification planes and in general with a predominance of Alentejo sections and flat roofs. It consists of a partially flooded main conduit that ends in a siphon, and a small side gallery, near the entrance, which likewise ends in a siphon. Its development is just over 200m. At its exit several waterfalls stand out with showy tuffaceous terraces, formed by the decalcification of the waters drained by the upwelling. Its environment is conditioned for tourism with viewpoints, paths and bridges. (In Exploration)

CAVITY SITUATION

GEOGRAPHICAL AND GEOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK; HYDROGEOLOGY ( same as for Fuente de la Cueva ) La Lora de Valdivia and Lora Alta are located to the south of the Cantabrian mountain range, and form a small part of a large páramo sector ( Lora de la Lora structural platform ) that with a WNW-ESE orientation extend from the northeast of the province of Palencia to the northwestern area of ​​that of Burgos. Its main morphological characteristic is that of an extensive elevated platform, practically horizontal and that structurally constitutes an inverted relief as it is formed by a hanging syncline, where the elevated levels correspond to the core of the syncline.

The materials on which it develops are predominantly calcareous and belong to the Cretaceous series, being constituted by sandy limestones, oolitic limestones and limestones with rudists (middle-upper Turonian - Coniacian) having as impermeable base level, hydrologically speaking, a layer of sandy loams of the lower Turonense.

The surface of both Loras is approximately 1,400 Ha and constitutes the main recharge area of ​​this aquifer, as the limestone materials that emerge show intense fracturing as well as a notable profusion of sinkholes, which together promote rapid infiltration. The underground circulation of water is fundamentally conditioned by Covalagua mouththe underlying layer of marls and by the syncline structure, whose axis crosses from WNW to ESE lasLoras. This syncline structure also presents small transverse anticlinal undulations in the SSO-NNE direction, which possibly determines that in the Lora de Valdivia there is, roughly, a drainage hydrogeological divide, with a part of the water flowing towards the W (Ivia valley). and another to the SE.

Thus, the two main discharge points of this aquifer are, to the WEST, the Covalagua Upwelling - Source of the Ivia River - and to the EAST, the so-called Fuente de la Cueva - Source of the Mundilla River -. The first pours its waters into the Duero hydrographic network, while the second does so into the Ebro. In addition, there are several intermittent springs bordering both Loras that are located at similar levels, as they emerge at the contact between the limestone and the lower section. of marls.

HISTORY OF THE EXPLORATIONS The Covalagua Spring is surely known from yesteryear by the inhabitants of the area, as the waters that flow from it form a beautiful set of waterfalls with showy tuffaceous deposits. At the speleological level, the first incursions and exploration works were carried out by the Humboldt Group, who also carried out a topography of the cavity as well as a detailed description of it (Alcalde Crespo G.: 1997 La Lora, Tomo I La Montaña Palencia) The explorations are stopped in two siphons, one upstream at the end of the main gallery, and another located in a small side gallery near the entrance. Its development is about 200m.

advancing siphon inNEW EXPLORATIONS (2009-2011) AND BRIEF DESCRIPTION (This exploration has been carried out by the NUBIS, FLASH, GEODA, MINAS and GAEM interclub and has had the support of CEFME as well as the collaboration of members of GET, GUIAS and ISER ) In 2005, during the data collection and bibliographic consultations related to the exploration works carried out in Fuente Deshondonada ( Rebanal de las Llantas, Palencia ) an area with a striking name caught our attention, from a point of view speleological, located a few kilometers from Aguilar de Campoo: - Covalagua Natural Space -.

If in general this area seemed very promising at the exploration level, given its peculiar characteristics, it was even more so because it had a very interesting resurgent cavity ending in a siphon (Covalagua Upwelling ). However, the pending exploration work in other areas and cavities kept “stand-by” plans to inspect this cavity and especially its surroundings.beautiful setting

Year 2009; At the end of 2009, colleagues from the NUBIS Speleology Club (Palencia) contacted us, since in addition to having topographed the Covalagua upwelling, they were interested in exploring the final siphon, which they had already preliminarily inspected with equipment immersion. Thus, in December a team made up of members of the NUBIS , MINAS , GEODA , FLASH and GAEM clubs visited the upwelling, with the intention of exploring its final siphon.

The results of this first visit were the exploration and topography of 355 meters of submerged conduits, with a point (maximum penetration ) of 257m. In its first few metres, the siphon had large dimensions, with sections similar to the area before it, that is, the widths prevailing over the heights (6mx1.5m). From 90 meters (bubble) and after leaving three lateral branches, two of which form a loop, the gallery continued south through a long straight section of small section developed in favor of a NS-direction fracture. From here, the conduit adjusted to a new series of fractures in a predominantly WW-ENE direction, being able to progress some 97m more (257m) until finally running into a collapse that obstructed the passage.

It is worth noting the rapid response of the cavity to rainfall: from one day to the next (rainy weather) the level rose about 40cm, submerging all the equipment left at the end of the cavity.

Year 2010; In a new exploration it is possible to force the collapse (257m) after moving several flakes; From here the siphon progress resumed, until another obstacle appeared again, this time made up of a couple of large blocks. The attempt to force a narrow rolling mill under the blocks only made it possible to advance 5m. At this point, the shallow depth of the duct stands out (-1.5m).

Motivated by the possibility of surfacing, exploration of the siphon soon resumed, this time equipped with shovels to try to break through the narrow laminator, taking advantage of the fact that its bottom is gravel. Upon reaching the top and the level of the siphon being slightly lower, it is observed on the ceiling, on the right side of the blocks, that there is a surface in the distance. After several attempts to force the small gap between the blocks and the wall, it is finally possible to pass. Unfortunately the surface is nothing more than an air chamber in which the head barely fits. Crawling over the blocks, it is found that 10m later the conduit is siphoned again.

A quick incursion confirms that the siphon continues, although with End of the vadose zone. siphon startvery small vertical dimensions, mainly due to the large deposit of gravel and pebbles that occupy the bottom. Finally, exploration is stopped at 330m/-5m at the beginning of a very low rolling mill.

In general, the very little average depth reached in the siphon stands out (which is an advantage) and the smallunevenness of the cavity, which reflects the slight dip of the strata on which it develops. Thus, we find that from the mouth (elevation 0) and after a subhorizontal section, the gallery rises slightly to +3.4m at the start of the siphon. From this point, the siphon descends very gently, reaching a maximum depth (height of the water column) of 6m, a level that is located about 2.59m below the inlet.

During a dive to graphically document the siphon, a small hanging aerial gallery is located on the small bubble located at 87m; Explored with the hope that it would communicate with a higher level, it finally became impenetrable after just over 20m of travel.

Year 2011; In view of how uncooperative the final siphon of the cavity was, it was decided to look back at the inhospitable siphon located at the end of the small side gallery near the entrance (Siphon I downstream). After inspecting it, bottle in hand, it was It progresses through a circular duct 1m in diameter, breaking the surface after just 9m of travel. On the other side there is access to a vadose gallery with good prospects for continuation. Forty meters later, these “promising” perspectives were cut short: the duct ended up bifurcating: to the left in an impenetrable loss, and to the right in a very narrow, but penetrable, semi-flooded duct.title

This small side gallery near the entrance is nothing more than a loss of water emitted by the upwelling and in principle it is assumed that the waters that circulate through this side gallery end up emerging on the outer slope, at some point close to the waterfalls. of Toba located under the entrance of the cavity.

Siphon straight line NSOn a new visit to topograph this area, the small semi-flooded conduit is forced, until it ends up being siphoned (Siphon II downstream). Despite the unfriendly nature of this new siphon, it was decided to try their luck because although very narrow, it is penetrable and has water circulation. Bottle in hand again, it is possible to advance about 5m to a small circular room, continuing the conduit from here with even smaller dimensions, if possible; Intrigued to know where this conduit will end, the continuation from here is forced for another 20m until prudence advises starting the return (reverse) The small conduit, despite having dimensions of just 60 x 60cm, has continuity …

Upstream, a new point is carried out in the final siphon, beyond 330m. The small rolling mill was presented with a width of about 2m but very little height; As soon as a little more than ten meters of thread has been deployed, a new collapse appears. The strong current encourages them to try to force this new obstacle, succeeding with great difficulty. On the other side, the rolling mill increases its height slightly and can easily progress another 20m, until it begins to decrease again. Upon reaching 384m, barely 3m deep, it was decided to stop the exploration, having already been semi-embedded for several meters due to the meager 40cm height of the conduit. Ahead it looks like it continues…

EXPLORATION IN PROGRESS: Currently the explored and topographed development of Covalagua is 854 m . Exploration is ongoing, both upstream in the final mill of the siphon (384m) and downstream in the small siphon II. In the first, it is expected to continue forcing this laminator, perhaps resorting to underwater clearance; In the second, and despite its delicate exploration, it is intended to gain meters little by little (familiarization) because it is an enigma where these waters are going to stop, which are already moving away from the outer upwelling zone.
