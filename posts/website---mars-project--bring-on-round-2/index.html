As usual for this time of year, it’s been pretty quiet on the Mighty Ginge for the last few months. And as usual, I’ll be making grand claims of regular updates here in the coming year. Unusually though, there may actually be a little substance to this year’s claim. Because instead of trying to write an entertaining comedy post here each week, 2014 has started very differently to any other for me. Or maybe more importantly, 2013 ended with some of the most amazing news I could have hoped for.

On December 30th 2013, Mars One notified me that I am one of 1058 astronaut applicants to progress to their second round of selections. Over 200,000 people applied to the program, so being asked to be part of the next selection round is a huge honour.

Details are expected to follow in a January 6th email, but round 2 is slated as the “Regional Selection” round: all of the 1058 round 2 applicants will be put through a series of televised physical, aptitude and teamwork tests to help Mars One to narrow the selection field further. The “International Selection” round will follow, all ahead of the June 2015 announcement of between 24-40 astronaut-trainees. These trainees will be paid employees of Mars One, spending the following 9-10 years training & competing to be the first team of 4 on Mars.

Understandably the media has gone pretty wild for all of this, particularly the selection of less than 0.5% of the initial applicants to progress to round 2. I spoke to Peter and Tony on Melbourne 3AW’s Breakfast show yesterday (January 2nd), holding my eyes open at 3:30 in the morning because of the time difference! For the many that weren’t selected to round 2: I wish we could all go. There are some really great people who haven’t moved on to round 2, and while I’m sure Mars One have their reasons it’s still heart-breaking. I’ve been tweeting back and forth with former NASA astronaut & ISS-resident Clayton Anderson (@astro_clay) since the December 22nd spacewalk to replace an ammonia pump on the ISS, and he’s a shining example that being knocked back initially often makes you a stronger applicant in the long run. He was rejected 15 times by NASA before he was successful on his 16th, and as he’s said “My 15 applications served me well; patience/passion and never quitting are the keys”. I only hope that many of the fantastic people like Austin Bradley and Dave Hunter who unfortunately didn’t get through will reapply when applications next open.

In the meantime Mars One themselves are doing amazing work, announcing on December 10th they’ve secured contracts with both Lockheed Martin and Surrey Satellites for concept studies on their 2018 robotic mission. They also announced their first Indiegogo campaign to provide a chance for the public to contribute and help fund the robotic lander and communication satellite they hope to launch to Mars in 2018. Most of Mars One’s funding is coming from private investment and media deals, but this is a great way for people all over the world to help contribute and play their own small part in making the colonisation of Mars a reality. The campaign has already raised over $110,000 (as of January 3rd), and I hope you’ll all contribute to helping Mars One reach it’s fundraising goal of $400,000.
