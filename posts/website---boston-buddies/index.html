Flying into the US is always an entertaining experience. Travelling with just two carry on bags certainly makes the whole luggage side of things a lot simpler, and customs have never been an issue, but I’ve had mixed results with immigration every time. Things didn’t get off to a great start when the folks in Iceland wouldn’t even let me get on the plane because my electronic visa wasn’t being recognised.

After a bundle of confused phone calls I realised I’d provided them with my old passport number – the one I’d flown on for 10 years till it expired mid last year – because I’d used it enough to memorise the number and didn’t recognise the passport number had changed. The bigger question is how I got out of Australia, through South Africa, the UK, Ireland & Iceland, all without anyone else noticing… but without thinking about that too much, once I got my numbers straight leaving Iceland there was no issue. Not a great start before flying to the US for 2 months though.

US immigration turned out to be a breeze, probably because I freaked out right before the flight from Iceland & gathered together every possible piece of info on my trip that I thought they might ask about at the immigration desk. So without too much trouble I was on a bus and on my way to downtown Boston! The vague US plan has been to travel down both the East & West coasts entirely by train: stopping along the way to meet as many Mars One candidates and see as many space things as possible, while getting to watch the country side go by from a train carriage along the way. And while I know the West coast pretty well & have plenty of friends there, my only experience with the East has been a couple of very brief stop-overs in New York.

Boston was completely new, and a fairly sharp reminder that things are just straight up different in the US. Like adding tax and tipping. What the hell America Showing me a price, THEN adding tax AND a tip is bordering on rude. Tell me what the whole thing is going to cost upfront, and pay your dining staff minimum wage or more. You know, like everyone else on the planet does. And while we’re at it: imperial measurement. WHAT. THE. FUCK. You know that the only countries that haven’t switched over primarily to Metric are the US, Myanmar & Liberia, right

It’s like the founding fathers arbitrarily set fire to a bunch of stray cats in a park, then measured how far they ran before they put themselves out or died, and used those measurements to define different distances (eg. one flaming cat furlong) then multiplied that by the number of cats who survived to define larger measurements (eg. the total distance run by the 14 cats who survived the flaming is equal to 1 US flaming cat mile). I can only guess Fahrenheit is how many flaming cats it takes to melt American cheese or something; that weights are measured with how much fur the cats lost through this whole process (1 burnt cat fur pound); and fluids are measured in units of how much milk the cats drank during the partial lunar eclipse in Virgo a month afterwards (1 flaming cat lunar Virgo gallon).

Seriously: you lost a spaceship mixing up imperial & metric, and accepted a 93m tall copper statue & Freedom Fries from the French – why not do everyone a favour and borrow their system of measurement too?

Ahem Rant over, moving on…

Truth be told Boston itself didn’t hold a lot of appeal, it was only a very brief visit to catch my breath after Iceland and see a few folks who live there. After a day in the hostel trying to kill a lingering cold, it was great being able to catch up with Alex: an old friend I hadn’t seen in 10 years, who in one of those quirks of circumstance now does post-doctoral research into cloud-formation on Mars!

Alex and I had barely stayed in touch since our physics undergrad days when she was on exchange in Perth, so it was all a little bizarre catching up for a few hours to chat about all the weird and wonderful things we’ve both done over the last 10 years. Even weirder sitting in a cafe at MIT and looking back to see how a fairly even mix of hard work & pure chance have led each of us to where we are respectively now.

We couldn’t spend too long reminiscing though – she had a 1-year-old bub to get back to, and I had a meeting with the folks from Boston’s Museum of Science! Given all the publicity around the #bringhimhome event and Cosmic Nomad, the museum had reached out to see if there was a way for us to collaborate on some sort of public event, and while nothing completely solid has come out of the meeting just yet it was fantastic being able to talk to folks who are passionate about science communication for an hour about the challenges of colonising Mars. They also invited me to roam their gorgeous museum afterwards…

…where I found the small but awesome Gemini/Apollo exhibit full of signed memorabilia and a moon rock…

… and when they kicked me out at closing time I spotted this awesome guy near the adjoining planetarium.

Apparently he ran the Boston marathon…

There wasn’t much time to hang out with Toby the Long-Distance Running T-Rex though, as there were 4 other Mars One candidates converging on a Mexican restaurant just up the road!

(L-R) Sara, myself, Yari, R Daniel & Peter

Sara, Yari, R Daniel, and Peter had all cleared their schedules so the 5 of us could meet up, eat amazing Mexican food, and so I could get them to add their signatures to my Mars One book. It’s always great meeting fellow candidates and hearing their motivations for applying & staying with Mars One – every time I’ve met other candidates it’s been a fantastic reminder that they’re all extraordinary people trying to do something literally out-of-this-world. But the meetup in Boston held a few surprises too: a couple of the candidates started a new trend by writing messages in the book rather than just signing it, which was a brilliant addition to what might have just been a book with a collection of signatures in it otherwise. But the biggest surprise was finding out that Yari has been looking after the one shirt:

** **Former Mars One candidate Randy Bevins had a shirt printed to say “Bas is sending ME to Mars!” a few years back, and since then it’s been mailed around the world to candidates for them to get a photo in it, along with a notebook to track its progress. I’d tried to catch up with the shirt about 18 months ago when it looked like it might be posted to Australia, but it completely dropped off the radar and we’d heard little about it since. So having Yari turn up with it was completely unexpected, and it was an amazing surprise to cross paths with a little piece of Mars One folklore.

After Peter very kindly put me up for the night at his house and dropped me at the station the next morning, it was time to start my epic journey down the coast… with a relatively short 5-hour trip to New York! The trip was long enough to carve a substantial chunk out of the relentless emails, but not long enough to really get on top of it. But if I’m brutally honest I really wasn’t looking forward to New York at all – a month into the oversea leg of the tour I could feel unresolved issues catching up with me, and I started to struggle both emotionally and physically.

Without going into a lot of detail, I had a lot of things end in the final months of 2015: I left the first stable job I’ve had in years, I moved out of the first sharehouse I’d been in since 2008, and a pretty life-changing relationship petered out and died without much resolution. In the months that followed I had a whole raft of fresh starts though: I began working as a TA for the International Space University, the Cosmic Nomad world tour kicked off, and I’ve met a bundle of truly wonderful new people who I’ve formed different relationships with, but all based on strong mutual respect (if not love) as well as clear communication and honest expectations.

But while I had no hesitation leaving my job in Melbourne (I’ve left plenty before) or living with no fixed address to travel the world performing comedy & doing space stuff (actually a dream come true), it’s definitely felt like I’ve been carrying a wound in the relationship department that I’ve been desperately trying to cover up since last year. I’d told myself at the time that I’d learnt a lot from it – and I really did. The issue was that even though in many ways I’d learnt what I needed then tried to move on, there was still an unresolved & lingering hurt associated with all of it.

In one of those weird cosmic coincidences, about a month ago I met someone in Cork that had gone through a similar experience at roughly the same time, and was now opening herself up to pursue whatever opportunity felt right to her intuition – exactly the way I’ve been trying to operate. The entire afternoon & most of the evening disappeared as we chatted about life, the universe and everything; and besides realising that I’d hidden away some key parts of my personality, it also dug up a lot of the hurt from 2015 that I’d tried to bury and reminded me that I’d had a lot of the same lessons when another major relationship went belly-up in 2010.

I had a little time in Dublin to think about it, Iceland was 4 days by myself to get away from outside influences and go through a lot of what had resurfaced, and besides the chat with Alex my time in Boston was so rushed I barely stopped to think. But New York became the focal point for all of it: I was in the city that this ex wanted to bring her Mum to see Broadway shows, where we’d talked about touring together – both of us performing standup in some of NYC’s classic comedy clubs.

And as usually happens when you’re dealing with a lot from having the emotional band-aid ripped off, my health went to shit at the same time. I’d been getting a sore throat the day I left Iceland (not related to the ice river swim, before any of you armchair web doctors chime in), but now it seized up to the point where I couldn’t sleep at night because of the pain of swallowing, and was pairing up with a lovely headache every morning. Frankly everything felt pretty bloody average.Unwilling to be a miserable git for too long though, I loaded up on lemsip and forced myself out of the hostel to see Times Square…

Less than amusingly, I had to walk along Broadway to get here

…before heading to the USS Intrepid…

… where I’d get to see my first space shuttle – the Enterprise.

She may never have orbitted, but Space Shuttle Enterprise was key in the aerodynamics tests to validate the entire Space Shuttle program. USS Intrepid also had with a flown Soyuz capsule, that they keep under the tail of the Enterprise in the background for size comparison.

Spurred on by an amazing morning at the Intrepid, I headed uptown to the Rose Center for Earth & Space – where Neil DeGrasse Tyson is Director of the Hayden Planetarium.

I didn’t see Neil, but I did see another moon rock – this one collected by Dave Scott during Apollo 15

I also very nearly bought a life-size Triceratops plush toy because fuck you it’s majestic.

The day out in the cold New York air got to me though, and besides a few ventures to the local Chinese take-away I mostly stayed in the hostel feeling shit, trying to recover, and fighting through a long-overdue email inbox.

It was still worth going to New York. But given everything that had been dredged up in a few weeks before, along with all the baggage attached to being in New York, it was even better knowing that the worst part of this entire global tour was undoubtedly over as I climbed onto the train to Washington DC. With all that baggage staying in New York where it belonged.

I probably could have skipped it and gone straight from Boston to DC, but that would have meant continuing to ignore what’s been eating me for months… as well as missing out on seeing a fucking spaceship. And my new relationship rules are if you can’t talk about something that’s troubling you, or if they’re a barrier seeing a spaceship, then they don’t deserve to have an influence in your life. The other rule is if they don’t at least giggle at Rick & Morty or Airplane! the first time they see it then you’re straight-up wasting your time.

I ended a relationship once mostly because a girl didn’t laugh in the first 15 mins of this movie

New York was the bit on the rollercoaster where it pauses upside-down during a loop-de-loop. And while you’re hanging from your chair feeling sick, all the shit you should have left behind falls out of your pockets for the carnies to collect. Seconds after you tip over the balance point you’re racing through the loops and turns again, and there’s no point thinking about what fell out – you’ve let go of the shit that you thought was so precious before, and will hopefully learn not to bring it with you on the ride next time… or better yet, not carry it at all.

As you’d expect though, this story gets a lot better. Between getting to DC to meet more Mars One candidates, seeing another space shuttle, an SR-71 Blackbird, and the Apollo 11 command module (all before I got to Florida to embrace the space madness on a whole new level there) you’ll probably understand why the last two weeks of this trip have finally started to feel like this is my trip now, without the emotional baggage… and why I fell so far behind with these updates, trying to process it all of that while on the move through a foreign country.

Washington DC and Florida up next 😉
