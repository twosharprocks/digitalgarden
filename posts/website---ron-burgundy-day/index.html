As you might have guessed from the tone of the posts since we’ve restarted, this isn’t really the time of year I feel like spreading love and joy (jove? loy?) with the world. We’re coming down off the high of all the family love of Christmas, and the fuzzy memories of our crazy New Year’s antics are starting to fade. So what do we have to look forward to now?

Valentine’s Day…

Now, I get it – it’s a chance for people to celebrate their love for their partner through buying chocolates, flowers, obscene soft toys – not the cool soft toys I collect, but little teddy bears holding red hearts – and a variety of other crap to “show you care”. But for singles like yours truly, I DREAD Valentine’s day. It’s not because most women in the U.K. and Australia treat ginger hair like it’s a symptom of the Ebola virus; nor is it because I’m a bitter, hateful little man that pours scorn on anyone who’s open about love – quite the opposite.

Clearly, I’m here to be all rainbows & lollipops

It’s because it turns out most people regularly lie in relationships to make them “work” anyway, and Valentine’s day is the day these lies and emotional misgivings culminate in some sort of squishy dreamland where couples are convinced they’re in the “perfect relationship”. “Noooo, honey! Ofcourse you don’t look fat in that leopard-print crop top!”, “Curry chips and cider on the beach? You’re such a romantic!”, and my personal favourite: “OFCOURSE I love you Josh!”

Not that we should really be surprised it’s the focal point of so much emotional fabrication. If you trace back the traditions and history of Valentine’s Day, it basically all comes from 1382 when some musty old English poet decided to steal a random saint’s fest day and arbitarily make it a day for lovers just because the king was getting married. So I’ve decided if that guy can steal a Roman festival, and Pippa Middleton’s bum can steal an entire royal wedding, then I’m allowed to steal Februrary 7th and rename it “Ron Burgundy Day” in honour of Ron Burgundy from Anchorman.

Ladies, please refrain from throwing your panties at the screen

Ron Burgundy showed no fear in telling women what he felt about them – as such “Ron Burgundy Day” is about singles telling each other exactly how they feel… in their pants. Personally, I think we just need to be in touch with how we’re really feeling, without the over-the-top gooey bullshit. Sexual testimony, Erotic exposition – whatever you want to call it. And it’s exactly a week before Valentine’s Day, in the hope all this “amourous honesty” will splash over and stop Valentine’s Day being so cheesy and false.

There are a few basic traditions to Ron Burgundy Day – obviously, the traditional greeting of the day is “Just going to put it out there… I want to be on you” – which you can shorten to simply “I want to be on you”. However if someone greets you in this manner on Februrary 7th, tradition dictates there are only three ways you’re permitted to respond;

1st – Scream “TAKE ME TO PLEASURE TOWN!”
Now this may seem a little too eager. But lets be honest – if you’re going to put out, why wait on formalities?

“Look, the most glorious rainbow ever” “Oh! Do me on it!”

2nd – “When in Rome!”
Quite clearly, this phrase makes no sense in the context – which perfectly reflects your initial uncertainty, but wary willingness to see where thing go…

3rd – “I’m sorry, my religion prevents it”
We need to respect people’s religious beliefs. And it might be your religion not to sleep with anyone except your partner. It might also be your religion to not ever sleep with anyone who walks up and says “I want to be on you” – whatever. It’s your religion. But if you DO end up talking and find they’re interesting, you can’t sleep with them till after midnight. I’m sorry, but there are traditions to be respected here. So till midnight, absolutely nothing beyond mutual masturbation.

Also, since this is the only response that automatically precludes sex, it makes Ron Burgundy Day a very exciting time to be an atheist.

“I want to be… ” “Sorry, I think I just found Jesus”

And while Valentine’s Day gladly excludes singles, Ron Burgundy Day has no such discrimination against couples – just be honest with your partner about what you want! Ladies, tell him it’s not a headache – it’s that the thought of him thrusting away for 3 and a half minutes then rolling off and falling asleep makes you feel physically ill. Men, ask your lady to put the toilet seat back up once she’s finished.

The only thing I request… nay, demand: You HAVE to wear a condom. I don’t care if “putting it on will ruin the mood”, or if you’re fatally allergic to latex – if I’m giving you a sex-based holiday tradition, I am NOT taking the blame when 9 months later November 7th rolls around and everyone is popping out little passive-aggressive Scorpio babies.

So in conclusion;

  • There’s a Facebook event for Ron Burgundy Day – I highly recommend taking part, flirting with each other on the wall, then getting out there and telling people you want to be on them. God knows I will be.

  • I’ve written a short ukulele serenade in celebration of Ron Burgundy Day. Traditionally a serenade is a song about how beautiful a woman is and how the singer isn’t worthy of her. And it’s not done on a ukulele (aka The Guitar Made for Hobbits). But because it’s written in the spirit of Ron Burgundy Day, this a serenade is more about being open and honest about who I am, and letting the ladies out there decide once they have all the facts. Gentlemen, feel free to copy it onto a tape and play it through a boombox, while standing outside a girl’s window (preferably in the rain). It’s not about you, but if she’s still up for it after hearing this you’re onto a winner. Full and final disclosure – I stole all the chords from a match.com advert.

Ladies, please don’t flood the comments section all at once – The Ron Burgundy Day Serenade
