Adelaide (September 27-October 1st)

Make no mistake – I’ve done the flight between London & Australia numerous times now. In 2010 alone I did it 6 times. What I hadn’tdone befor was fly into Australia at 8am on grand final day and then experience the sinking feeling that everything is closed and you can’t check in till 2pm… Nor have I ever called my Mum immediately after one of these flights and babbled on deliriously outside a Woolworths.

Anyway, I did eventually manage to check-in and get some sleep, even in a 6 bed dorm – never doubt the power of the classic “Sleep deprivation & industrial earplugs” combo. It was just as well, as the next morning I needed to be up to help organise Australia’s first SpaceUp conference, hosted by Flinders University.

A couple of my old course mates from the ISU Southern Hemisphere program had decided earlier in the year to run it, and had asked me help out with social media and coordinating volunteers on the day. We had some fantastic speakers throughout the day, from students to asteroid miners, defence personnel to commercial space start-up operators.

Mark Sonter from Deep Space Industries, talking about asteroid mining

Tim Parsons from Delta 2.0, discussing the future of commercial space start-ups in Australia

SpaceUp was also the first opportunity I’ve had to discuss my ongoing work with UWA Motorsport to develop the Helena payload for Mars One’s 2018 lander, as well as hosting Australia’s first Mars One astronaut candidate meetup.

Seated left to right: Mars One candidates J. Henry Ledyard, Dianne McGrath and Tarrant Skorji

Having emailed all the candidates in Australia at different times, it was great to finally meet a bunch of them in person at once: special thanks to J. Henry Ledyard, Dianne McGrath, and Tarrant Skorji for being part of the panel, and to fellow candidate Tristan Perkins for coming along too.

The entire conference was a huge endeavour, with both Michael Leslie &Rodrigo Rodrigues working their hearts out to make it the massive success it was. After all the talks were done and the place cleaned, we finished the with post-conference drinks and a poetry reading from my National Science week buddy Bronwyn Lovell - sharing the latest draft of her verse novel based on Mars One, Migration.

Bronwyn Lovell reading sections of Migration

And the day after the SpaceUp conference? That’s when the Australian Space Research Conference (ASRC) started. In fairness the ASRC could have been a nice change of pace from the previous 4 weeks of chaos: food and coffee abounded, as an ISU/UniSA student I still had wifi access in the lecture theatres, and you could pick and choose which presentations suited you best. But why would I take a chance to slow down?

Day 1 (Monday) of the conference was reasonably chilled out (and gave me a chance to answer a few urgent emails), but it wasn’t to last. Day 2 (Tuesday) of the conference was the science communication stream, so I was back out there again: talking about all my National Science Week events, presenting the success I’d had at integrating comedians and poets into space science communication, as well as talking about the incredible outreach potential of the Mars One project.

Discussing my National Science Week events and how I’ve used Mars One as a “Big Ass Hook” for space science outreach

Glad to say quite a few handy contacts were made, I just wish I’d had time to follow them all up since! After the Tuesday night conference dinner and some very misguided late-night karaoke, I skipped out on the third and final day of the ASRC to get myself to Newcastle in time for…

National Young Writer’s Festival (NYWF) Newcastle (October 2nd – 5th)

Newcastle – a magical town of coal & fake wooden clock towers

The trip to Newcastle was bliss. Multiple theories exist as to why, but I suspect it’s either because I was still suffering the effects of karaoke and at this point ANY ESCAPE FROM ADELAIDE WOULD DO… or because the flight to Sydney was short, I caught the train without rushing OR waiting, and the train ride to Newcastle is BRAIN-MELTINGLY PRETTY.

LOOK! LOOK HOW PRETTY THIS IS! Ignore the power lines and the window reflections… LOOK AT THE PRETTY!

There was the added bonus of staying in a hostel organised by the wonderful folks from NYWF that was just off the beach. So while I was there I went running (most) mornings along this:

PREEEEEEETTY

I’d hit Newcastle on the Wednesday – a day before the festival technically started – so I’d finally have a chance to pass out without some early morning event or travel trauma waiting for me the next morning. It’s amazing what happens when you’re not LIVING ON THE GOD DAMN EDGE FOR ONE NIGHT. As it was I had to get up on the Thursday to speak to the folks at 1233 ABC Newcastle anyway… but it was such an amazing interview at the relatively luxurious hour of 11 am that I didn’t mind. There’s a write-up & recording of the interview with Jill Emberson that’s well worth checking out.

On the way back, I managed to find an amazing cafe with great breakfasts and free wifi to finally sort out a few more urgent emails and find out what was going on with the interview Channel 9’s Today show had booked me for in their Sydney studio for the start of World Space Week the coming Monday. The producer I’d been dealing with had gone on holidays a week before, but had assured me that one of her colleagues had taken over. So when I called the Today show’s Executive Producer to say “Hey, I’m in your studio at 8am Monday, what time do you need me there?” and he didn’t know anything about me or the World Space Week segment…

I’d cancelled a school visit in Brisbane and re-arranged my flights in and out of Sydney so I could do an interview that had been confirmed for 5 weeks… needless to say the Today show are off the Christmas card list for the foreseeable future.

I got back to the hostel to find I had to move rooms too, but in the grand scheme of my global movements this was nothing. However, in the grand scheme of the trip’s success it was an awesome turn of events. Seems the festival organisers were grouping a bunch of “quiet writers” into my original room (Room 1) and shifting all the maniac comedians, musicians, journalists & general mischief makers into one big nest of generalised madness in Room 2.

In order of Room 2 appearance: Dylan “Earplugs” Burns, Matt “The Friendly Rocker” Tomich, Lauren “Boklecopter” Bok, Rob “Scared Bret Easton Ellis” Coleman, Claire “The Naked Beagle” Sullivan, Dave “Trivia Warcrime” Warneke, and this guy. Very special mentions to honorary Room 2 members Kaitlyn “Live Tweet” Plyley & Alan “Wolfman” Vaarwerk too.

Dylan & I had gotten in particularly early and gone to the Meet & Greet, where we both stood around awkwardly until I took a step to introduce myself to a lady raiding the corn chips. She asked me what I did, I told her, then she scowled and told me it was the worst pick-up line ever. Which was pretty unkind, but probably not as unkind as what I replied with. Then I felt the overwhelming desire to leave…


By the time I got back to Room 2 Matt had arrived, Lauren checked in right on dinner time, then the 3 of us wandered off to find the ONLY place open for food – a bizarre boutique fish & chips shop with major hipster-attitude. Back at the room Claire & Dave had shown up, while Dylan had escaped the Meet & Greet. So all 6 of us headed up to the Launch Orgy… where I think we ALL wished we’d paid more attention to the event title.

This is two guys launching a literary zine. Not a euphemism

I think quite a few of Room 2 left feeling confused by the entire experience…

All hail the literary dildo…

The next day I had to tell a bunch of writer’s How to Become an Astronaut for two hours, which is considerably more challenging than it sounds when you’re in a 30°+ room. Lauren & Claire came along to keep things rolling with questions, but most of the audience just started at me in a mixture of confusion & heat stroke. Thankfully the Writers Other Jobs panel I did an hour later was air-conditioned, and we had some really great discussion going. Room 2 hero Rob, and honorary member Alan, joined me on the panel with Duncan Felton to talk about the stuff we do when we’re not writing.

So basically  a librarian, an editor, an editorial director and an astronaut candidate talk about their day jobs… Pick the odd one out.

Called it early on the Friday – it was just way too good knowing I was done by 6pm and didn’t have to travel anywhere the next day. In retrospect,  pretty glad I did call it early… because it’d be the last real sleep I’d get for the next 3 days. Or for the 4 days after that too…

The Saturday itself was pretty relaxed – went for a run, had an awesome breakfast and brainstormed some ideas for trying (again) to get Kerbal Space Program into Aussie schools. I bounced around between a few events, before tagging along to see Lauren and Claire represent Room 2 on the Funny Ladies panel with Kara Jensen-Mackinnon & Gen Fricker for what was arguably the best event of the festival.

Newcastle’s tendency to shut down from 3pm – 6pm was starting to become a real issue in the “we missed lunch but need to be on the other side of town for the Intergalactic Ball at 8pm” stakes. Lauren, Claire and I managed to find an open pub with some epic burgers, along with another pub that thinks it’s WAYfunnier than it actually is…

You should totally use it in your RAW Heat, bro!

Saturday night brought the Intergalactic ball, but more importantly it brought the chance for some epic space-inspired costumes. After buying rolls of aluminium foil and getting a mountain of booze and cardboard from a helpful bottle shop owner, the pre-drinking/costume building began. Although in fairness only two of really went all out and posed in front of the biggest K.F.C. in the Southern Hemisphere.

The Intergalactic ball itself was… weird? I don’t know, I’d been having so much fun with the Room 2 Crew I’d forgotten how of place I felt among the other “writers”. I’d also forgotten how out-of-place I am at nightclubs, especially with an al-foil covered cardboard box on my head.

The plastic cups did nothing to quell my overwhelming boredom

I tried to socialise (read: got drunk), but at ~10:30 suggested leaving and found a bunch of Room 2 ready to bail too. Five of us did a runner back to the hostel, got changed then headed down to the beach for late night drinking and ukulele shenanigans till 3am. This would prove to be both awesome (at the time) and a huge mistake (in the morning), as I’d offered to help out at Lauren’s comedy workshop The First Five at 11am… the same morning New South Wales wound their clocks forward coming off Daylight Savings.

Not pictured – me wanting to die

The last thing either of us needed was to be back in the same little bakehouse I’d done my How To Become an Astronaut workshop in while running on 4 hours sleep and a hangover. But Lauren’s workshop was brilliant regardless: the folks who came were all blown away by what they’d produced & enthusiastic to try their material for real. I found out later Lauren had run her first comedy workshop through Laneway Learning – the same guys I’m now looking to run Kerbal Space Program workshops with in Melbourne.

Anyway a bunch of Room 2 were leaving that same afternoon, so after an audio-guided tour to the beach and some epic chicken snacks, there were goodbyes and the bulk of Room 2’s awesomeness left. For the rest of us though, we had to prepare for our final events – Dave with his Epic Word-Nerd Battledome, Matt sexing it up at Play That Sexy Music, and me… ranting about mutant space ants eating the Earth at After the Apocalypse.

‘…and then THOSE ants start eating the OTHER ants, leading to the 3nd Ant War of 2256…”

Ben McKenzie closed the night with an amazing improvised story about a dystopian future filled with energy-efficient fluorescent globes, the NYWF team closed the festival with a great set of speeches… then headed down to the outdoor pools at 2am in the morning during a major storm for a swim. Needless to say I tagged along to squeeze a tiny more out of the 4 days of madness that was the 2014 National Young Writer’s Festival.

Pretty… pretty bloody cold

Sydney (October 6th)

After the Today Show debacle the Friday before, I didn’t have to take the 2:35am train from Newcastle to Sydney for an 8am interview anymore. Instead Dylan and I had a chilled out breakfast, then I wandered down to the station at the far more reasonably timed train at 10am.

Easily the best Eggs Royale I’ve had – THANKS A LOT CHANNEL NINE

Hitting Sydney, I met up with Twitter-pal and science teaching superstar Sally-Anne Robertson & her daughter to check out The Mars Lab at the Powerhouse Museum.

The future on Mars is so bright there’s lens glare

Sally and I traded sci-com war stories while passing some fairly harsh commentary on the exhibits – the Powerhouse generally seems like a fairly “traditional” museum with a few interactive bits, whereas we both actively encourage kids to ask questions and investigate science in a sandbox- type environment rather than just showing them stuff and hoping they’ll care.

That said, I’m very pleased with The Mars Lab – I’d met the team that run it at the ASRC the week before – and I’m really looking forward to being there on November 27th for a Mars One meetup to talk to schools.

By the end of the Powerhouse visit though, I’d had enough – I really just wanted to go home, curl up in a ball and sleep for a month. I said goodbye to Sally and her daughter, headed out to Sydney airport, and made the mistake of drinking a cappuccino followed by a double strength iced coffee.

Keep it together Josh, you’re just tired & having a caffeine crash – there’s no need to cry… *whimpers*

Which is when things got a little weird…

OBEY THE PENGUIN

… and I wound up with a new lemur.

This is Stevie the Space Lemur. HE NEVER BLINKS

By the time my sister picked me up from Brisbane airport, I was pretty much singing to a soft toy.

Brisbane (October 7th – 9th)

The morning after the flight to Brisbane I was heading down to speak at Windaroo Primary, where Wyatt from ABC’s “Life at Nine” series goes to school. I’d contacted Wyatt’s science teacher Rebecca Johnson back in August after I’d seen Wyatt talking about what he wanted to do when he grew up: “An artist, and a scientist… and in the army so I can save people”. Having done all three I thought I might have something to offer.

Although I will never have Richard Dean Anderson’s majestic mullet

The visit was wonderful with the kids asking some amazing questions about Mars One… but almost immediately it all started to fall apart. Not the school – me and any form of travel. First I missed the bus out of Windaroo and waited an hour. Then I missed then connecting bus back to Brisbane. Dinner with my sister was uneventful, but the next morning I was heading down to Toowoomba to speak at the University of Southern Queensland (USQ) and I’d lost my debit card. My sister lent me hers, then I found mine again…

Meeting the team at USQ was great, but pretty surreal too – I’d gone down there believing I had somewhere to stay, only to be informed on arrival there wasn’t even a couch. Bonus. So instead I did my presentation on using comedy, poetry & Stevie the Space Lemur in space science communication, jumped in a car on the way to Brisbane’s Western suburbs with the head of USQ’s Astronomy group for an hour & a half discussion about physics, psychology and philosophy… then spent the next 2 hours on buses trying to get back to my sister’s…

I kept turning around thinking someone was playing a banjo

You’d think the bus-terror would keep me awake, but I’d genuinely hit the wall – from around the time most of Room 2 cleared out on the Sunday afternoon I was on borrowed time, and it ran out after the Windaroo visit. I startled awake as the bus stopped at Roma Street, and I leapt off it with all my worldly possessions… except my wallet which had fallen out of a side pocket as I hustled off the bus. Thankfully I’d kept my passport & an emergency debit card separate so it wasn’t a huge loss, but it was a clear indicator I just needed to go home now please.

My lemurs, ukulele and I would like to leave now

Thankfully, the next morning I did exactly that – landing back in Perth at the end of 6 weeks of near-constant travelling & performing, to sit in my parents’ spa eating cheese toasties and drinking lemon, lime & bitters.

Fuzzy focus instagramming wanker

So much has happened in the 6 weeks following I suspect I’ll need to do another post to catch up to the current chaos, but at least I survived with both my kidneys and made a hell of a lot of friends along the way.

I’d do it all again in a heartbeat… but maybe next time I’ll schedule in a couple of days down time halfway through just so I don’t implode at the end again.

Maybe.
