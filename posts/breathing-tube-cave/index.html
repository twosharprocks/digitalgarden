About#

2021-Aug: MW visited & photographed. Initially mismarked as L517, error identified when yellow Forestry sign with “L300” was found. Definitely worth further investigation. 2023-April: BC and MW visited & videoed, MW climbed inside. 4m solution tube, unlikely to be anything open at the bottom of tight vertical tube, MAYBE return with tripod and winch.

References#

  • LSECR: Page 588 Discovered and named by the author (in the company of Andrew Cox and Vien Bach) on 16 April 1990, after he finally get around to following-up a Woods and Forests Department worker (Bill Peacocky’s tip from the late 1970s, Breathing Tube Cave is entered via a very sandy, sloping and rather narrow chimney-like solution tube in a pine forest. The tube is less than half a metre in diameter and 8 metres deep, and it was found to enter the main cavem near a small floor-hole through which a substantial breeze was passing The cave is a north-west to south-east trending joint-controlled feature and blocks off in an area of narrowing walls about 8 metres from the entrance tube. Many bones- both “old” and recent were seen throughout the cave (including in some inaccessible areas) and some were cemented on the walls in places A subsequent digging trip by Grant Pearce and Chris Murphy resulted in the successful opening up of the floorbole, enabling Grant to manoeuvre about 5 metres further down a very tight fissure before progress was again halted by narrowing walls. The source of this large volume of moving air therefore remains a mystery No further extensions appear to exist at this time.
