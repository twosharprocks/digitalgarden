Mini Essay - Frustration at People and Communities

I’m starting to realise that the issues I’ve had with Australian cave divers over the last 5-6 years has a lot of parallels with the way I struggled dealing with people in mining and in the military. Because right now it feels like the Australian cave diving community is bringing out the worst in me much the same way that the Royal Marines brought out the worst in me, and mining nearly drove me to suicide. I know that it’s the schemas that I use to view the world that are creating these parallels, but that doesn’t mean that they impact me any less - it simply means that I can address them by identifying my own thought patterns.

Like my experience in the military, my deep emotional investment in cave diving amplifies the positive and negative emotions I experience with it. Like the military, I’m also very good at cave diving and often judge others based on their capabilities at what we’re doing together. I’m very forgiving of people developing their skills, but I also need to see genuine acknowledgment of their skill gaps and a clear effort to be better. I expect nothing less of myself, and hold others to the same standard.

Above all though I want to do cool shit, and while I’m happy to share my experiences I don’t actually need to have them with other people around. But I will deeply resent other people if they get in the way or try to hold me back from doing what I love. This can take the form of incompetence, jealousy, being dismissive of my efforts, or just straight out insecurity on their part.

Facebook isn’t the source of this, but it absolutely amplifies all of it. It makes me crave those communities that don’t want me to be part of them, and makes me feel like even more of an outsider than I usually do. Generally I quite like my outsider nature, but Facebook absolutely makes it feel like a negative - like something I should be ashamed of and try to change. Facebook makes me crave acceptance by the “tribe”, and I’ve always hated tribalism - breaking free of that mindset is what made Mars One so appealing. The idea that we as a species could lift above our petty nationalism and small-minded squabbles to do remarkable things has always been the most important thing for me.

Hearing Martin and Chloe say “People are shit” was incredibly challenging, because I know they’re not. I get frustrated and rage against people and communities because so often I see them acting selfishly and at their most basic levels rather than acting in the highest and best interests of those around them. Instead of acting in the best interests of the universe. I see them harm others out of fear for themselves, like drowning swimmers trying to climb on top of each other instead of helping each other swim to the shore. I think people are absolutely remarkable sparks of light, that we are all tremendously powerful, and that we are all capable of doing extraordinary good for those around us. That we don’t need to be so scared all the time.

My frustration with Australian cave divers, with the military, and with mining, all stems from the belief that we are so much better when we help each other do remarkable things, and that we lose nothing by helping. That help may not be appreciated or valued, it may even be taken advantage of because of other people’s insecurities. But no matter what, it’s still helped. Through your action you’ve helped push humanity along just a little bit further, and that’s to be admired. I’m grateful to still be here to help others, and I’ll continue to do it regardless of the outcome. Ultimately this is about creating small works of art and showing love, and I will never regret showing the universe how much I love being here.
