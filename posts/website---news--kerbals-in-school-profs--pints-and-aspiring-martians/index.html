School visits
Launch Pad Speaking has started to roar along since Mars One announced there is just 706… correction, 705 astronaut candidates left – someone dropped out the day the announcements were made. I’m currently coordinating with schools on a raft of different speaking visits, and  I’ve just produced a new set of promotional flyers explaining what Mars One & my school visits are all about if you don’t already know.

June 13th - TeachMeet at ScitechI’ll also be heading into Scitech for “TeachMeet” in a few Fridays time to meet about 130 WA science teachers for dinner & presentations on science education. I’ll be presenting partly about Mars One and my school visits, but what I’m really excited to talk to them about is…

Trialling Kerbal Space Program in Schools

For the last few weeks I’ve been setting up a 5-week pilot program to teach rocketry, physics & space history to Year 6-12′s using Kerbal Space Program (KSP). If you haven’t seen KSP before, essentially you design and launch your own space program, with green-skinned “Kerbals” as your fearless space explorers. The program has a full sandbox mode, allowing you to use 100′s of different parts to build your own rockets, satellites, interplanetary landers and space stations. It all sounds pretty cool & inspirational, and it is…

Watch archived videoThe folks at Squad are doing incredible things with KSP, and one of the most exciting developments is their new KerbalEdu mod being developed for use in schools. KerbalEdu includes the full retail version of Kerbal Space Program, as well as early access to the teaching aids provided by the mod too – so schools can purchase this incredible teaching tool for a radically reduced price (over 50% off retail) AND get access to the learning tools as KerbalEdu develops too.

It also means that you’ll get to laugh at your students when their precious rockets explode. And they will explode. REGULARLY. But all in the name of science.

If you’re a Year 6-12 science teacher interested in having an astronaut candidate teach your kids about space exploration with a game they will love, get in touch – I’m offering to run pilot programs in two schoolsat no cost beyond the heavily discounted software. You purchase KerbalEdu (~$13 per licence) and I’ll volunteer 90 minutes a week for 5 weeks to work with you to make the magic happen –  showing your students how rockets & space stations are designed, what it takes to get into orbit, and what it takes to land on another planet, all while explaining how it links to real-world space science & the history of the space-age.

Check out the KerbalEdu trial program flyer more information or get in touch via the contact page and we’ll start launching some Kerbals from your school!

June 17 – Scitech’s “Profs & Pints”
Scitech is back running “Profs & Pints” again for a 5th year at the Flying Scotsman, and they’ve invited yours truly to help stir the pot on the ethics of sending humans to Mars!

“GROUND CONTROL TO MAJOR TOM’: Should we put a human on Mars?

Have you ever wondered why so many countries, organisations and people want to go to Mars? Have you ever wondered if we should? Is it about curiosity? Is it about knowledge? Is it about ‘the BIG questions’? Is it ‘cause we can’?

I have NO IDEA who they’re going to put me up there with/against, but I can assure you it’ll be a lively discussion. If you haven’t been to a Profs & Pints before, it’s a very similar structure to ABC’s Q&A, but run in a pub with scientists on the panel – guests providing a brief introduction on themselves and their field of work/research, and then answer questions from the audience and the live Twitter feed. Previous Profs & Pints have tackled things like genetic engineering & scientific censorship, and featured Nobel Prize winners.

August 16-24th – Big Screen Kerbal Chaos at National Science WeekAustralia’s National Science Week is huge, and I’ve been invited back by Scitech to present at the Perth Science Festival. But this won’t be the usual “Hi everyone, I’m doing science stuff on a stage for 20 minutes, look at MEEEEEEEE!****“. This will be “Hey everyone, what’s the BIGGEST screen you’ve ever played a computer game on? Was it EIGHT METERS TALL? And the game you were playing – were you launching MASSIVE ROCKETS?”

That’s right – we’re setting up a computer to play Kerbal Space Program on the Super Screen in the Perth Cultural Centre
Still hashing out many of the details, but one thing is for certain – I will be inviting random strangers to use an LED screen as big as the side of my house to launch rockets and land a capsule on Mars (or “Duna” as the Kerbals call it) during the August 16th launch of National Science Week. I’ll also be running a stall with KSP in the cultural centre, promoting Mars One and the mountain of other activities I’ve got planned – stay tuned for more updates, but this will be 100 billion hot dogs of AWESOME on the Eddie Izzard scale.

September 28th - Aspiring Martian Meetings in Australia & Canada

I told you there was a lot of epic news to share… Here’s FINAL piece of incredible news I’ve been hanging onto until everything was organised.

As I mentioned in the previous post, I’ve joined the organising committee for Australia’s first SpaceUp, being hosted in Adelaide on September 28th. What I didn’t tell you is that I’m stepping in for two reasons: 1) Help run their social media and web content (so keep an eye out for more content both here & at SpaceUp Australia), and 2) Organise an “Aspiring Martian Meetup” for all the Mars One candidates in Australia to meet for the first time in Adelaide for the conference!

At this stage we’re expecting 15 Mars One candidates at the SpaceUp, and are currently negotiating with Mars One to have Bas Lansdorp or Dr. Norbert Kraft videoconference in, ***or present in person,***depending on the schedule of the global candidate selection process!

I’m also coordinating with several Canadians to organise a simultaneous Aspiring Martian Meetup in Toronto at the same time – the world’s biggest space conference (the 2014 International Aeronautical Conference) starts on September 29th, so we’re hoping the team in Canada can get a mountain of Mars One candidates & space speakers and have them videoconference our SpaceUp in Adelaide at the same time.

So if you’re based in Canada (especially if you’re in Toronto) and want to help organising the Toronto Aspiring Martians Meetup… get in touch!

I may have bitten off more than I can chew this year… but I’m convinced it’s better to try ALL of the things and fail, than wonder what I could have done instead.
