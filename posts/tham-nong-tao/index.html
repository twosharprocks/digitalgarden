Additional Notes#

Thai Cave Exploration, Day 1 Our initial plan was to go dry caving and extend the existing survey done by Didier Rateau and his team. Once at the first site (Tham Nong Tao) we were surprised to find out that the whole cave system was underwater. This obviously was a good thing for us cave divers but I remember swearing as I was prepared for documenting dry… The water level was too deep to walk so we used a tiny “one-man-research-kayak” we found stranded at the entrance to carry our land gear to avoid swimming with it. At 250 metres in we reached the end of the surveyed area and Naomi Allen had the privilege to be the first diver ever in this system and to discover that the cave still goes further!
Near by the cave there is a man made damm that restricts the water flow out. According to a local cave expert Manatthawut ChuSaeng, this unique feature enables this system to be completely submerged during rainy season and almost dry the rest of the year. Cat fish live inside so there will be water through out the year, just need to find where it goes the next time. Our second target (Tham Lum Pho) under the same mountain had surprisingly no water at all? Ofcourse we now had a bit if dive gear and left the land camera equipment in the car… Luckily we didn’t carry our ccr’s with us.

Thai Cave Exploration / Tham Nong Tao (Turtle Swamp Cave) New addition to the growing number of diveable caves in Thailand. 🇹🇭 This marks the 11th overhead discovery suitable for recreational cave/mine diving since we started the search in 2022 with Bottomline Projects Filthy Few dive team. Story of Tham Nong Tao starts a way back as it is a solution cave formed hundreds of thousands of years ago in to a karst mountain in Krabi province. We got the coordinates from dry caver friend Didier Rateau (and his team) who is mapping the dry sections of this cave system. We were invited to survey the underwater parts and for our surprise there was plenty of it. This system is very unique as the water level can variate by 4 meters depending of the season. What makes it even more special is the water reservoir build right next to the cave entrance that restricts the water from flowing out and creating this crystal clear confined environment during the rainy season. We have only just started to explore the submerged parts but so far we have mapped (mNemo + Digital 3D) over 500 metres of fully or partially flooded cave. The maximum depth variates seasonally from 4-8 metres. We recovered all the lines with us for safety and nature protection reasons for now, but might lay permanent guid lines to the completely submerged parts, once we know where they actually are.
