Akiyoshidai

#diving 

About#

From https://tech-diving.jp/akiyoshidai

What is Akiyoshidai? Akiyoshidai is Japan’s largest karst plateau , located in the central and eastern parts of Mine City, Yamaguchi Prefecture. Its limestone distribution covers approximately 93 km², a scale unparalleled anywhere else in Japan.

More than 400 limestone caves have been identified in Akiyoshidai, including Akiyoshido Cave , one of Japan’s three largest limestone caves and a designated special natural monument.

Akiyoshidai is divided into two plateaus, Higashidai and Nishidai, by the Higashikawa River . Higashidai (Akiyoshidai in the narrow sense) was designated a national park (Akiyoshidai National Park) in 1955 and a special natural monument in 1964. In addition, individual caves are registered as natural monuments, and meticulous care is taken in their maintenance and management.

Akiyoshidai Groundwater System The limestone that makes up Akiyoshidai is thought to have begun forming about 340 million years ago, and then risen to the surface several million years ago. Over long periods of time, this limestone layer was eroded by water, forming a groundwater system, and it is believed that a complex network of underground waterways has now been formed.

The groundwater system of Akiyoshidai has been studied since the 1950s, with a particularly large number of surveys and studies conducted in the 1980s. While some of these included surveys involving actual diving, the limitations of cave diving technology and surveying methods at the time meant that detailed underwater surveys, systematic investigations, and research on the underwater environment were not fully conducted.

Groundwater system of Higashi-Akiyoshidai Underwater surveys of Higashidai, particularly those concerning Akiyoshido Cave, have progressed relatively well. The groundwater system of Higashidai is broadly divided into two parts: the “Kanoide water system” in the north and the “Akiyoshido water system” in the south, with their respective catchment areas estimated at 1,200 hectares and 1,400 hectares. 

  1. Intermittent surveys have been conducted both on land and underwater at Akiyoshido Cave, and as of December 2017, the total length of the surveys, including both the land and submerged sections, reached 10,700 meters. 
  2. Approximately 30% of this area is submerged, leaving room for exploring new side tunnels through diving.

However,

  • A certain level of research had already been conducted between the 1970s and 1990s.
  • Akiyoshido Cave is the only cave designated as a Special Natural Monument, and therefore requires more delicate experience and environmental understanding during its investigation.

Based on the above points, this project will begin with the Nishidai groundwater system, for which there are relatively few survey examples.

Groundwater system of Nishi-Akiyoshidai Compared to Higashidai, Nishidai has fewer underground lakes, and previous research on its groundwater system has been limited.
While there are records of diving in some areas in the past, as far as I could find, there have been no underwater surveys or specific field studies conducted underwater.

The groundwater system of Nishidai is

  • Atufuku Beppudai Hydrological District
  • Iwanagadai Hydrological District
  • Isadai Hydrological District

They can be broadly divided into these three categories.

Within this area, the Ofukubeppu-dai hydrological district contains “Terayama no Ana,” the largest underground lake in Nishidai . In this project, we first considered the possibility of starting a diving survey from “Terayama no Ana.”

It is believed that the groundwater around “Terayama no Ana” drains from west to east, 3) and if this hypothesis is correct, it is possible that “Terayama no Ana” is connected underwater to “Shirakawa no Ike” downstream .
In particular, the groundwater levels at both locations are almost identical at approximately 90 meters, and furthermore, measurements indicate that there is little fluctuation in the groundwater level due to rainfall throughout the year.3 ) Given these conditions, it is possible that they are connected by a relatively large underground waterway , and it can be assumed that there is space through which a person can pass.

Previous studies have stated that “this section was affected by the former intense erosive action of the Koto River, as indicated by the V-shaped valley (Tanaka and Fujii, 1975; Fujii, 1980) buried beneath the downstream Seto Polie, and the cave has expanded considerably downwards. However, due to the rise in groundwater level associated with the deposition of alluvial deposits, this lower section is now submerged and cannot be directly observed.” 3) However, conducting a diving survey may allow for the direct observation of the subsurface structure, which has previously only been inferred indirectly.
