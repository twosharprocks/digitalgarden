While there’s been a rough plan on where to go and what to do this trip since July last year, I’ve deliberately held off booking things too far in advance to keep myself open to random opportunities along the way. So a week before I was slated to fly from Dublin to Boston I decided I should actually book the flight, and was in for one hell of a surprise.

Far and away the cheapest option was to go via Reykjevic, and after the Ethiopia episode there was NO way I was going to fly all the way to Iceland to spend the entire time in the airport again. Even if it DID have free wifi. So I booked flights and set 4 days aside to explore the land of fire & ice.

I woke up in Dublin the day before the flight to Reykjavic cheerful, bouncing out of bed ready for the next adventure and put the kettle on for my morning coffee. Waiting for it to boil, my stomach suddenly dropped as I realised: – I don’t speak Icelandic, – I’m travelling so light I don’t even have a jumper, and – I actually have no idea what the hell to do in Iceland for 4 days.

My entire knowledge of Iceland in 4 minutes & 7 seconds

After some furious research I created something vaguely resembling a plan, made a couple of bookings, was given a jumper, and figured if it all went horribly wrong at least my frozen corpse would be cryogenically preserved striking an awesome pose inside some Icelandic glacier.

Hopefully long enough there’s been 3 or 4 alien invasions to really clear the place out before I’m revived

First impression flying into Iceland was that it’s not… really… inviting…

Unless you’re really into lifeless volcanic outcroppings

Also turns out you don’t fly into Reykjavik: you fly into Keflavik, out on the Reykjanik peninsula about 50km away from Reykjavik. Which suited me just fine, as where I’d hired a campervan from was right near the airport and I’d had more than enough of seeing/hearing/smelling/tasting people over the last few months – avoiding the capital fitted well with my plan to escape humans for at least a few days.

Picking up my “Happy Camper”, I decided to test out this whole “driving on the right-hand side of the road business” by driving around the local neighborhood to get the hang of having everything reversed. After driving the back streets (only going around a roundabout the wrong way once) I managed to grab camping food for the next 3 days from the local supermarket (using a combination of mime & animal noises to communicate with the cashier who spoke perfect English) and found an awesome Viking statue.

My bird, beard & axe game are severely lacking here

Emboldened by my Viking brethren, it was time to start the adventure and venture out onto the highway: I was on it 2 minutes before I stalled the camper trying to take an exit, stranding me halfway across a dual-carriageway on a blind corner. The camper refused to start, the car behind me started beeping, I threw on the hazard lights sending the car behind me into a blind honking-rage, before the engine eventually restarted on the 6th go and I pulled clear of an oncoming semi-trailer.

Glad to say the driving for the rest of the trip was relatively uneventful…

Rather than racing to all the usual tourist spots I wanted to get out and away from people (especially ones driving other vehicles). Hafnir was relatively close, on the coast, and sounded vaguely like the name of a battlemech in Mechwarrior (because I’m psychologically 7 years old). And as you’d expect from a place I randomly chose based on its tenuous link to a video game I played 15 years ago, there was nothing to see except the set of Biodome 2.

Pauly Shore films don’t get the budgets they used to

But just down the coast was a place where you could walk across a bridge connecting the North American & Eurasian continental plates…

Shout out to my high school geography teacher who said “plate tectonics are just a theory”… just like gravity

And another place called Gunnuhver, where the Icelandic believe a demonic ghost lives and geothermal steam jets out of the ground (these two things are probably connected).

“Please don’t shove your face in the vent of stones producing super-heated steam that we named after a demon”

Working my way around the peninsula, I found myself being followed by a pair of German tourists. This became a common theme during my time in Iceland whenever I was on any of the “ring” roads – the same people stopping to look at the same incredibly stunning scenery you stopped to look at, leaving after about the same amount of time it took you to get bored with it, before driving in the same direction and turning up to ruin your next stop with their peopleness.

The Atlantic Ocean – largely untainted by peopleness

We reached peak peopleness when I stopped at the Blue Lagoon. The Blue Lagoon itself held little to no appeal to me, but everyone raves about it being a “must visit” and it was on my way, so I figured I’d at least call in to see what all the fuss was about. And lo and behold, it’s a pale blue lagoon.

Please hold your gasps of shock & astonishment

It was also terrifyingly touristy. The whole place was setup like a hotel-resort, funneling everyone into a lobby and restaurant, charged access to swim in the lake, and you literally had to exit via gift shop. And every asshole in Iceland was there with their obnoxious screaming kids. I snapped a selfie as evidence I’d been there (above), used their marble toilets and bolted out of there.

How’s the serenity?

The whole point of spending a few days in a camper driving around Iceland was that I didn’t have to interact with people if I didn’t want to (I didn’t), and I could disconnect from the rest of the world to enjoy the natural beauty of Iceland. So I connected my SIM-less phone to the stereo, put the Martian soundtrack on loop, and followed the south coast.

The camper hire guy had said I’d get around Iceland a lot quicker than I might expect, and he was dead right – I’d driven half the south coast before midday, and my only plan had been to drive that before heading to Thingvellir national park to camp overnight ready for my dive at Silfra the next morning. So with about 11 hours of sunlight left (welcome to the land of the midnight sun) I headed inland towards Geysir instead, where the word “geyser” comes from.

The Icelandic are a very literal people

With the icy wind blowing off the mountains, I decided to have my first meal in the camper – cracking the side door just enough not to gas myself on the stove fumes but without freezing either.

It’s a delicate balance being this classy

After another venture into a hyper-touristy hotel/resort at Geysir – to abuse a toilet, drain the dregs of my 2-minute noodle soup out in the car park, then leave again – I decided “national park” sounded like the place likely to have the lowest peopleness. Heading south-west into Thingvellir national park, you can see why they filmed Game of Thrones here. I think. I mean, I’ve never watched it and never plan to, but I know there’s a bunch of medieval families straight up murdering each other at an awesome wedding, and vague claims of dragons. Which all sounds like it should be filmed in the same place Vikings came from. It also probably explains why the show “Vikings” is filmed here. Which I’ve also never seen.

Probably because I’m too busy visiting these places instead of watching shows filmed here

There was still plenty of light when I parked at Silfra – where I’d be diving the next morning – so I decided to have a look around. Walking along a path through a schism in the sheer rock face, I turned around and saw this…

All of the pretty

… about 10 seconds before another tourist walked up to me and said “Excuse me, do you know which side is North America?”. Just like the bridge between two continents, Thingvellir national park is situated right where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates are pulling apart. I worked out we were facing North and told him “The mountains on the left”.

All that smart ass geography knowledge didn’t mean a damn when it came to settle in for the night though – the guy who hired me the camper told me that “most campsites are open all year round” but do you think I could find one with an unlocked toilet and shower After driving around the whole park I eventually found one that at least had the disable toilet unlocked, and seems like it was the only one in the region too because I had multiple people knock on my camper door around twilight asking if I’d seen any other sites with unlocked toilets and showers. So much for escaping peopleness.

I’d be in for a little more peopleness the next morning, but it’d all be worth it though: I’d be taking the plunge at one of the top 10 dive sites in the world. While all of Thingvillir national park is formed by the North American & Eurasian tectonic plates pulling apart, Silfra is the spot in Thingvillir where water running off the mountains emerges after 75+ years of limestone filtering to form a freshwater lake. So besides the fact you’re diving between two continents, you’re also diving in the highest visibility water in the world (measured at over 120m/393ft), and it feels a bit like you’re spacewalking instead of diving.

Camping just up the road meant I was there before all the divers driving from Reykjevik, so I chatted to our surface support instructor Tanya while she set the gear up & we waited. I was qualified as a TDI Advanced Trimix diver and instructing for both PADI & SSI prior to moving to the UK to join the Royal Marines, but I really haven’t been diving regularly since then. So while the knowledge & experience are all still there I was definitely rusty, and it was great having the team from DIVE.IS looking after us. The water at Silfra is also about 2°C, which meant wearing some incredibly sexy dry suits…

Dry suit is borrowed but the sexy beanie is all mine.

I was grouped up with Johnathon (an English uni student who’d just done his Open Water course in the Red Sea) and Pien (an Californian lady who was only on her 4th dive ever) to be led by our dive guide Rob who’d just finished a season instructing and leading dives through the WW1 German wrecks at Scarpa Flow (one of my all-time “places to dive before I die/go to Mars”).

Our first dive was really just a check out so we could get used to the dry suits, but it was still extraordinary. Pien struggled with her buoyancy & ears, so Rob spent most of the dive looking after her. I kept a casual eye on Johnno at the same time, but he adapted to the dry suit really quickly so we spent most of the dive soaking up as much of the underwater scenery as we could.It was still bloody cold though, and after the first dive and a hot chocolate on the surface Pien was so cold she decided to skip the 2nd dive. Which is a shame because it’s when Rob, Johnno & I got the best pictures, like hanging onto two continents at once…

North American plate in my left hand, Eurasia in my right

… and diving on something that felt like a blue-tinged moonscape.

You can check out the full gallery here, but it’s safe to say the experience was absolutely extraordinary.

After swapping cards and hugging a huge thanks to everyone, it was off on the next adventure into the Icelandic highlands! In July 2015 Mars One’s CEO Bas Lansdorp was scouting locations for a mock Mars habitat, and posted photos & coordinates for two possible sites in Iceland – one near Vik, and the other in the highlands near Suðurland. So I figured if I was in the country and it was so easy to get around I’d check them out myself, starting with the Suðurland site.

Welcome to the Icelandic Highlands

Turns out driving around Iceland is really easy unless you want to drive on an “F-Road”. I can think of a few things the F stands for, but basically you’re not able – physically or legally – to drive on an F-Road unless you’re in something that is half 4WD & half monster truck.

Do your wheels even lift bro?

There’s no way my puny campervan was going to cut it, but sure enough as I pulled up at the sign marking the start of the F-road the camper’s Tom Tom was telling me the Mars One site was another 8km ahead. What the Tom Tom wasn’t telling me though was that I’d be slogging the whole way through about 2 feet of snow drift to get there. There’s no doubt it was cold when I started the hike, but it was less than 20 minutes before I was stripping out of my thermals down to a shirt & jeans. And hour & a half later I was walking around in circles trying to get a GPS lock on the coordinates Bas had shared…

… before finally finding the right spot It’s difficult to describe just how desolate the whole area is. Besides some power lines way off to East that you really have to look for to see, there’s no sign of humanity or life generally. I’d kept my headphones in during the hike there, and was dumbfounded by how still & quiet the place was when turned the music off. As in eerily quiet – no wind, no birds, nothing.

After snapping my photo in a place specifically selected for its barren emptiness, it was time to hike back to the camper. With everything covered in snow and no frame of reference I’d had a nightmare trying to navigate in, but using the power lines and the hill I’d climbed to get to the plateau as a heading the return journey was much quicker… and also lead me straight towards a cliff I’d skirted on the way in. After some improvised tobogganing (eg. holding my bag to my chest and sliding on my ass down 200m of snow drift) I managed to get back to the river just a kilometer from where I’d parked.

The snow on the right is a LOT higher than it looks

So after about 4 hours of hiking and without another soul for at least 10km, I figured a trip to Iceland wouldn’t be complete without a skinny dip in water that was flowing off highland glaciers.

I could say this has been tastefully cropped, but the 2 degree water made overexposure less of an issue

There was fair bit of light when I got back to the camper, so I figured I’d at least try to get to the other Mars One site at Vik too. Heading back down from the highlands and toward the southern coast both fuel and light were getting very low, but I still stopped to grab a photo of this little gem.

The hotel of my people

By the time I nursed the car into the service station at Vik and figured out the bizarre payment system for fuel it was well and truly twilight, and there was no way I’d be able to find the second Mars One site in the dark. I’d driven passed some amazing waterfalls and watched the sunset on Eyjafjallajökull (the volcano that shut down air traffic in Europe for 6 days in 2010) and Katla (Eyjafjallajökull’s much bigger sister), so I headed back towards the camping grounds I’d seen on the way to Vik – trying to find somewhere with very few lights in the vain hope of spotting the aurora. With total cloud cover the aurora was a no-show, but unbeknownst to me I wound up parking for the night with a waterfall directly behind me… … and one of Iceland’s most famous waterfalls a short walk away.

Seljalandsfoss waterfall looking glorious

And as you’d expect from one of Iceland’s most famous waterfalls, the place was packed with tourists. After a few photos of the waterfall (and dunking my head in a stream as a pseudo-shower) I scampered out of there pretty quick. But with the drive/hike in the highlands and a few nights to myself without wifi, I’d had something of a breather from peopleness.

After a gorgeous drive to Selfoss through the southern mountains and skimming the edge of Reykjavik, it was time to drive back to Keflavik and return the camper. I stayed the night in a place about 9km from the airport, watching The Expanse till midnight & waiting for it to get dark enough to finally catch the aurora. But even with clear skies it never really got dark enough before the sun was coming back up again, so I guess I’ll just have to come back when the nights are longer!

And probably stay in one of these bubbles for it

The next day it was time to say goodbye to Iceland, so after a slow start & an awesome breakfast I figured I had plenty of time to skip the taxi and walk to the airport – popping the headphones back in and starting the hour & a half walk. I’d been warned by a friend who’s a huge Kim Kardashian fan (don’t worry, I judge her too) that Kim & Kanye were in Iceland at the same time, and low and behold just as I was walking passed the end of the runway I had their private jet take off right over my head. Personally I’m just really glad Yeezy didn’t decide to flush at the same time.

Please don’t

There’s been so much happening I’m about 3 weeks behind on these posts (currently in Washington D.C ahead of the Humans 2 Mars conference starting Tuesday). But the next step was flying into Boston for a few days before a weird weekend in NYC, and without going into details a lot has changed personally since I got through that. Spending 6 days being a complete space geek in Florida certainly helped too – the trip feels very much like it’s “mine” now. So I’m looking forward to getting those trip entries written & published in the next few days too 😀
