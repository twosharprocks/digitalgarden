I have a confession to make –I like the Harry Potter series. An aunty bought me the Chamber of Secrets one Christmas,and I was hooked. As soon as I finished it,I went back and read the previous book,and then proceeded to read every single one of them as soon as they came out.

Now,I’ll happily admit J.K Rowling’s writing style is far from perfect. But for a kid who spent several long afternoons with the school psychologist trying to explain why my library lending history showed dozens of reference books being read every year,but never read a page of fiction –the fact that Rowling was the first author to get me to seriously read fiction,means she must be doing something right. I also happened to be reading the Deathly Hallows at a particularly strange time in my life –when things were going very,very wrong –so it holds a particularly special place in the gaping hole in my brain where emotions should form.

Which is why I’ve seen the latest Harry Potter film twice in the 10 days since it was released here in the UK. It’s also why I’m writing this now. But this is not to gush about how good it is (although it is very good). No,I’m going to tell you why you should go and leave the loud-mouthed little shit-stains you call children at home.

Trust me –I’m sure your child’s life worth the cost of the babysitter.

Context/Review
****************

Now the first time I saw the Deathly Hallow was an over 18′s screening,I’d had a great dinner beforehand with Wicket,and we got drunk to boot. Watching the film it was fantastic seeing the scenes from the book being faithfully played out,and for all the darkness there were some good laughs there too. It was great to see how both the characters and the three main actors have developed (even if there were a few over-acted teen-angst moments). All around,a good evening.

And the second time?

Have you ever sat in a cinema and prayed for a theatre fire? I don’t just mean a few burning smoldering curtains and a dodgy fire extinguisher;I mean some serious fire and brimstone shit –the screen exploding in a ball of white hot liquid acrylic. Think emergency exit signs melting. Think burning prams rattling down the aisles. Think…well think a little something like this -

Because this is what I was silently praying for the whole way through the second screening. So instead of a proper review,I feel I need to clear up a few things with you all here –just to check you’re all aware of a few hard truths about cinema. And I do this,because it seems most of the pre-pubscent cases for retrospective abortion that populated the theatre at our second showing were blissfully unaware that characters in films could sometimes be injured or even die.

Yes,FICTIONAL FUCKING CHARACTERS CAN DIE.

So when Hedgewig –Harry’s trusty pet owl of 7 years –gets shot out of the sky in a flurry of feathers and gore,sadness is the appropriate emotional response. When Dobby,Harry’s house elf friend,decides to catch a knife with his chest –being a bit upset is okay. Bawling your eyes out and kicking the living shit of the back of my seat,however,is NOT an appropriate response to the death of a fictional non-human character in a film. It’s a response likely to lead to more tears,when I turn around and drive a cheese Wotsit into your brain through your nose.

How could anyone have avoided seeing a violent death on screen before going to the 7th Harry Potter film? Clearly kids these days arn’t being exposed to enough violence –my Dad had me killing rabbits with my bare hands when I was 4,and just look at how well I turned out.

Also,what part of the “Don’t let a phone ruin your movie”pre-feature adverts don’t you little shits understand? Oh I’m sorry –you’re under 18,does that not apply to you? This is a fantasy film –the idea is to forget the real world and be consumed by the film-maker’s creation.

So it might come as a surprise to you,but your iPhone’s illuminated 5x7cm screen kind of breaks the fucking spell. This is not a trip to the zoo –you are not seeing a giraffe for the first time. So why are you taking photos of the screen?

I counted no less than 6 illuminated mobile phones at one point –when did this become acceptable? Does “Turn off your mobile phone”now mean “Turn off your mobile phone…unless it has a camera,then it’s perfectly okay to use it during the movie” DID I MISS A MEETING? If I’d had more left in my Coke bottle, it would have had enough weight in it,that I could have thrown it the 20 meters or so to knock one of these obnoxious little fucks out. To steal a line from the movie,the sword of Gryffindor appears to those in need –I needed it to stab one of these kids through the chest,ask “Is there an app for that?“,then return to my seat leaving little Billy skewered to his chair as an example to the others.

Oh,and while we’re on social etiquette –this is a movie,not the DVD. And even if it were the DVD,I very much doubt they’d get a potentially autistic 10 year-old to do the director’s commentary,so SHUT YOUR FUCKING MOUTH. I paid 10 quid to see a movie,not to get your expert opinion on it. Especially when that opinion is composed of “You STINK”,“GIVE US ME CRISPS,GEORGE!”,or “Eh,what’s that ginger guy doing? Looks like he’s gotta pen –is he writing? HEY! Whatchu doing?!”

Hey,here’s an idea:why don’t you call something else out,particularly during the gorgeous scene where Harry and Hermione are dancing to a great Nick Cave song? I’ll show you a trick I can do with a ball-point pen –you’ll be able to show all the other kids at school when you get out of intensive care.

So in summary for all the cinema-rednecks out there,keep your fucking kids at home. If you can’t -

  • They’re fictional characters,and you probably arn’t a diagnosed Aspergers sufferer –get a fucking grip
  • I know you think it’s connected to your life-support,but turn your phone off when the big television screen tells you to. Otherwise I’ll force-feed it to you;
  • Finally –If you’ve finished puberty,you should know better than to share your bullshit opinion in a theatre. If you haven’t finished puberty,you don’t have an opinion to share,so feel free to SHUT YOUR FACE FLAP.

This has been a public service announcement from the Mighty Ginge to all potential cinema-goers in Northern London
