Go June to September? (Summer)

  • Calmest Weather & Longest Days:
    Iceland experiences milder weather, longer daylight hours (up to 24 hours near the solstice), and calmer surface conditions during summer. This makes logistics easier and safer, particularly when accessing remote dive sites.
  • Better Accessibility:
    Many of Iceland’s cave dive sites are in remote highland regions or near lava fields — during winter and spring, snow and ice can make these areas impassable. Roads are generally fully open by mid-June and remain accessible until early autumn.
  • Consistent Water Temperatures:
    Iceland’s groundwater-fed cave systems (like those at Silfra and Davíðsgjá) maintain constant temperatures year-round (~2–4°C), so water temps aren’t your limiting factor — surface conditions and access are.
  1. Silfra Fissure (Þingvellir National Park)
    • While not a cave in the traditional sense, Silfra offers crystal-clear visibility (~100m) in a tectonic fissure between the North American and Eurasian plates.
    • Accessible year-round, but summer offers the best overall experience due to comfort during gear changes and travel ease.
  2. Davíðsgjá
    • A darker, slightly deeper alternative to Silfra with limited overhead sections.
    • Best dived in summer for similar reasons — ease of access and surface comfort.
  3. Litlaá Lava Tube Cave Systems (near Lake Mývatn)
    • Actual underwater lava tube systems, rare and remote.
    • These are less developed for tourism and require expedition-level planning.
    • Only realistically accessible between July and early September.
