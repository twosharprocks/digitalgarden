Things have been pretty quiet around here for the last few weeks, but in typical ginge fashion there has been mischief afoot during the radio silence. But it wasn’t till yesterday when I started to update a friend in Perth that I realised just how much mischief has gone down, and how things have changed since I started the Mars project 3 weeks ago. And saying that the last post announcing the Mars project was a bit of a shocker is like saying Jimmy Saville had a bit of a secret dark side. But while those of you watching the weekly “Mars Project” videos on Youtube will be fairly up to date already, it’s nice to put it to electronic paper too for everyone – myself included.

During the Edinburgh Fringe I caught up with the exceedingly talented Cameron Davis, who’d virtually disappeared for about 2 years. Turns out he’d gone off to “find himself”, which apparently is a sophisticated way of saying he worked in a French cafe for 18 months but didn’t care enough to learn French. I’m guessing he also spent a lot of his time “reading” French porn buried in books about existentialism while occasionally staring off into the middle distance, because he’s written a book about “The Human Condition” and it’s hilarious.

I mention this because in the process Cam introduced me to self-publishing; served as a sounding board about doing a show on Mars; proved once you have a comedy book, turning it into a comedy show is relatively straight-forward; and inspired me to turn my ongoing show research into a book – a “Mars Bible” if you will. The idea of turning “Apocalypse Meow” into a book was thrown around for months after I wrote the show, but the research had already been done – half the fun of writing the show was researching all the crazy apocalyptic theories, but it becomes less amusing when you have to re-research them because you dumped all you old notes.

So if you missed it through all my inane rambling – I’m writing a book on Mars.

Which is great, but isn’t really news – every half-assed pseudo-intellectual is “working on their novel”, which will only get worse when all the wannabe Stephanie Meyer’s emerge during Nanowrimo next month to write 237 of their 50,000 word target. What IS news however is that all my research on preparing people for Mars keeps pointing in one direction – Antarctica. So hazard a guess where I’m headed?

Antarctica – Come for the Adventure,
Stay for the Rest of Your Life

The idea of going to Antarctica was kicked around as a bit of an adventure while I was still in uni, but shelved because I was studying and didn’t have the technical qualifications or experience. That’s no longer the case, so when I looked into what I could actually do down there and found I could now fill at least 5 different roles on any of Australia’s 7 Antarctic bases – gaining amazing experience, proving my suitability for isolated long-range work supporting scientific research, and getting paid damn well to it – the decision was easy.

But the biggest news (for my now justifiably terrified parents) is that I’m coming back to Perth do it – applications for the Australian Antarctic Division (AAD) open in January, and I’m coming back when they do to apply. It’ll mean I can’t apply for UK residency when my visa is up, but I can just reapply for the exact same visa I have for another 5 years when I want to return to the UK. And because I don’t need to work between now & when I go, I can spend the next 3 months writing, sorting a job in Perth before I get there, and working out how I’m going to move back to Perth on a semi-permanent basis without having a full-blown nervous breakdown when I’m there.

There’s a chance I could be picked up & sent to Antarctica within a few months of getting home – they may need someone with my skill set through the winter. But the greatest probability is that I’ll be interviewed & accepted in June, deploy to Tasmania for pre-training in September, then on to Antarctica in October – giving me a solid 8 months in Perth to work my ass off, write & re-adjust to life in sunny metropolitan Perth.

No Boromir – they most certainly do not

So the cliff notes for those of you who struggle with “big words” (ref: Martin Hughes)

  • I’m writing a semi-serious, semi-funny book on Mars
  • I’m back in Perth in January, most probably for at least 6 months
  • The return is so I can work for the Australian Antarctic Division later in the year
  • I’m going to Antarctica because it’s the closest thing we have to Mars here on Earth
  • Stop listening to shitty music and read a damn book

I also need to get back in the habit of writing here on the Mighty Ginge – things have been chaotic for the last 3 weeks in particular, but now there’s a plan laid out I can start writing here regularly again. Plus I know between all your cats, whatever the hell gangnam-style is, and this guy - you’ve all been dying for more sardonic posts from me.

So keep watching the videos, keep reading the posts, and keep telling me how amazing this all is – my ego won’t feed itself folks.
