Other Descriptions#

About#

Also known as Cocklebiddy 3 Mile Cave, the entrance is a small overhanging doline 20m in diameter. A 20m pitch leads to a loose talus and silt slope which becomes a boulder floored passage after the doline. The passage continues to a small lake at 89m. There are wetas near the entrance of the cave. There was a thylacine carcase found on early trips to the cave.

Uphill of the lake leads to further boulder floored chambers with bats. There are 300m of low maze passages beyond the lake, which contains gypsum flowers and needles. The cave breathes in maze section.

Beyond the lake underwater, the cave extends into a series of passages and boulder floored chambers with an average depth of 10m. There is a section of silt floor and parts of the cave passages which have a colouration similar to “coffee and cream”. There are three cave passages which have small air chambers.

Access#

The cave is close to a vehicle track. It is possible to camp near the cave. A typical trip into the cave would take up to 5 hours.

Rigging#

A 20m ladder or SRT is required. Rigging off trees near the entrance is the best option for anchors.

Cave Diving#

There are fixed lines in the side passages, these are shown on the map N47-CEG34002, parties will need to use their own reel to reach these fixed lines. It is possible use tripods to lower gear from the high side of the doline to reduce transporting of gear down the steep talus slope. Rigging to a vehicle is the best option for anchoring the tripod from this point.

Land use#

The cave is on Nuytsland Nature Reserve, and specific permission is required to visit the cave.

Potential Leads#

  • Interfaces around the “large rock” in the main chamber
    Breakdown piles often mask bypass tubes. Check behind and above the boulder for blind domes or tube lips; inspect for faint percolation staining or clean, eddy-scoured floor patches indicating through-draft water movement.

  • “Gary’s Back Passage” and the entrance-to-main-tunnel shortcut Both are named as Advanced segments—good flags that they’re tighter/less travelled. These are prime spots to find side tubes that were skipped when the main line was established. Slow, close wall-scans often reveal inset phreatic notches.

  • Joint-controlled leads aligned with regional structure Nullarbor caves commonly exploit joint sets; historical mapping notes exist for Murrawijinie (SA) showing joint frequency plots—useful analogs for what to look for in plan view at Murra-el-Elevyn: straight runs, right-angle turns, bead-string chambers.

  • Ceiling domes & cupolas in the half-filled network
    In mixed air/water sections, inspect cupolas for high-level feeders. Dry-tooling to a visible rim may reveal a tubes’ continuation once submerged a few metres farther on.
