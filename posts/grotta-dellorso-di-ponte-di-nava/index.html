About#

The cave was discovered by chance in October 1886 by the owner of the land while he was excavating for the construction of a wall. The cavity was explored by the owner of the land who found the bone remains of Ursus spelaeus. Hence the name given to the cave. The first systematic exploration was by Capello in 1952, the GSP passed the first siphon upstream in the sixties reaching the next tunnel. In 1991 the CSARI group from Brussels passed a series of siphons reaching a fourth impassable siphon.

The cavity opens approximately 500 m east of the hamlet of Ponte di Nava, in the upper Tanaro Valley, in correspondence with an isolated house, approximately 15 m from the road; the entrance, which leads directly into the initial room, is in correspondence with a dry stone wall and closed by an old wooden door.

The cave consists of a series of sub-horizontal galleries that branch off from the entrance hall. On the left, a large sub-circular gallery quickly leads to the active and large lake-siphon. A few meters of lake and the speleo-underwater part begins, at a first siphon crossed by a good flow of the main stream (S1, L = 60 m, depth 8 m, ) follows on the right a concretioned aerial gallery that extends for about 200 m stopping at an impassable narrow passage, while on the left are S2 (L = 15 m, depth -2 m) and S3 (L = 90 m, depth -18 m). The exit of the third siphon can also be reached from the previous concretioned gallery, from here starts S4 (L = 40 m, depth -9 m) which is narrow, difficult and with poor visibility. Returning to the initial room, a second central gallery leads after about a hundred meters, progressively narrowing, to narrow spaces closed by small landslides. Still from the initial room, on the right, a series of fairly complex, close and interconnected spaces allow you to reach a small lake and a small gallery closed by concretions. From a hydrogeological point of view, the cavity is crossed by an important stream in which it is not difficult to find small trout. The temperature of the water changes greatly during the year, in winter it is around 4° C, while in summer it can reach 10° C. From all these indications it seems very probable that the watercourse is linked to sub-channel losses of the Tanaro river. The resurfacing of the waters is near a series of pools, a few hundred meters downstream, near the river itself. (taken from: Atlas of the Piedmontese karst areas - Volume 2 (2010)

Discovered in 1886, this cave is situated few hundred of metres from the village of Ponte di Nava; it has been so called because of the skeleton of Ursus Spelaeus found in one of its halls. It is formed by the intersection of several tunnels in which the waters of the Tanaro river worked in different periods. Into this cave still flows a remakable river that has two siphons both towards valley and huphill.

[Translated from Italian] The cave “Orso di Ponte di Nava” (Ormea, CN) is a gym for all of Attilio’s students, including me. Inside the cave, a short distance from the entrance, there is a lake of clear water, from which a siphon begins, short but just over a meter high and with a sandy bottom. Attilio takes the neophyte cave divers into this siphon; after a few meters he stirs the sand on the bottom. When you can no longer see anything, the diver’s tranquility is put to the test in conditions of poor visibility, an essential prerequisite for cave diving.
