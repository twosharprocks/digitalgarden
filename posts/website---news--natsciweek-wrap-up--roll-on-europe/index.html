Nothing quite like taking things to extremes, then pushing a little further to see if you break. Then pushing even more…

National Science Week is over for another year, and this year was the biggest yet. I was directly involved with 12 different events across science week, and I’ll be honest I was solidly fried by the end of it. I didn’t have any events organised for Sunday the 17th, so atleast I had a slight breather for my birthday… but even that was spent working on “Becoming Martian” & preparing for the other events. Half the chaos was running the Indiegogo campaign: we hit the $2500 goal (THANK YOU ALL!) but showing people how Indiegogo works so they could buy tickets to Becoming Martian was a constant uphill battle.

The rest was utter chaos with school visits most mornings, then adult comedy events in the evening: Shapiro’s Science Special, Adam Peter Scott’s Science Death Match, then 3 nights of Becoming Martian.

Some of the schools went all out on decorations too

If that sounds like limit pushing it was – halfway through answering a question at the filming of Becoming Martian on the last night, there was a very clear moment where I thought I was going to drop. I’ll interested to see if you can see the moment on the DVD, but I haven’t even watched it myself yet. Luckily, 4 hours after the last performance of Becoming Martian I was on a plane to see Col. Chris Hadfield in Canberra… so I passed out for about 2 hours. Bliss.

Getting into Sydney, we had a few mishaps with Greyhound coaches, but eventually managed to get to the ANU Music Hall and witness “Space Oddity” first hand;

Just before it all kicked off

Col. Hadfield with the inevitable cover of Bowie’s “Space Oddity”

And now I’m Qatar about to board a plane to Berlin – I was only back in Perth three days, and STILL managed to fit in another school visit. Four weeks travelling through Europe visting schools, space facilities & festivals in Germany, the Netherlands (BRING IT ON REDHEAD FESTIVAL!), Ireland, Northern Ireland and the UK… it’s going to be pretty manic too. Oh and just to turn up the difficulty level, I’m also editing the book version of Becoming Martian while submitting a proposal for Mars One’s university payload competition.

So, you know… busy. Wouldn’t have it any other way, but it’s always entertaining discovering just how hard you can hang on. Looking forward to a days rest in Berlin before the first stop at the European Astronaut Centre in Cologne, and I’ll keep trying to put out updates here and through Facebook/Twitter as I go!
