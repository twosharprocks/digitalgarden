Looking back, the last Mighty Ginge update was August 29th: to say a lot has happened since seeing Chris Hadfield in Canberra is a gross understatement. You probably could have followed some of it via the fairly consistent Instagram, Facebook & Twitter updates, but here’s my vague attempt to sum up the two months of utter chaos that was the European & Australian tour of Becoming Martian.

Germany (August 29th-September 1st)

Meet “Identity Crisis Bear”

Berlin was supposed to be a breather. But coming off the flight from Doha what it really devolved into was a mad dash to try to find three things:

  1. WiFi to write & submit a proposal for a payload on a Mars lander
  2. A printer to print my train ticket onward to Cologne
  3. Food/Sleep

In that order of priority. The initial submission deadline for Mars One’s University Competition was August 31st, so I had 24 hours to write an outline for the Helena payload – a scientific payload proposal to generate oxygen from the water in the Martian soil, that also includes a digital art library to Mars. In the process of trying to find wifi to access the payload reference documents I got to wander around Berlin a little, but since I’d visited before in late 2006 I was primarily just trying to find a cafe that I didn’t need a translator to ask for the wifi password…

Even with the help of some friendly German cafes, the proposal still wasn’t written by the time I needed to board the train to Cologne. So I ended up writing and submitting a payload proposal for a Mars lander minutes before the deadline using the wifi on the train as it raced through the German countryside at over 230km/hr…

Welcome to the future

In Cologne I was back in more familiar territory, having stayed there for a few days last November while visiting Europe for the 2013 Living On Mars Convention. Not that I had time to enjoy the familiar sights – I crashed as soon as I checked in, and was up early the next day to visit the European Astronaut Centre

Do you know the way to astronaut street?

One of my fellow Mars One candidates Andrea Boyd is EUROCOM – the European Space Agency’s communicator to the International Space Station. So she invited me to see where the European Space Agency’s astronauts train for expeditions to the space station, and where I could muck around in a Soyuz simulator…

Not pictured: Hypoxia-induced hallucinations of George Clooney

 Netherlands (September 2nd – 8th)

There was actually a day or two during the week in the Netherlands where I didn’t have to rush anywhere… but it sure as hell wasn’t the day I arrived. There was barely enough time to drop off my bag in Rotterdam before it was back on a train to The Hague – just making it in time for an event on solar weather hosted by the Netherlands Space Society, featuring ESA Astronaut Andre Kuipers and World Space Week‘s Executive Director Remco Timmermans.

Aurora expeditions with Remco

Friday brought more space madness, meeting up with ESA’s Communications Director Juan De Dalmau for a tour of the European Space Research and Technology Centre (ESTEC) with fellow International Space University alumni.

Me being a “Price Is Right” showgirl for ESA’s next spaceship

The ESTEC visit had to be cut short though, because the same night the all important Redhead Days festival kicked off. I’d been in the Netherlands a month too late in 2013, so this year was a great chance to catch up with some old faces and see just how much the festival has grown since I last attended in 2012.

Redhead Days Launch

To be fair I was pretty burnt out by the time I got there, but something with Redhead Days also seemed to have shifted. The atmosphere of having so many redheads in one place from all across the world is still absolutely amazing, but it wasn’t quite the life altering event my first visit was. I’m certainly not the same person I was when I arrived lost at Breda train station two years ago, so maybe it shouldn’t have come as a surprise that there wasn’t the same impact coming back.

The pub crawl continued to be the best event of the weekend’s official program, and this year there was the added bonus of trying to meetup for an interview with a French TV crew during it – they eventually found me (slightly more sober) the next day. But the real highlight was ticking off #22 on the 101 Things To Do Before You Die list by going up in a hot-air balloon the Monday the festival finished.

Our balloon was obviously the one with the giant smiley face

It was brilliant being back for the Redhead Days festival, but more importantly this visit closed out a lot of unfinished business in the Netherlands from the last 2 years. Maybe one day I’ll go back again for a bit of a laugh, but it very much feels like the festival did what it needed to do – starting my transition from a successful but half-hearted comedian, into a fully fledged space advocate.

Ireland (September 9th – 14th)


If the Netherlands was about closing out unfinished business, Ireland was very much about starting afresh. I’ve been providing support (viaSkype ) to Niamh Shaw as she developed her Dublin Fringe show, so when I found out the premiere of  “To Space” was the night after the Redhead Days festival finished I knew it was the perfect chance to finally visit Ireland! Niamh’s premiere  was amazing, and it was great to meet so many fantastic people from Ireland’s burgeoning space industry at the drinks afterwards.

When Space Gingers Attack

One of the completely unexpected parts of the evening was Niamh drawing names out of a hat to win a piece of original space art from Clare Henderson, and suddenly finding I had to tick off another entry from the 101 Things To Do Before You Die list: #92 Own an Original Work of Art

Now safely mounted on my office wall

The 48 hours in Dublin was awesome, but with only another 4 days to see as much of Ireland as possible it was into the hire car and off to Cork! Fellow Redhead festival-goer Irene had come over to Ireland too, so we shot down to Cork and met up with Joleen Cronin – organiser of the Irish Redhead Festival.

Welcome to the Red-olution

We also discovered that Cork is infact a magical and never-ending series of multi-story carparks, after inadvertently driving into 4 of them…

Endless, ENDLESS, carparks

Depending on how everything pans out with Mars One in the next 6 months, there’s a good chance I’ll be heading back to Cork for the 2015 Irish Redhead Festival to lead their “Ginger Ukulele Orchestra”. Whether I’ll be too busy with training to colonise another planet – or if this planet is ready for hundreds of gingers to start playing ukulele together – remains to be seen…

Next stop on the flying Irish tour was the Ring of Kerry, where we witnessed panoramas like this:

So… pretty…

As well as some blatant racism towards my proud gold-loving cousins:

As if we need a special crossing point – we’ve got enough gold to OWN the road

Driving on to Dingle Bay, the leprechaun abuse stopped but the overwhelming pretty didn’t:

View from the hostel’s balcony

The western coast of Ireland – please collect your jaw before you leave

Most of the time in Kerry & Dingle Bay was spent just trying not to have an aneurysm over the scenery, and we barely saw 1/100th of what the Wild Atlantic Way had to offer. But there was onereally important historical stop we managed to squeeze in. Tom Crean was an Irish explorer that partook in some of the greatest Antarctic expeditions of all time: leading a team on Scott’s 1902 Discovery expedition, surviving Scott’s fatal follow-up Terra Nova expedition in 1910, as well as Shackleton’s ill-fated Endurance expedition. But once Crean had earned his 3rd polar medal & served a further 4 years with the Royal Navy, he retired to Annascaul… where he opened a pub.

This is a real place that serves “Crean’s Lager”. We had Coke

Four days in Ireland isn’t even remotely enough to even explore Dublin properly, let alone Cork, Kerry & Dingle Bay. But Irene had to head back to the US for work and I was off to Northern Ireland for the CultureTECH Festival. We stayed back in Dublin with Niamh Shaw on our last night in Ireland though, and ended the Ireland leg of the trip with Niamh giving me what is easily the best LEGO set ever:

XCOR’s Lynx Mark II

CultureTECH in Derry, Northern Ireland (September 15th  – 19th)

The CultureTECH festival in Derry was always going to be the trip’s pressure pot – 3 days as one of the featured speakers for a technology festival with hundreds of events & activities. I wound up staying for 5 days: speaking about Mars One at half a dozen schools that ranged from high-end boarding schools, to running technology workshops, playing Bowie’s “Space Oddity” on ukulele during interviews with BBC radio, performing “Becoming Martian” as a charity event for Children In Crossfire, and talking about having the courage to fail over and over and over again at the Create2014 student-run event.

Create2014 – Inspirin’ da yoof with failure

It was 5 days of abject chaos… but without a doubt one of the best parts of the entire trip. Because while I was running around like a headless chook trying to keep track of when and where I was, every step of the way there were incredible people to steer me in the right direction. The team from CultureTECH were simply amazing: lining up as many school visits as I could squeeze in, organising interviews with the BBC, and having taxis ready to get me wherever I needed to be. Not only that, they put me up in 5-star accommodation the whole time I was there so I could tick off a third entry from the 101 Things list: #30 “Stay in the Best Suite in a Five Star Hotel”.

It wasn’t just the CultureTECH organisers who were awesome either. My old Irish friend Barry had moved back to Derry a few months before, so it was fantastic to finally catch up with him – I’d barely seen Barry since late 2010, when we were sharing a mouldy flat in London and I moved out because my taste in girlfriends had suddenly improved. Without going into specifics Barry has had a particularly rough year, so I think it was good for both of us to catch up and remember that even with the dips he’s had over the last 12 months, life is still way better than it was 4 years ago.

Circa December 2010, aka “Evidence Exhibit A”

But while we were reminiscing, Barry & I were also causing chaos at the festival and making mountains of friends doing it. CultureTECH had brought in so many inspiring folks from all across the world it was hard to keep track of them all: Heather from Uproar Comics, Alastair from Startacus, the dozen scholarship winners from Honeycomb, and the hysterical team from Ministry of Science… whose children’s shows undoubtedly contain less innuendo than I like to pretend they do.

Emma wants to end on a big bang, but Mike isn’t sure they should, in front of all the kids…

CulutreTECH was genuinely out of control, and I loved every moment of it – from ridiculous dinner parties that ended with us getting kicked out of the dodgiest pub in Derry, right through to performing “Becoming Martian” while sounding like a koala with emphysema  because my voice had gone that morning. It was ridiculous, fun-filled chaos all the way through, and I genuinely hope I get a chance to go back again and do it all again.

London, Benenden & Brighton (Sept 20-26)

Leaving Northern Ireland was a stark wake up call to the realities of life though…

And let us never speak of this again

After the nightmare that was my flight out of Belfast to Bristol though, I managed to knock over a bunch of little jobs that I’d been carrying with me since National Science Week. Restitching my bag, sewing patches on my ukulele case, copying all my random scraps of paper covered in ideas into something more permanent… general travel admin. Once you’ve been living out of a bag for a few weeks there’s often a moment where you realise things are going to start falling apart on you if you don’t take a little time to fix them – landing in Bristol was that moment, so on the bus to Bath I sorted everything.

Things would start falling apart again before I got back to Perth, but that’s another story.

The return to Gloucestershire was very short though. I managed to catch up with Vicky & Olly – two great friends from my days working for Damien Hirst – for a few hours in Bath. But the next morning the infamous Martin picked me up for breakfast in my old town of Stroud, I managed to catch up with Mark Chatterley from InEarEntertainment for a few hours, then it was on the move again down to London that afternoon…

For tourists, this is a delightful photo op. For anyone who’s spent 6 hours waiting for a 5am train, this sign causes a rash

London was another rushed, overnight visit – caught up briefly with John & Jo to see what wonderful things they’d been doing post Edinburgh Fringe, but the very next morning it was off to speak to the students at the exclusive Benenden boarding school in country Kent. The kids and staff were brilliant, really enthusiastic about World Space Week, and I even managed to find a fellow ukulele-er among the staff

The compulsion to play “Duelling Banjos” was significant

But Benenden was really just a fun detour for where I reallywanted to be, was here…

Into the Temple of (burrito) Sacrifice

… with it’s amazing interior decorations…

Mexican eating establishment protected by The Space Jesus

Last stop of the Europe leg was back to that other old stomping ground of mine: Brighton, with the best burritos on the planet at La Choza. The world’s best housemate, Megan – whose garage I was living in when I decided to sign up for Mars One at the end of September 2012 – put me up for a few nights while I prepared for the return to Australia, partaking in all the awesome food and coffee Brighton has to offer at the same time. And in amongst it all I even managed to get up to London again to catch up with Lizzy from Ginger Parrot at an open mic cabaret night in an underground toilet.

With all of that going on, you’d think it would be time to go home and have a bit of a nap. And if you call allof Australia home, then yeah I did go home –“home” to run a space conference in Adelaide, present at a different space conference, speak & perform at a Newcastle writer’s festival, dress up like an aluminium-foil covered robot with plastic cups for boobs, bolt up to Brisbane to speak at schools & universities, as well as meeting dozens more amazing people along the way.

But I guess that will all have to wait until the epic conclusion of Josh’s Europe/Oz Odyssey…

This Odyssey involves considerably less scurvy…
